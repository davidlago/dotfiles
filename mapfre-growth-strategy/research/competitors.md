# Competitor Deep-Dive: The Carriers MAPFRE Wins or Loses Against in Massachusetts Personal Auto

**Prepared for:** MAPFRE Insurance (Commerce Insurance Co.) growth strategy engagement
**Date of brief:** July 2, 2026
**Core data spine:** Commonwealth Automobile Reinsurers (CAR) exposure data as republished by Agency Checklists' bimonthly "Look" market-share series (the best public tracker of the MA private passenger auto market), carrier annual reports/10-Ks, and trade press. All figures carry as-of dates. Where a figure could not be sourced publicly, this is stated explicitly.

---

## 1. Market structure: a three-tier market with MAPFRE on top but eroding

The Massachusetts private passenger auto (PPA) market comprises roughly 4.96 million written exposures (Dec 2025) [1]. It is unusually concentrated and unusually agent-centric: the top 5 carriers hold 59.1% of exposures (Oct 2025) and the top 10 hold 84.6% [4]. Three tiers matter for MAPFRE:

- **Tier 1 — MAPFRE/Commerce alone at the top:** 18.33% share (Dec 2025), nearly 909k exposures — still ~50% larger than #2 [1].
- **Tier 2 — the national direct/hybrid writers:** Progressive (12.17%) and GEICO (11.50%) together now hold 23.67% of the market (Dec 2025) [1].
- **Tier 3 — the MA-domiciled agent carriers:** Safety (9.30%), Plymouth Rock (7.88%), Arbella (7.08%) — collectively 24.26% (Dec 2025), essentially a mirror image of the direct writers' combined share [1].

**The single most important fact in this brief:** since 2020, essentially all of MAPFRE's ~4 points of lost share maps to Progressive's ~7-point gain (with GEICO and smaller carriers supplying the rest). Progressive's MA share grew nearly 250% in five years [1]. The regional agent carriers (Safety, Plymouth Rock, Arbella) have collectively held their ground, which Agency Checklists attributes to "stable renewal retention among agent-distributed policyholders" [3]. MAPFRE is not losing to the agent carriers; it is losing to Progressive — much of it *inside* MAPFRE's own channel (see Section 3).

## 2. Carrier-by-carrier profiles

### GEICO (11.50% share, Dec 2025; #3) [1]
- **Channel:** Direct (phone/web/app) plus captive local GEICO agents; no independent agents.
- **Trend:** Declining in MA — 12.61% (Dec 2024) → 11.50% (Dec 2025) [1][2]. Nationally, GEICO shed ~1.7M policies in 2022 and posted a $1.9B pretax underwriting loss that year [5], then deliberately slowed new business through 2024 to restore margins [6]. It is now back on offense: ad spend rose an estimated 67% to ~$1.4B in 2024 [6], and by 2025 it recorded its best sustained underwriting run in 18 years (loss + LAE ratio below 75%), though premium growth (~5-5.6% in 2025) still lags its ambitions [6].
- **Pricing posture:** Historically the low-price direct benchmark; currently prioritizing rate adequacy and segmentation over volume, with heavy investment in catching up on telematics after admitting it lagged Progressive badly [5][6].
- **Telematics:** DriveEasy — app-based, available in 47 states + DC including MA (excluded: CA, HI, VT); as of July 2026 [7].
- **Bundling:** Auto-centric; home is largely brokered through partner carriers rather than underwritten by GEICO.

### Progressive (12.17% share, Dec 2025; #2 and the MA share-taker) [1]
- **Channel:** Genuinely dual-channel — the only carrier attacking MA from both sides. Direct (heaviest advertiser in the category) *and* the #1 writer of auto insurance through independent agents nationally, serving 40,000+ IA agencies [8]. See Section 3 for its MA agent play.
- **Trend:** 5.1% (2020) → 9.78% (Dec 2024) → 12.17% (Dec 2025); overtook GEICO for #2 in May 2025 [1][2][3]. Agency Checklists credits "stronger policy acquisition momentum through its agency network and digital platforms" [3].
- **Pricing posture:** Aggressive, segmentation-led; re-opened the growth throttle earlier than peers after the 2022-23 inflation shock (nationally it restored growth in 2024 while GEICO was still shrinking) [5][6].
- **Telematics:** Snapshot (app or plug-in device) — available in MA; not available in CA (and the Snapshot "Road Test" try-before-you-buy feature excludes MA) [9][10].
- **Bundling:** Sells home via Progressive Home and third-party carriers in its agency and direct channels; positions bundling as an agent retention tool. (Extent of Progressive Home underwriting appetite specifically in MA could not be confirmed from public sources.)

### Liberty Mutual (4.98%, Dec 2025; #8) [1]
- **Channel:** Direct + captive (Comparion) under Liberty Mutual brand; independent-agent business historically under Safeco. In March 2025 Liberty announced it would retire the Safeco brand, and the retirement completed April 25, 2026 — all IA-channel personal lines now sold as Liberty Mutual through its 22,000+ agency network [11][12]. It also absorbed IA books via transfers from Main Street America and Columbia Insurance Group (2024) [12].
- **Trend in MA:** Declining — 5.27% (Dec 2024) → 4.98% (Dec 2025) [1][2]; consistent with its national personal-lines pullback for profitability.
- **Pricing:** Firm/restrictive posture in recent years (profitability-first turnaround; record 2024 results accompanied the Safeco decision [12]).
- **Telematics:** RightTrack (app-based). MA availability not independently confirmed in this research — flagging as unverified.
- **Bundling:** Full-suite auto+home+umbrella bundler in both channels.

### Arbella (7.08%, Dec 2025; #6) [1]
- **Channel:** 100% independent agents; Quincy, MA-based mutual-holding group writing in MA, CT, NH, RI [13].
- **Scale/health:** $1B+ annual written premium, $2B+ assets; AM Best "A", with outlook revised to positive in 2025 on improved underwriting results driven by pricing/underwriting/claims initiatives [13][14].
- **Pricing:** Took corrective rate 2023-24 like the rest of the market; positions on local service and claims, not price leadership [13].
- **Telematics:** Wheel Focused — a free safe-driving app powered by Cambridge Mobile Telematics, aimed at distracted-driving prevention (brand/engagement play, open to non-customers); not a full behavioral-pricing UBI program [15].
- **Bundling:** Auto+home+umbrella through agents; heavy community/charitable branding in MA (Arbella Insurance Foundation, 20 years of giving) that reinforces agent and consumer affinity [13].

### Plymouth Rock (7.88%, Dec 2025; #5) [1] — see Section 5 for 5-year strategy
- **Channel:** Hybrid by design: an Independent Agency Group (its largest segment), a Direct Group, and a Home Group; writes in MA, NH, CT, NJ, NY, PA [16].
- **Trend:** Slowly gaining in MA (7.70% Dec 2024 → 7.88% Dec 2025) [1][2]; group premiums in force ~$2.3B after 15% growth in 2024 [16].
- **Pricing:** Recently rate-adequate; group combined ratio improved to 100.4% (2024) from 106% (2023) [16].
- **Telematics:** Road Rewards, powered by Cambridge Mobile Telematics — points/rewards for safe driving, launched for MA customers [17]; chairman's letter says the direct strategy "leans toward the use of telematics, offering unusually attractive prices to customers willing to have their care [car] monitored" [16].
- **Bundling:** @Home homeowners product deliberately paired with auto in mature states; home business passed $250M premium by Feb 2021 [18].

### Safety Insurance (9.30%, Dec 2025; #4) [1] — see Section 5 for 5-year strategy
- **Channel:** Exclusively independent agents — "unlike some of our competitors who use multiple distribution channels. We believe this gives us a competitive advantage with the agents" (10-K) [19]. 828 agents in 1,079 locations across MA, NH, ME (Dec 31, 2024); 734 voluntary agency agreements [19].
- **Trend:** Third-largest MA PPA writer; 9.63% (Dec 2024) → 9.30% (Dec 2025) [1][2]. Also #2 in MA commercial auto and #3 in MA homeowners (6.3% share, 2023) [19].
- **Pricing:** Took large corrective rate: average premium per policy +10.9% in 2024, driving direct written premium +20.4% and revenue over $1B for the first time [19][20]. GAAP combined ratio: 107.7% (2023) → 101.1% (2024) [19]; 99.0% reported for 2025 per its year-end earnings release [21] (figure from company release; exact URL not archived — treat as company-reported).
- **Telematics:** "Drive with Safety" app with a 5% participation discount [22] — a light program versus Progressive/GEICO behavioral pricing.
- **Bundling:** Full personal/commercial suite through agents; four price tiers via separate underwriting companies (Safety Insurance, Safety Indemnity, Safety Northeast, Safety P&C) [19].

### USAA (4.29%, Dec 2025; #9) [1]
Direct writer restricted to military members/families; slowly gaining share in MA (4.14% Dec 2024 → 4.29% Dec 2025) [1][2]. Not winnable business for MAPFRE's channel; effectively a structural carve-out of the market. Telematics: SafePilot nationally (MA availability not verified in this research).

### Amica Mutual (3.02%, Dec 2025; #10) [1]
Rhode Island-based mutual direct writer, premium service/loyalty positioning, flat share (~3.0% through 2024-25) [1][2]. Competes for the mature, high-retention preferred segment — overlapping the profitable core of MAPFRE's book — but is not a growth threat.

### Hanover (2.47%, Dec 2025; #11) [1]
Worcester, MA-based, independent-agent carrier; national IA franchise with account-rounding/bundled "Platinum" positioning (company positioning; specific MA program details not verified). Share drifting down slightly (2.60% → 2.47%, Dec 2024→Dec 2025) [1][2]. Competes with MAPFRE for the same agents' preferred account business but is de-emphasizing monoline auto.

### Norfolk & Dedham (1.95%), Quincy Mutual (1.56%), Vermont Mutual (1.04%), Preferred Mutual (0.52%) — all Dec 2025 [1]
Small, home-first regional mutuals distributing 100% through independent agents (Vermont Mutual: 800+ agency offices across New England/NY [23]). Their auto books are largely companions to homeowners accounts. Quincy Mutual's auto share is eroding (1.80% → 1.56% over 2025) [1][2]. None has a meaningful telematics program that surfaced in public sources. Strategically, they are share donors in auto, not threats — but they compete for the same agency shelf space and for home-auto account rounding.

## 3. Direct writers: who is growing fastest, why — and Progressive's IA-channel play

**Fastest grower: Progressive, and it is not close.** +7 points of MA share since 2020 vs. GEICO roughly flat-to-down over the same window and Liberty Mutual shrinking [1]. Drivers, per public evidence:

1. **Dual-channel attack.** Progressive entered the MA independent agency channel in May 2014 — Massachusetts was the 50th and final state in its IA rollout [24][25]. It therefore harvests both the price-shopper (direct) and the agent-loyal customer (IA) — the latter being MAPFRE's home turf.
2. **Segmentation and telematics pricing.** Progressive's telematics lead is explicitly cited (including by Berkshire's own Ajit Jain) as the reason GEICO lost ground on rate-to-risk matching [5]. Snapshot operates in MA [9][10].
3. **Speed through the rate cycle.** Progressive restored rate adequacy and reopened growth earlier than GEICO and Liberty, capturing the record shopping volumes triggered by MA's 2023-24 rate increases (top-7 MA carriers: +12%+ average in 2023, ~+5% approved in 2024 [26]).
4. **Agent economics and tooling.** In the IA channel Progressive offers fast quoting platforms (ForAgentsOnly), consultative field sales, SEO/referral support for agencies [24][25], and a brand the customer has already seen on TV — the agent closes what the advertising opens.

**GEICO** is the cautionary tale: its MA share decline (12.6% → 11.5% during 2025 [1][2]) is the local expression of a national retrenchment — 1.7M policies shed in 2022 [5], underwriting-first reset, then a 2024-25 re-expansion with 67% higher ad spend and DriveEasy telematics now live in MA [6][7]. Expect GEICO to be a more dangerous price competitor in MA in 2026-27 than in 2022-25.

**What Progressive is doing in the MA IA channel specifically:** appointing agents continuously since 2014 with no cap disclosed [25]; per Agency Checklists' analysis, its 2025 overtaking of GEICO reflects "policy acquisition momentum through its agency network and digital platforms" [3] — i.e., in MA its growth is materially agent-fed, not just direct. Progressive is, in effect, MAPFRE's most direct competitor for the *next policy an MA independent agent writes*.

## 4. How the agent-centric competitors court independent agents

**Safety (the most explicit public playbook, from its 10-K [19]):**
- Goal of being #1 or #2 carrier in every agency it appoints; it is ranked first or second in over 70% of its agencies by DWP [20].
- **Compensation:** "competitive commission schedules and profit sharing programs"; a loss-ratio-based agency incentive commission paying agents a percentage of premium; recognition tiers (Chairman's Elite, Chairman's, President's, Executive's, Preferred Agent's Club) [19]. Specific commission percentages are not publicly disclosed.
- **Service/tech:** Agents Virtual Community portal (policy status, billing, claims online), marketing toolkits, and an Innovation Lab shipping features like electronic claims payments and two-way claims texting (2024) [19][20].
- **Discipline:** annual performance review of agents; terminates a few underperforming agencies each year — appointments are a managed franchise, not open distribution [19].
- Also owns Safety-Northeast Insurance Agency (commission income +14.5% in 2024), giving it an in-house service-center capability [20].

**Plymouth Rock [16][17]:**
- Chairman's letter frames the IA Group's growth engine as staying "an unusually high-service carrier" in agents' eyes; agent goodwill is explicitly cited as a constraint on underwriting tightening in the Home Group [16].
- Digital tools for agents: @Home instant-quote platform, Text-to-Quote (customer gets a home quote by texting an agent), digital self-service that keeps the agent in the loop [27].
- Pilgrim Insurance, its residual-market TPA, grew volume 65% in 2024 [16] — servicing MAIP/residual business is itself an agent-relationship service.
- Commission specifics not publicly disclosed.

**Arbella [13][15]:**
- 100% IA distribution; compensation disclosure confirms base commissions for new and renewal business plus incentive arrangements (details behind agent portal; percentages not public).
- Courts agents with localness: Quincy HQ, local claims handling, and one of the most visible charitable brands in MA (Boston Business Journal "Most Charitable Companies," 17 consecutive years) [13] — community equity that agents can sell.

**Common thread:** none of the three competes for agents primarily on headline commission; they compete on *service intensity, ease-of-use technology, profit-sharing upside, and being the agency's #1 shelf position*. That is exactly the ground MAPFRE must defend.

## 5. Plymouth Rock and Safety: strategic moves, last five years

**Plymouth Rock (2020-2025):**
- **Portfolio reshaping:** sold InsuraMatch (its national digital agency) to Travelers in Dec 2020 [28]; doubled down on underwriting.
- **Home growth then correction:** @Home digital homeowners product (launched late 2019) drove home premium past $250M by early 2021 [18]; growth outran underwriting discipline, producing losses, followed by tightened rules and a slowdown — targeting "profitability with moderate growth" in home for 2025 [16].
- **Geographic build-out:** NY and PA identified as the paths to "seriously increased scale," with pricing/segmentation corrections approved and appetite to expand share through 2025 [16]. MA + NJ still = three-quarters of IA business [16].
- **Direct-channel offensive:** hired Greg Kalinsky, 37-year GEICO veteran and former GEICO EVP who ran its $40B direct book, as Direct Group president (announced Jan 2025) [16][29]. Core Direct book is ~$100M (+6% in 2024) with an explicitly telematics-led pricing strategy; Direct Group combined ratio ~99% in 2024, first underwriting profit in four years [16].
- **2024 results:** premiums in force ~$2.3B (+15%); IA Group $1.3B (+23%), sub-100 combined ratio; group combined ratio 100.4% [16]. Over half a billion dollars of IA auto premium in MA alone [16].
- **Partnerships:** Fetch pet-insurance discount tie-in for policyholders (May 2024) [29].

**Safety (2020-2025):**
- **Hard pricing correction and record top line:** DWP +20.4% in 2024 (policy count +8.5%, average premium +10.9%), revenue >$1B for the first time; combined ratio path 107.7% (2023) → 101.1% (2024) [19][20] → 99.0% (2025, company-reported) [21].
- **Geography:** deliberate, modest diversification into NH and ME (still >85% MA) [19][22] — no new-state announcements in the period.
- **Digital/innovation:** Innovation Lab productionized e-claims payments and claims texting (2024); launched a leak-and-freeze IoT monitoring device program for property customers [20]; Drive with Safety telematics app with 5% discount [22].
- **Residual market frachise:** reappointed as one of only four CAR Commercial Auto Program servicing carriers for a five-year term from Jan 1, 2022 [19] — a structural moat in MA commercial auto (#2 share).
- **Capital posture:** steady dividends (e.g., $0.90/quarter declared Feb 2025 [30]); no M&A; book value per share $60.98 at YE2025 (+9.2%, company-reported) [21].

## 6. Pricing environment (context for "pricing posture")

- 2023: the seven largest MA carriers (~75% of the market) raised rates >12% on average — the largest one-year hike since at least 2015; 2024: ~5% average approved increases (both per Division of Insurance filings reported by the Boston Globe, Oct 2024) [26].
- July 1, 2025: first increase in MA minimum limits since 1988 (H.5111, signed Dec 2024): property damage $5k→$30k, bodily injury 20/40→25/50; Bankrate/Quadrant estimated minimum-coverage premiums would jump ~36% to ~$711/yr [31][32].
- Implication: 2023-25 produced record shopping behavior — the moment when telematics-priced, heavily advertised carriers (Progressive, and now a re-arming GEICO) convert agent-channel and MAPFRE customers. MAPFRE's own economics improved through the same cycle: MAPFRE North America FY2025 net result €139M (+42%) on ~€2.6B premiums, auto combined ratio 97.8% (-3.3 pts), U.S. premiums ~€2.3B [33] — but the profit recovery coincided with continued share loss.

## 7. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy (analytical take)

1. **The war is with Progressive, and the battlefield is MAPFRE's own agents.** MAPFRE's ~4-point share loss since 2020 [1] mirrors Progressive's rise, and Progressive's MA growth is substantially agent-fed [3]. Every MAPFRE agency is almost certainly also a Progressive agency. Defending share therefore means winning the *agent's default recommendation*, not just the customer: quote speed, ease of doing business, and commission/profit-share economics benchmarked against Progressive — and against Safety's explicit "#1 or #2 in every agency" playbook [19].
2. **Telematics is now table stakes, and MAPFRE is behind.** Progressive (Snapshot), GEICO (DriveEasy, live in MA) and even the regionals (Safety's 5% discount app, Plymouth Rock's CMT-powered Road Rewards) all have live programs [7][9][17][22]; MAPFRE's DriveAdvisor dates to 2013-14 and no current MA behavioral-pricing program surfaced in public sources [34] — an explicit gap to confirm internally and close. Both regional programs run on Cambridge Mobile Telematics, a Boston vendor, so a fast partner route exists.
3. **GEICO's MA weakness is temporary — the window is now.** GEICO shed MA share while fixing profitability; it is re-entering growth mode with a rebuilt tech stack, telematics, and a 67% bigger ad budget [6][7]. The 2026-27 period is likely MAPFRE's last easy chance to consolidate defecting GEICO customers via agents before GEICO's reacceleration reaches MA.
4. **The direct-channel gap is strategically dangerous but fixable at moderate ambition.** Plymouth Rock — MAPFRE's closest structural analog — shows a credible template: a hybrid model where a ~$100M Core Direct book coexists with a dominant IA franchise, run by a hired GEICO veteran and priced via telematics [16]. MAPFRE's quote-but-can't-bind website is worse than either a pure-agent or hybrid stance: it creates shopping intent and hands the close to competitors. Minimum viable move: bind-online-with-agent-of-record (protecting channel economics), mirroring how Plymouth Rock lets customers "buy online" while keeping agents attached [17].
5. **Bundling is the retention moat the regionals are building.** Safety (#3 in MA homeowners [19]), Plymouth Rock (@Home paired with auto [16][18]) and the small home-first mutuals all use property to lock in auto. With ~80% of MAPFRE revenue in MA personal auto, account-rounding via home (own paper or partner) is the highest-leverage retention investment.
6. **Watch the flanks, not the mutuals.** Quincy Mutual, Vermont Mutual, N&D and Preferred Mutual are share donors in auto [1][2]; Liberty Mutual's IA consolidation under one brand [11][12] may create short-term agent disruption MAPFRE can recruit against. The residual market is also strategic real estate: Safety's CAR servicing-carrier role and Plymouth Rock's Pilgrim TPA (+65% volume in 2024) [16][19] monetize infrastructure MAPFRE, as the market leader, should match or exceed.

**Open items not resolvable from public sources:** MAPFRE's and competitors' actual MA commission percentages and profit-sharing terms; MAPFRE's current telematics status; Progressive's MA agent count and MA-specific direct-vs-agent mix; carrier-level MA loss ratios (available via NAIC/DOI data purchase); Progressive Home underwriting appetite in MA.
