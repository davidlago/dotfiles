# Massachusetts Private Passenger Auto Market: Size, Shares, Dynamics

**Research brief for MAPFRE Insurance growth strategy engagement**
**Prepared: July 2, 2026. All figures carry an explicit as-of date; market share data are primarily from Commonwealth Automobile Reinsurers (CAR) statistical reports as republished by Agency Checklists, and from NAIC statutory data.**

---

## 1. Executive summary

- Massachusetts private passenger auto (PPA) is a **~$7.4 billion direct written premium market (2024)**, up from ~$5.6 billion earned premium in 2021 — roughly **+30% premium growth in three years**, driven almost entirely by rate, not unit growth [1][11].
- **Commerce (MAPFRE) remains #1 with 18.2–18.6% of exposures (Aug 2025 – Feb 2026)**, but has ceded roughly **4 points of share since 2020** (22.2% in Nov 2020) and ~10 points since 2012 (28.0%) [4][6][3][12].
- The structural story of the last four years is **Progressive's doubling from ~5.8% (Nov 2021) to ~12.3% (Feb 2026)**, displacing GEICO as the #2 carrier in May 2025, while GEICO fell from a ~16.3% peak (Sept 2022) to ~11.4% [12][6][4][3].
- Rates rose hard and fast: the seven largest MA carriers took **average approved increases of 12%+ in 2023** (GEICO ~26%) and **~5% in 2024**, for a cumulative **~38% since 2022** per Division of Insurance filings analyzed by the Boston Globe [8]. The July 1, 2025 increase in mandatory minimum limits (20/40/5 → 25/50/30) added a further **~36% to minimum-coverage premiums** [9].
- Loss costs are severity-driven: bodily injury severity +9.2% YoY nationally (2025), diagnostic scans on ~88% and ADAS calibrations on >35% of direct-repair estimates, and MA's mandated zero-/low-deductible full-glass option amplifies glass/ADAS cost exposure [16][15][17].
- Profitability whipsawed: MA PPA earned a **6.6% underwriting profit in 2021**, swung to a **–3.2% underwriting loss and 4.6% return on net worth in 2023** (still better than most states; MA loss ratio 71.6% vs ~76.6% derived national), and by 2024–25 the market re-rated back to health — statewide PPA loss ratio **62.2% in 2024**, and MAPFRE's own North America combined ratio improved from **105.4% (2023) → 98.7% (2024) → 95.4% (2025)** [11][10][1][13][14].
- Latest CAR data (Feb 2026) show a **slightly shrinking exposure pool (~-18,700 YoY)** with Progressive and Travelers gaining and Safety, Arbella and Plymouth Rock slipping; Commerce's share has stabilized and even ticked up [3].

Interpolating between the anchored endpoints, and consistent with the rate-approval record (~12% in 2023, ~5% in 2024 for the top seven carriers [8]), 2022 and 2023 MA PPA DPW were plausibly in the ~$5.9–6.8 billion range — **treat as estimate, not sourced fact**. ### 2.2 Exposures (units)

CAR written-exposure counts (cumulative year-to-date, so only same-month comparisons are valid):

- **October 2025: 4,251,405 written exposures** across all carriers (10 months YTD) [2].
- **February 2026: 828,179 written exposures YTD, down ~18,700 (-2.2%) vs Feb 2025** — the pool of insured vehicles is flat-to-slightly-declining [3].
- Commerce's own exposures fell **-3.71% YoY (June 2025 vs June 2024, -17,960 exposures)** even as its share held [5].

**Takeaway:** this is a mature, no-unit-growth market. All premium growth 2022–2025 came from rate; from 2026 the rate tailwind is fading (see §4), so top-line growth reverts to share capture.

### 3.3 Who is gaining, who is losing (2020 → 2026)

**Losing:**
- **Commerce (MAPFRE): 28.0% (2012) → 22.2% (Nov 2020) → 21.0% (Sept 2022) → 18.2% (Aug 2025)** — a decade-long secular decline of ~10 points, ~4 points of it since 2020 [6][12][4]. Importantly, the bleed **stopped in 2025**: share held in an 18.1–18.3% band all year [4] and "slightly increased" in early 2026 [3] — but absolute exposures still fell -3.7% YoY (June 2025) in a shrinking pool [5].
- **GEICO: 16.3% (Sept 2022) → 11.4% (Feb 2026)** — roughly 5 points surrendered during its national retrenchment and after a ~26% 2023 MA rate increase [6][3][8].
- **Liberty Mutual: 8.2% (Nov 2021) → ~5.0% (2025)** [12][4].
- Smaller erosion at **Arbella** (7.7% → 6.9–7.2%) and, in the most recent data, **Safety and Plymouth Rock** giving back small amounts; **Allstate and Vermont Mutual** saw "notable reductions" (Feb 2026) [3].

**Gaining:**
- **Progressive: 5.8% (Nov 2021) → 12.3% (Feb 2026)** — more than doubled; overtook GEICO for #2 in May 2025 and shows "the largest percentage gains" in the latest CAR data [12][3][4].
- **Safety: 7.9% (Nov 2021) → 9.2–9.6% (2025)** — grew aggressively through the hard market (PPA policy count +14.7% in 2023) at the cost of a 107.7% combined ratio that year [12][4][20].
- **Plymouth Rock: 6.5% (Nov 2021) → ~8.0% (2025)** [12][4].
- **Travelers/Standard Fire** posting gains in the newest data [3].

**Reading:** the direct writers now hold ~23.5% combined (Progressive + GEICO, Aug 2025) [4], but the two are on opposite trajectories. Share is consolidating toward carriers with either (a) direct/digital distribution and pricing sophistication (Progressive) or (b) IA-channel players that priced ahead of trend and leaned into the hard market (Safety, Plymouth Rock). Commerce/MAPFRE sat out the land-grab while restoring margin — a defensible choice that now leaves it with a stabilized ~18% share and a repaired combined ratio, i.e., capacity to go back on offense.

## 4. Rate environment, 2022–2026

Massachusetts is a "file-and-deviate"/prior-review state where PPA rate filings go through the Division of Insurance; the record per DOI filings analyzed by the Boston Globe (Oct 2024) [8]:

| Period | Approved rate movement (seven largest carriers, ~75% of market) |
|---|---|
| 2022 | Increases resumed (most major carriers filed increases; AG objected) [19] |
| 2023 | **Average >12%** — largest one-year hike since at least 2015; GEICO ~**26%** [8] |
| 2024 (through Oct) | **~5% average**; GEICO ~10.8% [8] |
| Cumulative since 2022 | **~38%** (a $1,000 premium in 2021 → ~$1,380 by late 2024) [8] |
| Jul 1, 2025 | Statutory minimum limits raised 20/40/5 → **25/50/30**; average **minimum-coverage** premium +**36%** to ~$711/yr [9] |
| 2026 outlook | Smaller, targeted adjustments; rapid increases "beginning to calm" (directional, agency commentary) [21] |

MAPFRE itself reported US rate increases "as high as 13%" during 2023 to combat claims inflation [22]. Safety stated in early 2024 it "continues to file for rate increases across all major lines" [20]. CAR's residual-market (MAIP) rates were re-filed effective Nov 1, 2024 [23].

**Implication:** 2023–24 was the margin-restoration window; 2025–26 is a decelerating-rate environment where growth again depends on retention and new business win rates — and where the July 2025 minimum-limits change gives every minimum-coverage customer a shopping trigger.

## 5. Loss-cost trends

MA-specific, fully-public loss-cost decompositions are scarce; the picture assembled from carrier results, NAIC data, and national claims datasets:

- **Severity, not frequency, is the driver.** The NAIC 2023 profitability data for MA note a "frequency uptick tempered by stable bodily-injury severities" in 2023 [10], but by 2025 severity dominates: **bodily injury severity +9.2% YoY** nationally (LexisNexis 2025 US Auto Trends), and BI indemnity is now **52% of liability dollars paid** [16]. MA agent/trade commentary for 2026 echoes that frequency is flat while severity "continues to steadily grow" [21].
- **Repair complexity / ADAS.** Diagnostic scans now appear on ~**88%** of direct-repair-program estimates and ADAS calibrations on >**35%** (CCC Crash Course, Q4 2025) [15]. Illustrative: a Honda Civic bumper now has 26 components vs 11 previously [8].
- **Glass.** Massachusetts requires insurers to offer optional **full-glass coverage at a $0 or $100 deductible** [17]; combined with camera-behind-windshield recalibration requirements, glass claims that once cost a few hundred dollars now routinely carry calibration charges — a disproportionate MA exposure given the mandated low-deductible option. (Windshield replacement runs ~$100–$1,500 before calibration [17].)
- **Theft and behavior.** MA auto theft +16% in 2022; distracted-driving violations among younger drivers +24% in 2023 [8].
- **What it did to carriers.** Safety Insurance — a pure MA/NE proxy — saw losses +30.6% in 2023 and a **107.7% combined ratio (vs 97.2% in 2022)** [20]. MAPFRE North America's combined ratio hit **105.4% in 2023** before rate caught up [13]. The statewide PPA **direct loss ratio was 71.6% in 2023** [10], improving to **62.2% in 2024** (carrier range: Liberty Mutual 43.7% to Safety 76.1%) [1].
- **Residual market.** MAIP/FAIR-plan exposures shrank slightly in 2023, returning business to voluntary books [10] — i.e., the MA residual market did not balloon the way some states' did; no evidence of availability crisis.

| Metric (PPA unless noted) | MA | Benchmark | As of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Underwriting profit % of premium | **+6.6%** | US PPA return fell 10.5% (2020) → 4.2% (2021); 10-yr avg 4.9% | 2021 [11] |
| New England peers, u/w profit | MA 6.6% | RI 7.2%, ME 6.4%, NH 12.3%, VT 13.3%, CT –3.7% | 2021 [11] |
| Direct loss ratio | **71.6%** | ~76.6% national (derived) — MA ~4 pts better | 2023 [10] |
| Underwriting profit | **–3.2%** | national also negative | 2023 [10] |
| PPA return on net worth | **4.6%** | 7.3% national all-lines RONW | 2023 [10] |
| All-lines MA RONW | **12.5%** | 7.3% national — top-quartile state | 2023 [10] |
| Statewide PPA loss ratio | **62.2%** | — (post-rate-correction level) | 2024 [1] |
| MAPFRE North America combined ratio | 105.4% → **98.7%** (auto 101.2%) → **95.4%** | — | 2023/2024/2025 [13][14] |

**Interpretation:** MA personal auto has historically run *better* than the US average on loss ratio (regulated conservative limits until 2025, mature IA market, residual-market cession credits [10]), while producing *lower* returns on net worth than the best states because leverage and investment allocations differ. The 2022–23 severity shock hit MA later and slightly less hard than the national market, and the 2023–24 rate actions restored the market to solid profitability by 2024–25. NAIC's 2024 Profitability Report (expected ~April 2026) was not yet available to confirm 2024 state-level RONW at time of writing [24].

**Conflict note:** Agency Checklists' NAIC-derived 2024 statewide PPA loss ratio (62.2% [1]) vs. the 2023 NAIC loss ratio (71.6% [10]) implies a very sharp one-year improvement; both are NAIC-derived and plausible given ~17% cumulative 2023–24 rate flowing through, but we would re-verify the 62.2% against the NAIC 2024 profitability report when published.

## 7. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy

1. **The defensive phase worked; the window to re-attack is now.** MAPFRE traded ~4 points of MA share (2020–2025) for a combined-ratio turnaround (NA: 105.4% → 95.4% in two years [13][14]) and its share has stabilized at ~18.2–18.6% [3][4]. With statewide loss ratios back to ~62% [1] and rate momentum fading [21], 2026–27 is the cheapest point in the cycle to buy growth — before Progressive fully earns in its exposure-share lead.
2. **Progressive is the real threat, not GEICO.** Progressive doubled to 12.3% in four years, passed GEICO in May 2025, and its exposure share (12.0%) exceeds its premium share (10.6%) — meaning its premium share rises even if it stops growing units [2][1][3]. It wins on segmentation, telematics, and a direct+agency dual channel. MAPFRE's ~6-point lead over Progressive (18.3 vs 12.0) could halve within ~4 years at current trajectories.
3. **The absent direct channel is now a quantified leak.** ~23.5% of MA exposures already sit with direct writers [4], the July 2025 minimum-limits repricing (+36% on minimum coverage [9]) forced hundreds of thousands of price-sensitive customers to shop, and the Globe's core consumer advice is "shop around" [8]. A quote page that cannot bind converts MAPFRE's brand traffic into competitors' policies. Even a bind-capable direct channel positioned as agent-of-record-neutral (routing fulfillment to IA partners) would plug the leak without detonating the 80%-of-revenue agency franchise.
4. **Defend the IA base against the IA-channel insurgents.** The share MAPFRE lost went not only to Progressive/GEICO but to Safety and Plymouth Rock — independent-agency carriers that out-executed on agent experience and appetite during the hard market [12][4]. The Feb 2026 data showing Safety/Plymouth Rock/Arbella slipping [3] suggests their post-107%-combined-ratio re-pricing [20] has opened a re-capture window in the agency channel.
5. **Unit growth must come from share and cross-line, not the market.** Exposures are flat-to-down (-18.7k YoY, Feb 2026 [3]). Growth levers: win share in a decelerating-rate market; deepen auto+home bundles (MAPFRE is already the #1 MA voluntary home insurer at ~12.5% [25]); and use glass/ADAS-era claims excellence (calibration networks, severity management) as both a margin and an agent-facing selling point, given MA's zero-deductible glass mandate [17][15].
