# Commerce's Massachusetts Price Position: SERFF/DOI Rate-Filing History, Telematics Filings, and Group Deviations

**Research brief for MAPFRE Insurance growth strategy engagement — prepared 2026-07-02.**
All figures carry as-of dates. Primary sources are the Massachusetts Division of Insurance (DOI) filings-on-file register and the DOI's insurer-by-insurer group-deviation postings, both pulled directly on 2026-07-02. One important access constraint is flagged throughout: the public SERFF Filing Access portal for Massachusetts (filingaccess.serff.com) blocks automated retrieval (HTTP 403), so filing-level *approved rate-change percentages* had to be triangulated from press, MAPFRE S.A. investor disclosures, and carrier statements rather than read off SERFF disposition pages. Where a magnitude could not be sourced, that is stated explicitly rather than estimated.

---

## 1. Regulatory context: how MA private passenger auto rates get made

Massachusetts moved to "managed competition" in April 2008; carriers file their own rates with the DOI, which reviews them under Bulletin 2009-13 standards before they are "placed on file," and the Attorney General's office can and does intervene in individual filings [1][9]. The DOI maintains a public register of every private passenger motor vehicle rate/rule filing placed on file since February 1, 2008, with SERFF tracking numbers and effective dates — this register (1,388 filings, accessed 2026-07-02) is the backbone of Section 2 [1]. Two structural features matter for MAPFRE:

- **Group marketing rate deviations** below the filed rate are permitted under G.L. c.175 §193R; the statutory requirement that 35% of a group's members be insured within two years has been legislatively waived, most recently through **2030** (Chapter 286 of the Acts of 2024, signed December 31, 2024) — so group discounting is a durable, expandable lever [12].
- Effective **July 1, 2025**, Chapter 275 of the Acts of 2024 raised mandatory minimum bodily-injury limits (to 25/50), forcing compliance filings across the market and raising minimum-coverage premiums (Bankrate estimated ~36% on average for minimum-coverage policies) [13][14].

**Cadence read-out:** Commerce files roughly annually (anchored on a May 15 effective date), and put through an unusually dense sequence in 2022–23 (four rate events effective 7/2022–5/2023) and again in 2025 (three rate events effective 5/2025–12/2025). Progressive files *twice a year, always as a matched pair of entities* (Direct + Casualty). Plymouth Rock is the most active filer (11 rate events in the window); Safety has settled into a semi-annual rhythm (Jan 1 / Jul 1–Sep 1); Arbella files once a year (Nov 1 or Mar 1); GEICO filed heavily in 2022–24 and — notably — has **no rate filing in the DOI register after its 09/2024-effective filing** through at least May 2026 [1].

### 2.2 Approved magnitudes (where publicly sourced)

The DOI register does not display rate-change percentages, and SERFF public access is blocked to bulk retrieval, so magnitudes below come from press and investor sources:

- **2022:** Among the five largest carriers, only MAPFRE/Commerce (**+3.0% overall**, with individual policyholders up to +13%) and Liberty Mutual (+3%) took increases; GEICO was flat, Arbella **−0.5%**, Safety **−2.3%**. The AG's office (December 2021 letter) called MAPFRE's increase excessive; the DOI let it take effect in January 2022 (Boston Globe, 2022-05-11) [9]. (Note: the Globe's "January" characterization vs. the register's 07/26/2022 effective date for CMRC-133147429 suggests the +3% was the earlier filing cycle; both are reported as filed.)
- **2023:** The seven largest MA insurers were approved for an **average rate increase of 12%+**, the largest one-year jump since at least 2015; **GEICO alone took ~26%** (Boston Globe, 2024-10-17) [8]. MAPFRE told investors its U.S. auto rate hikes ran **"as high as 13%" in 2023** (Q2-2023) [10], and its FY2023 presentation cites **cumulative U.S. tariff increases of more than 29% since January 2022** (as of Feb 2024) [11]. Safety's average written premium per PPA exposure rose **10.8% in 2023** [15].
- **2024:** The seven largest carriers received approvals averaging **~5%** through October 2024; GEICO requested no change through mid-2024 (Boston Globe, 2024-10-17) [8]. Nationally, approved private-auto rate increases averaged 7.2% YTD through Sept 2024 (S&P Global MI) [16].
- **2025–2026:** No public filing-level percentages found for Commerce's 05/2025, 07/2025, 12/2025 and 05/2026 filings — **this is a genuine gap that requires pulling the SERFF dispositions manually** (see Open Questions). Directionally, MAPFRE's FY2025 investor deck describes North America premiums as "practically stable" in local currency with the auto combined ratio improving to **97.8% (−3.3 p.p.)** — consistent with rate adequacy having been largely restored and the pricing cycle softening [17].

### 2.3 Is Commerce priced above or below agency-channel peers?

No public source publishes filed base-rate comparisons, but three triangulation points as of 2025–26:

1. **Comparison-site quote data (ValuePenguin, rates as of 2026-05-19):** GEICO $123/mo full coverage, Plymouth Rock $132, Safety $154, Arbella $163, Progressive $167 (state average $181). **Commerce/MAPFRE does not appear in these comparison tables at all** — it cannot be quoted online by aggregators, so its street price is invisible to shoppers [18].
2. **Share trajectory as a revealed-price signal:** Commerce's PPA share fell from ~22% (2022, Globe) [9] to 19% (2024, Globe) [8] to **18.29% of exposures (Oct 2025, CAR data via Agency Checklists)** [19], roughly stabilizing at 18.64% in Feb 2026 monthly CAR data while Progressive (12.32%) and GEICO (11.44%) posted "the largest percentage gains" [20]. Commerce led the 2023 hard-market repricing (rate events effective 1/2023, 2/2023, 4/2023, 5/2023) while Safety and Arbella had *cut* rates in 2022 and lagged on the way up — implying Commerce spent 2023–24 priced *above* its agency-channel peers on renewal books, which is when the share slide accelerated.
3. **Current posture:** MAPFRE's FY2025 auto combined ratio of 97.8% [17] versus flat local-currency premiums suggests Commerce is now at or near rate adequacy but still not price-led; Safety and Plymouth Rock continued filing increases into 2026 (Safety: 1/2026, 7/2026, 11/2026 effectives; Plymouth Rock: 4/2026, 10/2026) [1], which gives Commerce a closing window in which peers are still raising while Commerce could hold.

**Bottom line (assessment):** Commerce is best characterized as having been priced *ahead of* (i.e., above) Safety/Arbella through the 2023–24 cycle and roughly converged by 2025–26; against GEICO it is clearly above on street price for clean risks [18]. Filing-level confirmation requires the SERFF dispositions (rate-change % and rate-level histories), which are retrievable manually through the DOI/SERFF Filing Access portal but were not machine-accessible for this brief [21].

## 3. Q2 — MotionSmart's shrinking enrollment discount, and the DOI's revealed telematics tolerance

### 3.1 MotionSmart history

- **Launch (July 2021):** MotionSmart, built on Cambridge Mobile Telematics (CMT), offered **up to 10% at initial enrollment** and **up to 20% savings at renewal** based on driving behavior; new-business only, Massachusetts [2][3][4].
- **Today (page accessed 2026-07-02):** MAPFRE's MotionSmart page offers **"up to a 2% discount on your new Auto policy,"** states that **"policy premium may increase for the riskiest drivers,"** requires a first trip within 30 days, and reserves the right to **use collected data to rate current and future policies** [5].

So over 2021→2026 the program moved from a large up-front participation credit toward a small enrollment credit plus a behavior-based renewal adjustment **including upward movement for risky drivers**. The intermediate SERFF filing steps (which CMRC filings amended the MotionSmart rule, and when the 10%→2% cut happened) are not visible in public reporting; the DOI register shows Commerce rule and form/rate/rule filings in 09/2022, 12/2023, 01/2024, and 2025 that are the candidates, but the dispositions must be pulled from SERFF to date the change precisely [1][21]. **No public evidence was found that the DOI forced the reduction.**

**Revealed regulatory tolerance:** the DOI has approved, for Plymouth Rock, a **10% at-quote telematics enrollment discount and a ±20% renewal swing including surcharges** [6], and Progressive's MA program contemplates surcharges for opt-outs and risky drivers [22][23]. That is far wider than MotionSmart's current 2% front-end credit. **Implication: MAPFRE's discount shrink was almost certainly a business/actuarial decision (participation-discount loss cost, adverse selection, hard-market margin repair), not a DOI ceiling.** MAPFRE has regulatory headroom to re-file a materially bigger telematics offer.

## 4. Q3 — Third-party at-quote driving scores (Arity IQ, TransUnion/CMT) in approved MA filings

- **No approved MA private passenger filing using a third-party at-quote driving score (Arity IQ / Drivesight, or TransUnion's CMT-based score) as a rating input was identifiable from public sources.** Arity states Drivesight is "filed and approved in 48 states and Washington, D.C." (elsewhere: 2.0 in 47 states; 3.0 in 33 states), but publishes no state list, and no MA-specific Drivesight approval could be confirmed [27][28].
- Arity does claim — in its own marketing, so treat as directional — that **"one carrier used [Geosight, ZIP-code-level aggregated driving-behavior data] to revise and gain approval for rates in Massachusetts — one of the toughest states for filings"** [29]. That is territory-level driving data in an approved MA filing, not an individual at-quote score, but it shows the DOI has accepted third-party telematics *data* in ratemaking.
- The closest MA precedent for telematics-informed **new-business** pricing is Plymouth Rock's DriveSavely 10%-at-quote discount (prospective monitoring commitment, not a purchased historical score) [6].
- Massachusetts also bars credit-based insurance scores and education/occupation in PPA rating, and is broadly regarded as one of the most restrictive rating states [29]; any Arity IQ-style filing would face first-mover regulatory work. MAPFRE's five-year MotionSmart/CMT dataset is a proprietary near-substitute: its own policy language already reserves the right to use collected data "to rate your current or any future policy" [5].

**Verdict:** the DOI would plausibly entertain consent-based telematics at quote (Plymouth Rock precedent), but nobody has yet banked an approval for third-party purchased driving scores at quote in MA that we can point to. First mover would need to build the filing case.

With the 35% membership rule waived through 2030 [12], group deviations are the cleanest approved mechanism to deliver targeted price cuts through independent agents without repricing the whole book.

## 6. Q5 — Progressive's channel-differentiated rating in MA, and what it implies for a MAPFRE direct/hybrid channel

The DOI register shows Progressive files **every rate change as a same-day pair of separate legal entities: Progressive Direct Insurance Company (direct channel) and Progressive Casualty Insurance Company (agency channel)** — e.g., PRGS-134054730 and PRGS-134042629, both received 2024-05-03 and effective 08/02/2024, and the 2025 pair PRGS-134388413/-134388645 effective 07/01/2025 [1]. Progressive Direct has filed in MA since managed competition began (initial rate filing SERFF #112178, filed effective 05/01/2008) [1], and the independent-agent association (MAIA) tracks "Progressive Direct" filings as a distinct company page [36]. Because each entity files its own rates with its own expense provisions, Progressive can — and structurally does — carry **different expense loads and rate levels for direct vs. agency business** in Massachusetts. (The entity-level *rate relativities* between the two books are inside the SERFF filings and were not machine-retrievable; the structure itself is documented [1][21].)

**Implication for MAPFRE:** the accepted Massachusetts template for adding a direct channel is a **separate underwriting company with its own filed rate set**, not a channel factor inside one filing. MAPFRE USA already owns multiple MA-licensed entities in the Commerce group (e.g., Citation Insurance Company; American Commerce Insurance Company, the historical ACIC direct/affinity writer) through which a direct or hybrid book could be filed with a lower acquisition-expense load — leaving The Commerce Insurance Company's agency rates, and its 280 agent-anchored group deviations, untouched. The Plymouth Rock precedent is also instructive: it now lets MA customers "manage their auto policy directly" and enroll in DriveSavely in-app while remaining an agency-first carrier [6][7]. A "quote online / telematics at quote / bind through your agent-of-record" hybrid is filable today with no novel regulatory ask.

## 7. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy

1. **The price story is a timing story.** Commerce led the market up in 2023 (four rate events in 10 months; U.S. cumulative increases >29% since Jan 2022 [11]) while Safety/Arbella lagged from a lower 2022 base — that relative-price gap is when Progressive/GEICO took share and Commerce slid from ~22% to ~18.3% [8][9][19]. With auto CR at 97.8% (FY2025) [17] and peers still filing increases into late 2026 [1], MAPFRE has a 12–18 month window to *hold rate*, reopen the competitiveness gap versus Safety/Plymouth Rock/Arbella on agent comparative raters, and recapture agency share — the cheapest growth available.
2. **Re-arm MotionSmart; the DOI is not the constraint.** The regulator has already approved 10% at-quote and ±20% renewal swings with surcharges (Plymouth Rock DriveSavely) [6]. MotionSmart's retreat to 2% [5] concedes the self-selecting best risks — precisely the segment Progressive harvests with Snapshot — to competitors. A re-filed 8–10% participation discount funded by a symmetric surcharge structure is squarely within revealed regulatory tolerance.
3. **Group deviations are the under-levered agency weapon.** Commerce has the state's biggest group franchise (280 groups) but the shallowest standard discount (4% vs. peers' 5%, with peer approvals up to 10%) [30–34]. With the 35% rule waived to 2030 [12], deepening top groups to 5–6% and adding employer groups is an approved, targeted, agent-friendly way to buy back share without a general rate cut.
4. **A direct channel does not require repricing the agency book.** The Progressive two-entity template is 18 years old in Massachusetts [1]. Filing a direct/hybrid rate set on an existing affiliate (Citation/ACIC) with a lower expense load is a known regulatory path; the binding constraint is distribution politics and build cost, not the DOI.
5. **Close the evidence gap before finalizing pricing moves:** pull the actual SERFF dispositions (rate-change %, indicated vs. taken, expense exhibits for Progressive Direct vs. Casualty; MotionSmart rule amendments) via manual SERFF Filing Access requests or a DOI public-records request — the portal blocks automated access [21], and these numbers are the difference between a directional and a bankable price-position map.
