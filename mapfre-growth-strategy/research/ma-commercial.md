# Small Commercial and Other Growth Lines Through the Same Agents
## Research brief for MAPFRE Insurance (U.S.) growth strategy — Massachusetts small commercial opportunity

**Prepared:** July 2, 2026. All figures carry their as-of dates; where sources conflict, both are shown.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- Massachusetts small commercial is a real, sizable adjacency: roughly **$1.4B in commercial auto (2024)** [3], roughly **$1.3B in workers' compensation (2018, latest public state figure)** [5], and at least **~$1.0B+ in commercial multiple peril/BOP (last public state figure: $1.05B in 2014)** [8] — an addressable pool plausibly in the **$3.5–4.5B DPW range** within a ~$23B total MA P&C market [9].
- MAPFRE is not starting from zero. **Commerce is already the #1 or #2 commercial auto writer in Massachusetts** (13.15% share YTD Feb 2026 per CAR; 11.6–11.7% full-year basis), in a statistical dead heat with Safety [1][2][3]. It also has live BOP, commercial package, and commercial umbrella products in MA distributed through the same independent agents [4].
- Historically, MAPFRE ran commercial lines in multiple states but **stopped renewing commercial lines everywhere except Massachusetts in 2019** as part of its profitability-first retrenchment [10][11]. What remains is a profitable, MA-only commercial franchise attached to the largest P&C agency plant in the state.
- The economics are attractive but not free money: nationally, independent agents place **87.2% of all commercial lines premium** (2024) [16], commercial lines ran a **~94% combined ratio vs. 109% for personal lines in 2022** [17], and MA commercial auto ran a **60.5% direct loss ratio in 2024** [3]. But MA workers' comp rate levels have been cut hard (–14.6% average in July 2024; a +7.1% request rejected in May 2025) [6][7], and small commercial is scale- and technology-intensive, with large national carriers taking share.
- The closest comparable — **Safety Insurance, which built commercial auto + BOP to ~18% of its book ($213M of $1.19B DWP in 2024)** on the identical MA independent-agent chassis [14] — is the strongest single piece of evidence that this lane is credible for MAPFRE. Verdict at the end: **credible growth lane, if scoped narrowly** (commercial auto deepening + agent-driven BOP/account rounding), **a distraction if scoped broadly** (standalone workers' comp, middle market, new states).

## 2. Q1 — The Massachusetts small commercial market: size and leaders

### 2.1 Commercial auto (~$1.4B, MAPFRE already at the top)

Two data sources exist, and they broadly reconcile:

- **NAIC/S&P statutory data (full-year 2024):** MA commercial auto direct written premium was **$1.43 billion**, the largest of the six New England states (49% of a $2.93B regional market). The state's average direct loss ratio was a favorable **60.53%** [3].
- **CAR (Commonwealth Automobile Reinsurers) statistical data:** YTD October 2025 written premium was **$1.19B** (annualizing to ~$1.4B, consistent with the above); YTD February 2026 was $233.8M [1][2].

**Top writers (CAR data, YTD Oct 2025)** [2]:

| Rank | Carrier | Premium (YTD Oct 2025) | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Safety | $154.5M | 12.94% |
| 2 | **Commerce (MAPFRE)** | $138.9M | 11.64% |
| 3 | Arbella | $131.6M | 11.03% |
| 4 | Progressive | $107.9M | 9.04% |
| 5 | Travelers | $84.8M | 7.11% |
| 6 | Plymouth Rock | $57.5M | 4.82% |

By February 2026 (YTD), **Commerce had retaken the #1 spot at 13.15% vs. Safety's 13.05%**; Commerce and Safety together hold ~26.2% of the market [1]. On a full-year-2024 group basis, the order was Safety 11.86%, MAPFRE Group 11.71% (~$167M), Arbella 9.40% [3]. Either way: this is a two-horse race between the state's two big personal-auto agency carriers, with 68–70 carriers active and the top 10 holding ~69–75% [1][2]. Half the top ten are New England-based regionals [1].

### 2.2 Workers' compensation (~$1.3B, fragmented, rate-pressured)

- Latest public state-level market sizing: **$1.28B DPW in 2018**, written by 252 carriers; the top 20 held only 57.1% [5]. No single carrier is dominant: the largest writer, MA-based **A.I.M. Mutual (Associated Industries of Massachusetts), held just 7.51%** in 2018; Chubb (ACE American), AIG (New Hampshire Ins.), and two Travelers entities rounded out the top five at 3–5% each [5]. We could not find a more recent public MA-specific WC market-share table; this is a data gap (S&P Cap IQ Pro would close it).
- Rate environment is adverse to growth economics: the Commissioner ordered an **average –14.6% rate decrease effective July 1, 2024** (WCRIBMA had asked for only –8.3%) [6], and in **May 2025 rejected WCRIBMA's +7.1% increase request outright** (the AGO had argued for –6.2%), leaving rates flat [7]. Agency Checklists notes WC "remains a profitable line in Massachusetts" despite two decades of rate cuts [5] — profitable, but a shrinking-premium pond.

### 2.3 BOP / commercial multiple peril (~$1B+, weakest public data)

- The last public MA-specific figure we found: **$1.05B CMP DPW in 2014 across 213 insurers, with the top 20 holding only 50.2%** [8]. Given national CMP DPW grew ~12% in 2023 alone [18], the MA CMP market today is very likely in the **$1.5–2.0B range — but this is an inference, not a sourced figure**; treat it as an open item for NAIC/S&P data pulls.
- Nationally, U.S. commercial insurance DPW reached **~$295B in 2024** [19], of which small commercial is roughly one-third — **$100–103B** in McKinsey's canonical sizing (2016; larger today) [15][20].

### 2.4 Context: total MA P&C market

Massachusetts total P&C DPW is ~$23.1B, and **MAPFRE is the state's #1 P&C group at 8.56% share / $1.98B DPW** (NAIC data reported Sept 2025), ahead of Travelers (6.03%), Berkshire Hathaway (5.97%), Liberty Mutual (5.70%) and Safety (4.90%) [9]. The three small-commercial lines together (~$3.5–4.5B) are equivalent to roughly 45–55% of the MA private passenger auto market MAPFRE already leads.

## 3. Q2 — Does MAPFRE write commercial lines today? Did it historically?

**Yes on both counts — and the surviving book is bigger than most observers realize.**

- **Today (2026):** MAPFRE Insurance actively markets, in Massachusetts only, **commercial auto, BOP, commercial package policies, and commercial umbrella**, plus endorsements for cyber, D&O, professional liability, liquor liability, and snowplow coverage — explicitly targeted at "small and medium-sized commercial clients" in habitational, contractor, retail, services, restaurant, and professional-office segments, distributed through independent agents [4]. Commerce's commercial auto book alone was ~$139M YTD through October 2025 [2] and ~$167M on a full-year-2024 group basis [3]. **No public figure exists for MAPFRE's BOP/CPP volume** (statutory line-level data by state would be needed); given Safety's BOP is only $31M [14], MAPFRE's is likely small.
- **History:** Commerce Insurance (founded Webster, MA, 1972; acquired by MAPFRE 2008) has long held "a strong market position in private passenger auto, homeowners and commercial auto" in Massachusetts [10]. In December 2017, MAPFRE USA announced a reorganization effective Jan 1, 2018: exit five states (NY, NJ, KY, TN, IN), focus on 11, sell the New York and life subsidiaries [10]. In **2019 it stopped renewing commercial lines in all states except Massachusetts**, per S&P Global Market Intelligence's February 2020 report on further U.S. cutbacks [11]. So the MA commercial book is a deliberate survivor, not an accident.
- **Organizational signal:** MAPFRE posted (and has since filled) a **"Head of Commercial Lines" role in Webster, MA** [21] — evidence of current internal investment in the line.
- **Financial capacity:** MAPFRE's U.S. business has just completed a turnaround that makes growth investment plausible: FY2024 U.S. premiums ~€2.4B (+3.8%), North America net result €97.6M (vs. ~breakeven/loss in 2023), NA combined ratio 98.7% (–6.7 pts) [12]; FY2025 U.S. premiums €2.3B, U.S. combined ratio 97.8%, NA net result €139M (+42%), described by MAPFRE as a record, with General P&C (non-auto) at a 78.9% combined ratio [13].

## 4. Q3 — Economics of small commercial through independent agents; who has grown it well

### 4.1 Channel and segment economics

- **The IA channel owns commercial lines.** Independent agencies placed **87.2% of U.S. commercial lines premium in 2024** (87.3% in 2023) and 61.5% of all P&C [16]. Any small commercial strategy for an IA-only carrier like MAPFRE pushes with the grain of the channel, not against it.
- **Underwriting margins have recently favored commercial.** Commercial lines ran a **~94% combined ratio in 2022 vs. ~109% for personal lines** (Deloitte, citing industry data) [17]; MA commercial auto's 60.5% direct loss ratio (2024) [3] is far better than national commercial auto, which has been persistently unprofitable industry-wide.
- **But the segment is structurally expensive and consolidating.** McKinsey's foundational work: small commercial is ~$100–103B and hyper-fragmented (largest carrier ~6% national share), yet carriers >$2.5B in small-commercial DPW gained **12 points of share in six years**; 70% of small-business buyers start shopping online, 82% still bind with an agent, and ~60% say they would consider buying direct — meaning digital ease-of-quote is now table stakes even inside the agent channel [15][20]. Deloitte's winning models: industry specialization, **best-in-class agent portal/servicing experience**, price flexibility, embedded partnerships, and digital platforms [17]. Straight-through processing and low-touch service are what make sub-$10K-premium accounts profitable.
- **Massachusetts-specific caveat:** WC rate levels keep being pushed down by the Commissioner [6][7], so WC growth is volume-led, not rate-led; and CAR administers a commercial auto residual market (taxi/livery/car-service classes) with appointed servicing carriers — an operational obligation/opportunity for large commercial auto writers [22].

### 4.2 Carriers that have grown small commercial well recently

| Carrier | Model | Recent performance |
|---|---|---|
| **Hanover (Worcester, MA)** | IA-only national platform; small commercial center of excellence | Small commercial DWP +8.5% (Q2 2024), +5.6% (Q2 2025), +7.6% (Q3 2025); core commercial CAY ex-CAT combined ratio 94.3% in Q3 2025 (up 2.5 pts on commercial auto severity and WC large losses) [23][24] |
| **Travelers Select (small commercial)** | IA channel, BOP-led digital platform | Select Accounts +3–4% in FY2025; Business Insurance segment underlying combined ratio 88.0% FY2025 [25] |
| **Progressive** | Commercial auto-led small business, agents + direct | #4 in MA commercial auto at 9.04% (Oct 2025) [2]; #1 in CT (12.11%) and RI (14.16%) commercial auto (2024) [3] — proof a personal-auto-DNA carrier can win commercial auto in New England |
| **Safety (Boston)** | The direct MAPFRE analog: MA personal-auto IA carrier | Commercial auto $181.7M (15.2% of DWP) + BOP $31.1M (2.6%) in 2024 = ~18% of book; commercial auto DWP +15.7% YoY 2023→2024; 828 agents; 82.2% of home policyholders also hold Safety auto (account-rounding culture) [14] |
| **Arbella (Quincy)** | MA mutual, IA channel | #3 in MA commercial auto (11.03%, Oct 2025) with presence in RI and CT [2][3] |
| **W.R. Berkley** | Specialist small/mid commercial | Leading commercial auto writer in ME and NH (2024) [3] |

The pattern: the growers are either (a) regional agency carriers rounding out agent relationships (Safety, Arbella, Hanover) or (b) scale players with superior quote-flow technology (Travelers, Progressive). Nobody in the MA top tier grew small commercial through a direct channel — this is an agent's market [2][3][16].

## 5. Q4 — Credible growth lane or distraction? The evidence both ways

### Evidence it is credible

1. **MAPFRE is already a market leader in the anchor line.** #1/#2 in MA commercial auto with ~12–13% share [1][2][3] — this is expansion from strength, not entry. The underwriting, claims, and CAR residual-market infrastructure already exist.
2. **The distribution is already paid for.** The same MA independent agents who produce ~80% of MAPFRE's revenue place 87% of U.S. commercial lines [16]; agents structurally prefer carriers that let them round out an account (personal auto + home + the owner's business), and Safety demonstrates the account-rounding flywheel works in this exact state [14].
3. **The Safety proof point.** A carrier with a near-identical footprint and agent plant runs ~18% of its book in commercial auto + BOP at healthy margins and is growing it double digits [14]. MAPFRE matching Safety's commercial mix on its larger premium base implies a **$150–250M incremental premium opportunity** over time (analyst estimate, not a sourced figure).
4. **Profitability tailwind and diversification logic.** MA commercial auto loss ratio 60.5% (2024) [3]; MAPFRE's U.S. combined ratio is now sub-98 and its non-auto P&C runs high-70s/low-80s [12][13]. Small commercial would dilute MAPFRE's extreme concentration in one line (MA personal auto) that is exposed to regulatory rate cycles.
5. **Management signal already exists** (Head of Commercial Lines hire; live BOP/CPP/umbrella shelf) [4][21] — this is acceleration of an existing capability, not a cold start.

### Evidence it is a distraction

1. **The pond is much smaller than personal auto.** MA commercial auto is ~$1.4B [3] vs. ~$5B+ MA private passenger auto; MAPFRE's realistic incremental share gain in commercial auto (already ~12%) is modest. Real growth would require BOP/WC, where MAPFRE has little share and no public evidence of scale.
2. **Small commercial is a technology arms race MAPFRE hasn't run.** Winning requires straight-through quote/bind portals and servicing that Travelers, Hanover, and Progressive have spent a decade and billions building [15][17][20]; MAPFRE cannot even bind personal auto direct today, and large carriers gained 12 share points in six years nationally [15].
3. **Workers' comp economics in MA are rate-suppressed** (–14.6% in 2024; increase denied in 2025) [6][7]; entering a fragmented, long-tail line at the bottom of its rate cycle, with no WC book today, is a classic value trap for a personal-lines carrier.
4. **MAPFRE's own history counsels caution:** it non-renewed commercial lines outside MA in 2019 precisely because sub-scale commercial books underperformed [10][11]. Group discipline (Madrid's profitability-first mandate after the 2024–25 turnaround) [12][13] will not tolerate a loss-making expansion.
5. **Commercial auto severity is deteriorating even for the best operators** — Hanover's 2025 combined-ratio slippage was driven by commercial auto and WC large losses [24].

## 6. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy (analytical take)

**Small commercial is a credible growth lane for MAPFRE — but only in a tightly-scoped, agent-led, Massachusetts-first form.** The decisive facts are (i) MAPFRE is already co-leader of MA commercial auto, (ii) its agents write 87% of the nation's commercial premium and want account-rounding markets, and (iii) Safety — the same business model, in the same state, through overlapping agents — proves the model at ~18% of book. This is one of the rare growth options available to MAPFRE that requires **no new distribution, no new geography, and no direct-channel capability it lacks.**

Recommended shape of the play:

1. **Defend and extend commercial auto first** (contractors' fleets, service vehicles, snowplow niche it already endorses [4]): target moving from ~12% to Safety-parity 13–14% share, worth ~$20–30M DPW, at MA's favorable 60% loss ratio [2][3].
2. **Scale BOP as the account-rounding product**, not a standalone conquest line: pursue the ~500K MA small businesses via the agent force, with a modern quote/bind portal as the single biggest capability investment (Deloitte's "preferred carrier" agent-experience thesis [17]). A realistic 5-year ambition is Safety-scale BOP ($30–50M), not Travelers-scale.
3. **Do not build workers' comp organically.** If WC is needed to complete the small-commercial account (agents will demand it), source it via a partnership/fronting arrangement (e.g., with a WC specialist like A.I.M. Mutual) rather than balance-sheet entry into a rate-declining, long-tail line [5][6][7].
4. **Sequence it behind the personal-lines digital agenda, not ahead of it.** The same quote/bind technology deficit that blocks MAPFRE's direct personal channel blocks small commercial STP; one platform investment should serve both.
5. **Frame it to Madrid as diversification at accretive margins:** non-auto P&C is MAPFRE North America's best-performing segment (78.9% combined ratio in 2025) [13], and small commercial shifts mix toward exactly that profile.

Bottom line: a **$150–250M incremental DPW opportunity over ~5 years at commercial-lines margins**, achievable through existing agents — worth pursuing as the #2 or #3 growth initiative, behind fixing the core personal-lines retention/digital agenda, and explicitly fenced to Massachusetts and to commercial auto + BOP.
