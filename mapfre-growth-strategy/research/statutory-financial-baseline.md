# MAPFRE USA Statutory Baseline: Line/State Premium Mix, 2024–25 Results, AM Best Status, and Peer Cost Benchmarks

**Research brief — prepared 2026-07-02.** Scope: MAPFRE U.S.A. Corp. and its lead underwriting subsidiary The Commerce Insurance Company (Webster, MA), NAIC statutory and IFRS data 2019–2025, AM Best rating reconciliation, Massachusetts agency-channel peer benchmarks (Safety, Plymouth Rock, Arbella), and complaint-ratio verification.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- **The client's "~80% Massachusetts" claim is directionally right but imprecisely worded.** In 2024, MAPFRE's Massachusetts all-lines direct written premium (DWP) was roughly $2.0 billion [7] against total U.S. premiums of approximately €2.4 billion (~$2.6 billion at 2024 average exchange rates) [5] — i.e., **roughly 75–80% of U.S. premium is Massachusetts, all lines combined**. Massachusetts *private passenger auto alone* was $1.326 billion in 2024 [6], or **only ~51% of the U.S. book**. MA personal lines (PPA + homeowners) is ~66%. The strategy document should use the precise decomposition, because the non-auto and non-MA residuals matter for growth options.
- **A meaningful residual West Coast book exists (CA/OR/AZ/WA), not an OH/PA one.** The 2023 MA DOI examination lists the group's top five states as Massachusetts, Connecticut, **California, Washington**, and Rhode Island [1]; Commerce West still markets personal and commercial auto through independent agents in California, Oregon and Arizona [13].
- **The "$291 million" group DWP figure in the 2023 exam is a typo** — Commerce alone earned $1.14 billion net in 2023 at a 70.8% pool share [1]; the true group figure is on the order of $2.5–2.9 billion (see §3.3).
- **Commerce's statutory results were deeply unprofitable in 2022–23** (net losses of $54.5M and $41.5M; 2023 statutory underwriting loss of $109.2M on $1.14B earned) [1], but the U.S. operation swung to profit in 2024 (North America net result +€97.6M vs –€1.8M in 2023; combined ratio 98.7%) and posted a record 2025 (NA combined ratio 95.4%, U.S. result €118.2M) per MAPFRE S.A. IFRS disclosures [4][5].
- **AM Best status is unambiguous once checked at source: A (Excellent) / "a+", stable, affirmed 23 October 2025** for Commerce and the four other U.S. pool members [2][3]. MoneyGeek's "currently unrated per MAPFRE's request" is wrong for the operating entities; the only genuine withdrawal was MAPFRE Insurance Company of Florida in April 2022 after it left the pool [15].
- **On cost, MAPFRE is roughly at parity with the MA agency-channel peer set, not advantaged.** Its 2023 statutory other-underwriting-expense ratio was ~29.0% of net earned premium [1], versus Safety at 30.7% (2023), 30.2% (2024) and 29.0% (2025) [8][9]. Its problem in 2022–23 was the loss ratio, not expenses; both have since improved.
- **The complaint-ratio "conflict" resolves cleanly:** the flagship Commerce entity indexes below the 1.0 NAIC average (NerdWallet, using three years of NAIC data: "fewer than expected complaints" [11]); MoneyGeek's ">2x average" reflects a **range across subsidiaries of 0.62–9.99**, where tiny-premium pool members mechanically produce high indices [12].
- **The franchise is eroding where it matters most:** Commerce's MA private passenger auto share has slid from 22.1% (2020) to 18.33% (December 2025), while Progressive has grown from 5.1% to 12.17% over the same window [10].

### 2.2 Rest of New England and residual states

- Commerce is **licensed in eight states but writes in six**: MA, NH, ME, VT, CT, RI [1] (exam as of 12/31/2023, published 2024–25).
- In 2024, **MAPFRE does not appear in the top-10 private passenger auto writers in CT, RI, NH, ME or VT** [6], nor in the top-10 homeowners writers in any New England state outside Massachusetts [7a]. The northern New England books are therefore small (each below the ~1–3% share cutoff of those states' top-10 tables); exact per-state DWP is not published in free sources — flagged as a data gap requiring S&P Global Market Intelligence or NAIC Schedule T access.
- **Residual non-Northeast business is West Coast, not OH/PA/AZ-Midwest:** the group's top five states by DWP in 2023 were MA, CT, **California, Washington**, RI [1]. Commerce West (domiciled CA, founded 1948) currently offers preferred and non-standard personal auto plus monoline commercial auto through independent agents in **California, Oregon and Arizona** [13]; American Commerce (domiciled Columbus, OH) is the vehicle for AAA-club distribution. The 2017 reorganization designated 11 core states (MA, CT, RI, NH, ME, VT, OH, CA, OR, WA, ID), exited NY/NJ/KY/TN/IN, and put PA/AZ/FL on "reinforce before expanding" status [14]; the NY company was sold to Plymouth Rock (effective 1/1/2022) and the Florida entity left the pool in 2022 [1][15]. **No public evidence of a material Ohio or Pennsylvania book remains as of the 2023 exam's top-five state list** [1]; residual writings there, if any, are de minimis.
- MAPFRE's group technically wrote across "fifty-two states and territories" in 2023 [1] — small amounts of countrywide business (e.g., assumed pools, runoff) exist beyond the core footprint.

### 2.3 Verifying the "~80% Massachusetts personal auto" claim

Using 2024 figures: MA all-lines ~$2.0B [7] ÷ U.S. total ~$2.6B (€2.4B IFRS at ~1.08 USD/EUR average 2024 [5]) ≈ **~77%**. So "≈80% Massachusetts" is accurate **for the state**, but:

- **MA private passenger auto specifically ≈ 51%** of U.S. premium ($1.33B / ~$2.6B) [6][5];
- MA personal lines (PPA + HO) ≈ 66%;
- Caveat: numerator is statutory DWP, denominator is IFRS revenue; treat as ±3–4 points. A statutory-to-statutory ratio requires the group's NAIC combined Schedule T, which is not free — noted as an open item.

**Recommended phrasing for the strategy document:** "roughly three-quarters of U.S. premium is Massachusetts; roughly half is Massachusetts personal auto."

## 3. The Commerce Insurance Company pool: statutory results (Question 2)

### 3.1 Pool structure (as of 12/31/2023) [1]

Commerce is lead insurer, assumes 100% of pool premium and losses, and retrocedes: **Commerce 70.8%, American Commerce 10.9%, Citation 7.4%, Commerce West 6.1%, MAPFRE Insurance Company 2.4%** [1]. Externally, MAPFRE RE (affiliate) takes a 35% quota share of retained MUSA business (2023 premium $19M... ### 3.3 The "$291 million" figure is a typo

The exam states "The MAPFRE Group had a total direct written premiums of over $291 million in 2023 written across fifty-two states and territories" [1]. This is internally impossible: Commerce's own **net earned** premium was $1,144.2M representing 70.8% of the pool [1], implying pool net earned of ~$1.62B *after* a ~40% external quota-share cession; gross group DWP is therefore on the order of **$2.5–2.9B**. MAPFRE S.A. IFRS data show U.S. premiums of ~€2.3B in 2023 and €2.4B in 2024 [5]. Read the exam figure as **~$2.91 billion**.

## 4. AM Best status — reconciliation (Question 3)

**Verified current status (primary source, AM Best rating center, checked 2026-07-02):** The Commerce Insurance Company carries **FSR A (Excellent), affirmed 23 October 2025, stable outlook, pooled affiliation code "p"** — no withdrawal, no "not rated" flag [2]. The 23 Oct 2025 group action affirmed **FSR A / Long-Term ICR "a+" (stable)** for all five U.S. pool members: Commerce, Citation, Commerce West, American Commerce, and MAPFRE Insurance Company [3]. The U.S. entities' ICRs were upgraded from "a" to "a+" in October 2022 [17].

**Rationale language (2024–2025 cycles, group level):** balance sheet strength "very strong" (BCAR above the threshold for strongest), "strong operating performance," "favourable business profile," "appropriate ERM"; the 2025 release cites H1-2025 group net profit of €918M and "improved performance in the motor line of business" [3][16]. Since ~2022 AM Best has rated the U.S. entities within the MAPFRE S.A. group action rather than via a standalone MAPFRE USA release — the last standalone U.S. releases (2019–2021) affirmed A/stable while noting the U.S. units' weaker operating performance relative to the group [17][18].

**Reconciling MoneyGeek:** MoneyGeek's review (page dated 29 Dec 2025) states "Currently unrated per MAPFRE's request" [12]. This is **incorrect for the operating pool**; the plausible source of confusion is the April 2022 withdrawal of ratings on **MAPFRE Insurance Company of Florida** (post-pool-exit, at company request) [15]. **Trust AM Best's own rating center and the 23 Oct 2025 Business Wire release** over aggregator summaries. For the strategy document: rating adequacy is *not* a constraint on growth; A/a+ stable is fully competitive in the agency channel.

All four agency carriers landed at 99–101% combined in 2024 and improved into 2025; the differentiator in the channel is now growth, and there **Safety (+7.2% DWP) and Plymouth Rock (+23% IAG volume) are growing while MAPFRE's U.S. top line is flat-to-down in local currency** [9][9a][4].

## 6. Complaint ratios (Question 5)

- **Entity vs group resolves the conflict.** NerdWallet (review dated 19 Dec 2025, three years of NAIC data): MAPFRE "had fewer than the expected number of customer complaints filed to state regulators, relative to the company's size" [11] — consistent with the tech-ops brief's **0.62 entity-level index for The Commerce Insurance Company** (index <1.0 = better than average). MoneyGeek (page dated 29 Dec 2025) reports the NAIC complaint index as **"Mixed (0.62–9.99) — varies significantly by subsidiary"** [12]. Small pool members (e.g., Citation at 7.4%, MAPFRE Insurance Co at 2.4% of the pool [1]) have tiny denominators, so a handful of complaints yields indices far above 1.0; simple averaging across subsidiaries produces the ">1.0 group-level" figure some aggregators cite. **The premium-weighted reality is dominated by Commerce at ~0.62 — below average.**
- Direct 2024/2025 NAIC CIS values could not be pulled programmatically (the CIS lookup is form-based, not static URLs); the 0.62 figure is corroborated but not independently re-verified — flag for a CIS pull by NAIC company code if exact values are needed.
- The Massachusetts DOI does not currently publish a carrier-level complaint-ratio table in its downloadable reports (its 2024 home insurance report covers loss ratios and market structure, not complaint indices) [21]; the 2023 MA DOI exam's limited market-conduct review found Commerce "investigates and settles claims on a timely and fair basis" [1].
- Counterpoint for balance: MoneyGeek ranks MAPFRE 16th in its customer-satisfaction study and notes claims processing generates complaints [12]; BBB reviews are poor (typical for auto carriers). Service is a *retention* watch-item, not a regulatory red flag.

## 7. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy

1. **The real concentration is Massachusetts (~77%), and the real product concentration is auto (~63% of MA book).** The correct strategic frame is not "80% MA personal auto" but a two-axis concentration: state (MA) and product (auto). Diversification options differ by axis — homeowners cross-sell attacks the product axis cheaply through the same agents; New England expansion attacks the state axis where MAPFRE is currently *not even top-10* in PPA in five of six states [6] despite existing licenses and agent adjacency. That is an anomalously weak position for New England's #1 personal-lines carrier and the most obvious organic growth lane.
2. **Profitability is repaired; the growth engine is not.** 2025 delivered a record €139M NA result at a 95.4% combined ratio [4], but the MA auto share slide (22.1%→18.33%, 2020→Dec 2025) with Progressive's 5.1%→12.17% rise [10] shows MAPFRE traded share for rate. With underwriting margin restored, the group can now afford to compete on price/servicing — the strategy should time growth investment to this newly opened margin window before Progressive's agency+direct machine compounds further.
3. **There is no expense-ratio war chest.** At ~29% expenses MAPFRE is merely at peer parity [1][8][9]; funding a direct channel build or price leadership requires either genuine expense take-out (the 2026 single-brand consolidation to "Mapfre Insurance Corp." [13] is a start) or accepting a multi-year drag. Benchmark: Plymouth Rock runs a ~$100M+ direct book alongside a $1.3B agency book without channel conflict blowback [9a] — proof the MA agency channel tolerates a hybrid model.
4. **Ratings and parent capital are enablers, not constraints.** A/a+ stable [2][3], group BCAR at the strongest level [16], and a parent that extracted $247M of dividends 2019–23 [1] and just posted its first €1B+ year [4] — MAPFRE S.A. has both capacity and (per its raised strategic-plan targets) appetite to fund U.S. growth. The ask to Madrid should be framed as redeploying the historical dividend stream into share recapture while the margin window is open.
5. **The West Coast residual (CA/OR/AZ/WA, top-5-state scale in CA and WA [1][13]) is a strategic decision the client must make explicitly** — subscale carriers in California's post-2023 rate environment are either a hidden option or a distraction. Recommend a dedicated hold/fix/sell analysis; the Plymouth Rock (NY) and Safeco (KY/TN/IN) precedents show clean exits are executable [14].
6. **Data gaps to close with paid sources:** 2024/2025 statutory annual statements (surplus, underwriting result, dividends), Schedule T state-level DWP below top-10 thresholds, and exact NAIC CIS complaint indices by company code.
