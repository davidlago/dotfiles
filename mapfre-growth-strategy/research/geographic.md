# Geographic Footprint Options: Where Else MAPFRE Does or Could Play

**Research brief — MAPFRE Insurance (MAPFRE U.S.A. Corp. / The Commerce Insurance Company) growth strategy engagement**
**Prepared July 2026. All figures carry as-of dates; sources numbered at end.**

---

## 1. Executive summary

- MAPFRE U.S.A. today actively markets personal lines in **11 states** — a Northeast core (MA, CT, RI, NH, ME, VT) written through The Commerce Insurance Company and Citation, plus a Western/Ohio block (CA, OR, WA, ID, OH) written mainly through AAA-club-affiliated subsidiaries (American Commerce, Commerce West, and the new Auto Club MAPFRE Insurance Company) [13][9][17].
- The company remains overwhelmingly a Massachusetts business: **~18.3% of MA private passenger auto exposures as of December 2025 (#1, but down from ~22.1% in 2020)** [11], and **$1.33B of MA private passenger auto DPW in 2024 (17.96% share)** [10]. It does not appear in the **top 10 of any other New England state's** personal auto market (2024 NAIC data) [10].
- The 2017–2022 retrenchment (exits from NY, NJ, KY, TN, IN; commercial lines non-renewed outside MA in 2019; Verti direct brand shut down) was executed in an agent-friendly way — via sale of the NY subsidiary to Plymouth Rock and replacement-carrier deals with Safeco and Palisades — which limits, but does not eliminate, reputational damage with agents in re-entry states [1][5][6][8][24].
- Among adjacent states, on 2023 NAIC profitability data, **New Hampshire (PPA underwriting profit +4.7% of premium; return on net worth 10.4%)**, **Maine and Vermont (roughly break-even underwriting, ~6.6–6.8% RNW)** and **Rhode Island (-0.7% UW, 6.7% RNW)** were materially more profitable than **Connecticut (-9.4% UW, 1.4% RNW)** and especially **New York (-17.2% UW, -3.8% RNW)** [20].
- MAPFRE already holds licenses and files rates in the six New England states (Commerce writes all its business in those six states today [9]), so "expansion" in New England is a **share-growth and agent-appointment problem, not a licensing problem**. True re-entry (NY, NJ) would face a slower regulatory path, a residual-market obligation, and the memory of the 2018 withdrawal.
- A material near-term risk cuts across all of this: MAPFRE's **exclusive AAA Northeast joint marketing agreement — >$200M of 2025 premium — expires December 31, 2026**, and AAA Northeast is already steering members to its own carrier, per MAPFRE's January 2026 lawsuit [19].

## 2. Where MAPFRE plays today, and at what scale (Q1)

### 2.1 The corporate footprint

The Massachusetts Division of Insurance's examination of The Commerce Insurance Company (as of Dec 31, 2023; report dated May 22, 2025) is the best primary source on footprint [9]:

- Commerce is **licensed in eight states but writes the entirety of its business in six: MA, NH, ME, VT, CT, RI**, distributing "primarily … through a network of independent agents."
- Commerce is the **lead insurer of an intercompany pool**; 12/31/2023 participations: Commerce 70.8%, American Commerce (OH-domiciled) 10.9%, Citation 7.4%, Commerce West (CA-domiciled) 6.1%, MAPFRE Insurance Company (NJ-domiciled) 2.4%. MAPFRE Insurance Co. of New York was removed from the pool effective Jan 1, 2019 upon its sale to Plymouth Rock.
- The MAPFRE Group's direct premium was written "across fifty-two states and territories" (mostly residual/runoff outside the core), with the **top five states by DPW: Massachusetts, Connecticut, California, Washington, Rhode Island** (2023). (Note: the exam report states group DPW of "over $291 million in 2023"; this is internally inconsistent with the group's ~$2.9–3.0B actual volume and is almost certainly a typographical omission in the report — MAPFRE S.A. reported ~€2.4B of 2024 US premiums [15]. Flagged rather than relied upon.)

MAPFRE's consumer site currently lists **auto insurance in 11 states: MA, CT, RI, NH, ME, VT, OH, CA, OR, ID, WA** [13]; its corporate "Who We Are" page claims ~3,000 independent agents/brokers and rank as the **21st-largest US private passenger auto insurer (2024)** [12]. The 2021 fact sheet showed 12 states and **$1.9B total US written premium in 2020** [14], so one state (Arizona, still referenced on some third-party pages) has since dropped out of the marketed footprint; sources conflict on AZ's status and we could not find a formal withdrawal notice — treat AZ as residual/runoff pending confirmation.

Two structural observations:

1. **Massachusetts is ~55–60% of US premium on its own** (MA PPA $1.33B [10] plus MA homeowners and commercial auto, where MAPFRE also claims #1 positions [12], against ~$2.5–2.6B total US premium [15][16]); the client's "~80% of revenue from MA personal auto" claim is directionally consistent once MA home and the AAA book are included, though we could not verify the precise 80% from public sources.
2. **The non-Northeast footprint is not an independent-agent business.** The Western/Ohio block flows through AAA club relationships (American Commerce was created from AAA's Automobile Club Insurance Company [4-context]; the new **Auto Club MAPFRE Insurance Company, rated A- by AM Best in August 2024, is a majority-MAPFRE JV start-up writing personal lines in Washington and Idaho, 100% quota-shared into Commerce** [17]). So MAPFRE's live playbook for geographic reach outside New England is affinity partnership, not agency build-out.

### 3.2 What the exits did — and didn't — do to agent credibility

**Mitigating factors.** MAPFRE consistently arranged soft landings: a whole-company sale in NY, Palisades replacement paper in NJ, Safeco in IN/KY/TN — each announced with explicit commitments to a "seamless transition for our valued customers and independent agents" [5][6]. This is materially better agent treatment than a bare mass non-renewal, and it happened seven-plus years ago; the agency plant in KY/TN/IN was small.

**Aggravating factors.**
- The exits were framed publicly as a retreat to Massachusetts ("reinforce our market leadership position in our home state" [3]), and the 2019 commercial lines withdrawal hit agents in *retained* states (CT, RI, NH...) — agents outside MA saw MAPFRE narrow its appetite twice in three years [8]. An agent asked to invest in a MAPFRE re-entry will reasonably ask what happens at the next adverse cycle.
- In early 2020 the group was openly telling investors it wanted *further* US exits [8] — that record is public and discoverable by any agency principal.
- AM Best characterized MAPFRE U.S.A.'s operating performance as **"marginal"** with a "neutral" business profile through the period (Oct 2019 affirmation; language persisting in later affirmations) [18] — i.e., the exits were a response to genuine underperformance, not opportunistic pruning.
- The one direct-channel experiment (Verti, PA) failed and was shut without announcement [24] — relevant to any claim that a digital channel can substitute for agent distribution in new states.

**Implication.** Re-entry credibility is weakest where MAPFRE formally withdrew (NY, NJ, and the 2019 commercial-lines pullback), and strongest where MAPFRE never left and already has appointments (CT, RI, NH, ME, VT). In those six states the story agents need is not "we're back" but "we now have real appetite, competitive product, and a multi-year commitment" — a lower bar, but one that requires visible investment (dedicated field teams, state-specific product refresh, profit-sharing terms) to clear. The August 2024 WA/ID JV shows the group *will* fund geographic growth again when structured around an affinity partner and quota-shared into Commerce [17].

### 4.3 Independent-agency channel fit

- Massachusetts has the **highest independent-agency penetration in the nation: 78.8% of all P&C premium (2024)** [23]; the surrounding New England states are traditional IA strongholds as well, though the Big "I" report does not publish their individual figures — a data gap worth closing with the paid report [23].
- Nationally IAs place only **39% of personal lines** (2024) but share is growing (35.7% in 2020 → 39%) [23], and the New England top-5 tables above show the agency-borne carriers (Travelers, Safety, Plymouth Rock, Arbella, Amica) concentrated in MA/CT — i.e., **NH/ME/VT top-5s are dominated by direct and captive writers, leaving an under-served IA shelf** that MAPFRE, Safety and Plymouth Rock all court. Notably, MA-centric rivals **Safety and Plymouth Rock already out-execute MAPFRE outside MA** (Plymouth Rock top-5 in MA plus large NH/CT/NY/NJ books; Safety writes NH and ME) — evidence the adjacent-state agency play works for an MA carrier, and that MAPFRE is late.

### 4.4 Ranking (analyst view)

1. **New Hampshire** — best 2023 economics in the region (only state with positive PPA underwriting profit), $1.2B market, contiguous agent geography, MAPFRE already licensed/writing; Safety/Plymouth Rock prove the MA-agency-carrier playbook. **Grow hard here first.**
2. **Maine, Vermont** — small ($1.1B / $0.5B) but sane economics, low catastrophe exposure, thin direct-carrier service levels for IA agents; cheap to serve as an extension of the NH push (shared product/ops).
3. **Connecticut** — biggest adjacent pool ($4.2B) and already MAPFRE's #2 state [9], but worst New England profitability in 2023 (-9.4% UW) [20]; a *fix-and-scale-selectively* market rather than a growth bet until rate adequacy is demonstrated.
4. **Rhode Island** — profitable-ish but small, rate-shocked, and Progressive owns 35.6% [10][25]; opportunistic agency wins only.
5. **New York** — scale is tempting ($16.5B) but 2023 economics were dismal (-17.2% UW; negative RNW) [20], MAPFRE sold its NY company and renewal rights to Plymouth Rock in 2018 [5][7], and re-entry would mean rebuilding licenses, filings, residual-market obligations and agent trust from zero. **Do not re-enter this cycle.** (NJ: same logic, plus the Palisades replacement deal.)

## 5. Regulatory, capital and timeline requirements for expansion/re-entry (Q4)

**Licensing mechanics.** Expansion into a new state by an existing insurer uses the NAIC **UCAA Expansion Application**; uniform states target **review within 60 calendar days of a complete application** (clock suspended while amendments are pending) [21]. In practice, for a well-capitalized A-rated group the licensing step is the *fast* part; the long poles are the items below.

**Seasoning requirements** (foreign insurer must have operated N years before admission; largely moot for MAPFRE's 50-year-old carriers but relevant if a new-entity strategy like the ACMIC JV is used) [22]:

| State | Seasoning | Waiver |
|---|---|---|
| CT | 2 years | Merger-survivor waiver |
| ME | 3 years + demonstrated volume in requested lines | Regulatory waivers available |
| NH | 5 years | Commissioner may waive (e.g., wholly-owned sub of licensed group) |
| VT | 5 years | Waivable if parent/affiliate profitably writes the line |
| NY | 3 years | Superintendent may waive |
| RI | Retaliatory | — |

(California, for reference, requires 3 years of actively transacting the requested classes [21-context]; the ACMIC precedent shows MAPFRE can stand up a new rated entity — A- from AM Best at launch — inside ~a year when it is quota-shared 100% into Commerce [17].)

**Beyond the license — the real timeline drivers:**
- **Rate/form/rule filings** per state and line; personal auto re-pricing cycles mean 6–18 months from filing to a competitive book. In already-licensed New England states, MAPFRE can skip licensing entirely and go straight to product refresh and agent appointments.
- **Residual market obligations** accrue with market share (CAR in Massachusetts — the source of the exposure data above [11]; NY's and NJ's assigned-risk plans would attach on re-entry).
- **Capital**: no new statutory entity is strictly needed for New England growth (Commerce/Citation are licensed); growth consumes surplus via premium leverage, but the group holds A (Excellent) ratings with stable outlook (AM Best affirmations through Oct 2025) and explicit parent support [18][26], so capital is not the binding constraint — appetite is.
- **Re-entry frictions**: where MAPFRE sold renewal rights (NJ) or the whole company (NY), non-compete/renewal-rights terms from the 2018 Plymouth Rock transactions could constrain re-entry; terms were not disclosed [5]. Some states also apply statutory re-entry waiting periods after formal withdrawal plans; we could not confirm a specific bar for NY/NJ from public sources (open question).

**Bottom-line timelines (analyst estimate, drivers cited above):** scale-up in already-licensed New England states ≈ 12–24 months to meaningful volume (product refresh + agent appointments, no licensing); a new state where licensed-but-dormant ≈ 18–30 months; NY/NJ re-entry ≈ 3+ years including licensing/filings, residual-market onboarding and agency plant rebuild.

## 6. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy

1. **The geographic question is really a concentration question.** MAPFRE is #1 in a $7.4B MA auto market but its share has bled ~4 points since 2020 (22.1% → 18.3%, Dec 2025) [11] while Progressive and GEICO grew — and its single largest affinity feed (AAA Northeast, >$200M, exclusive) terminates 12/31/2026 with the partner already defecting to its own carrier [19]. Standing still in MA is a shrinking-share, shrinking-moat position; adjacent-state growth is the natural hedge that also de-risks the MA regulatory/weather cycle.
2. **The cheapest expansion is where MAPFRE already sits licensed and appointed: NH first, then ME/VT, fix CT.** No UCAA filings needed; the constraint is product competitiveness and agent mindshare. NH offers the region's best underwriting economics (only positive 2023 UW margin) [20] and Safety/Plymouth Rock have proven that an MA agency carrier can win there. A "New England parity" program — one product architecture, one agency compensation grid, MA-grade service levels in all six states — converts MAPFRE's MA service reputation into a regional asset.
3. **Rebuild trust with visible commitment devices.** After 2017–2020, agents will discount verbal commitments. Concrete devices: multi-year state-level appetite statements, guaranteed profit-sharing floors for early-adopting agencies in NH/ME/VT, dedicated regional underwriting teams, and public premium targets. The WA/ID Auto Club JV [17] is proof the group will fund growth again — market that fact.
4. **Do not re-enter NY/NJ this cycle.** Worst profitability in the region (NY 2023 RNW -3.8%) [20], sold renewal rights/entities to a formidable competitor, longest regulatory path, and zero agency plant. Revisit only if NY rate adequacy is restored for consecutive years.
5. **Decide what the Western block is for.** CA/WA/OR/ID/OH is strategically orphaned from the MA agency franchise and exists mainly through AAA-club economics [9][17]. Either scale it deliberately via the ACMIC affinity model — which the AAA Northeast litigation suggests needs contractual hardening — or treat it as a divestment candidate to fund the New England push (Plymouth Rock precedent shows exits can be executed cleanly [5][7]).
6. **The direct-channel gap matters more in expansion states than in MA.** In NH/ME/VT, the top of the market is Progressive/GEICO/State Farm [10]; an agency-only entrant fights uphill for digitally-shopping consumers. Verti's failure [24] argues against a standalone direct brand; a bind-capable digital quote flow that routes to agents (agent-assisted direct) is the minimum viable answer and is a prerequisite for expansion-state economics.
