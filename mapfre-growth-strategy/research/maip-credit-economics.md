# MAIP Economics: Rate Adequacy, Credit Values, and the Size of Commerce's Residual-Market Subsidy

**Research brief for MAPFRE U.S. growth strategy engagement — prepared 2026-07-02**

All figures carry as-of dates. Primary sources are Commonwealth Automobile Reinsurers (CAR) Rules of Operation, CAR committee records and exhibit packets, CAR bulletins and annual reports, Safety Insurance Group SEC filings, and Agency Checklists market-share data. Where a figure could not be sourced publicly, it is explicitly labeled an estimate with stated assumptions.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- The Massachusetts Automobile Insurance Plan (MAIP) is small — a rolling-12-month residual market share of **1.64% of statewide private passenger exposures as of November 2025** (81,196 assigned car years against a 4.96M-exposure market) — but it swung violently through the 2023–24 hard market: assignments grew **+209.5% in 2023 and +62.8% in 2024**, then fell **-38.4% in 2025** after MAIP rate increases took effect [10][11].
- The economically dominant feature of the system is not the assigned business itself but the **credit system**: under current (April 1, 2026) credit factors, **485,754 credit-eligible voluntary exposures (9.8% of the market) generate $1.465 billion of potential credit premium, of which an estimated $1.364 billion (93.1%) is actually used** to offset members' MAIP quota shares [11]. The 11 protected "DOI territories" account for **$1.081 billion (73.8%)** of that potential credit premium (analyst calculation from CAR Exhibit data) [11].
- CAR's own Rule 29 formula says the credit system is now massively over-scaled: the indicated April 1, 2027 credit offer would cut credit-eligible exposures **-50.5%** and potential credit premium **-43.1%**, with **99% of the decrease coming from the 11 DOI territories** [10][11]. The Governing Committee nonetheless recommended **no change** for policies effective April 1, 2026 (Bulletin 1200, September 25, 2025) — the same outcome as every year since credit factors were frozen in 2015 [9][12].
- Commerce's client-reported **21,433 assigned car years for PY2025** equals roughly **26–29% of all MAIP exposures** (PY2025 statewide assigned exposures were 74,533 through November 2025), against Commerce's **18.22% voluntary exposure share** — i.e., roughly **1.5–1.6x its raw pro-rata share** on these public denominators, consistent with the client's 1.7x figure on a premium/quota-share basis [11][14]. By contrast, **Safety Insurance carried only 5,464 MAIP exposures in 2025 (~7% of the MAIP) on a 9.62% voluntary share — about 0.7x its pro-rata share** — demonstrating what a credit-efficient urban book achieves [13][14].
- Directional dollar sizing (assumption-flagged in §6): Commerce's assigned book is on the order of **$50–65M of written premium**; every 10 points of combined-ratio underperformance versus voluntary business costs **~$5–6M per year**. A realistic urban-territory growth program that generated $30–60M of incremental credit premium could plausibly shed a third to more than half of the assignment burden while adding voluntary premium in territories where MAIP manual rates (post-2024/2025 increases) are now at historically adequate levels.

## 2. MAIP rate adequacy: how assigned business gets underpriced

### 2.1 The structural subsidy: the "lower-of" rule

MAIP rates are cost-based manual rates set by CAR and placed on file with the Division of Insurance (DOI). But by statute (G.L. c. 175, §113H), an assigned policyholder can never be charged more than the assigned carrier's own voluntary rate: rates apply "only in circumstances where these rates do not exceed the premium charges, which would be used by each risk's Servicing Carrier for that risk if such risk were written on a voluntary basis" [2][7]. Safety Insurance's 10-K states it plainly: "insurers may only charge MAIP policyholders the lower of the MAIP rate or the company's competitive voluntary market rate" [13]. The carrier therefore always keeps the *worse* side of the comparison: residual-market risks priced at whichever rate is lower.

### 2.2 The cyclical subsidy: MAIP rates lag the market

The rate filing cadence (all placed on file by the DOI) [1][2][3][4][5][6][7]:

| Filing / effective date | What changed | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Eff. May 1, 2023 | New MAIP rates — but built on data only through **March 2022** | CAR 2023 Annual Report [16] |
| Filed Sept 2023, eff. **May 1, 2024** | Full rate revision responding to "increasing trends in loss severities and repair costs" | CAR 2023/2024 Annual Reports [15][16] |
| Eff. Nov 1, 2024 (Bulletin 1175, Aug 6, 2024) | Revised increased-limits factors only; "no other changes" | Bulletin 1175 [1] |
| Filed Dec 6, 2024 (Bulletin 1184), placed on file April 11, 2025, eff. **July 1, 2025** (Bulletins 1191, 1193) | Full rate/rule revision plus new 25/50 compulsory BI limits | Bulletins 1184, 1191, 1193 [6][7][8] |
| Placed on file March 13, 2026, eff. **July 1, 2026** | Current rates | CAR MAIP rates page [4] |

CAR does not publish an overall rate-level change percentage in its bulletins, and the SERFF filings (e.g., CARI-134311007) are not readily machine-accessible; **no public overall MAIP rate-change percentage for the 2024–2026 filings could be sourced** — this is an explicit gap. (For historical calibration, the last publicly reported figure was **+4.6% effective February 1, 2018** [17].)

The volume data, however, tell the adequacy story unambiguously. CAR's 2023 Annual Report attributes the residual-market explosion to "the sharp increase in the number of assigned policies **rated with MAIP rates**" [16] — i.e., MAIP manual rates had fallen *below* voluntary rates for a large slice of urban/high-risk consumers, making the MAIP the cheap option and pulling business in. CAR's Feb 5, 2026 Actuarial Committee records then document the reverse: assignment volumes declined steadily after each rate filing, with the 12-month residual market share falling from **1.85% (June 2025) to 1.64% (November 2025), an 11% decline**, following the July 1, 2025 rate increase [10]. Committee members in September 2025 explicitly "recognized improvements not only in companies' voluntary rates but also in **residual market rates**" [9].

**Assessment:** the assigned-business subsidy is largest in hard-market years when MAIP rates lag (2023–24 was the extreme case), and compresses after CAR re-rates. As of mid-2026 MAIP rates are the closest to adequacy they have been since 2022 — and the Actuarial Committee has commissioned a **cell-level "MAIP rate adequacy" analysis by class/territory** (requested Feb 5, 2026), tacit acknowledgment that adequacy still varies materially by cell [10].

### 2.3 What MAIP rates look like in the credit territories (eff. July 1, 2025)

Sample manual rates from the MAIP rate pages, basic-limits liability package (Part 1 BI + Part 2 PIP + Part 4 property damage at $100K — the quota-share premium basis) [18]:

| | Class 10 (adult) | Class 20 (inexperienced) |
|---|---|---|
| **Territory 22 (Boston core, a "DOI territory")** | $3,232 | $6,396 |
| **Territory 3 (outer suburban)** | $1,341 | $3,200 |
| T22 collision, $500 ded. | $3,210 | $7,851 |

For context, statewide average *voluntary* premiums are roughly $520–710/yr for minimum coverage (the jump to ~$711 reflects the July 1, 2025 move to 25/50 limits) and roughly $1,200–2,400/yr for full coverage depending on source and profile (aggregator estimates, 2025–2026; sources conflict and none is authoritative) [19][20]. The point is not that MAIP rates are "cheap" in absolute terms — urban MAIP manual rates are now very high — it is that (a) the lower-of rule caps what carriers actually collect, and (b) when MAIP rates lag voluntary rates in urban cells, the MAIP becomes the market of choice and assignment volume floods in, as 2023–24 proved.

## 3. Assignment tenure and mechanics (CAR Rules of Operation, Rule 29, rev. 2026.03.24)

All from the current Rule 29 — Assignment Process [7]:

- **Quota share:** each member's share of MAIP assignments reflects its share of statewide voluntary private passenger property-damage-liability written exposures (trailing 12 months). The premium measure used is the basic-limits manual premium (20/40 BI + $100K PD + $8K PIP through the May 2026 accounting submission; **25/50 BI thereafter**), computed at MAIP cost-based rates for both MAIP insureds *and* credit-eligible voluntary risks. Applications are assigned in real time to the most undersubscribed member measured by assigned MAIP premium ÷ **credit-adjusted quota share**.
- **Tenure: an assigned risk stays with the designated Assigned Risk Company (ARC) for three consecutive years.** At expiration, the ARC may offer a voluntary policy or non-renew. If it non-renews, the producer re-applies to the MAIP and the risk **must be assigned to a different ARC** (with a 20% deposit bill at "the lower of the MAIP premium or the voluntary premium"). Safety's 10-K confirms: "personal automobile policies are assigned to us for three years, unless the policyholder is offered a voluntary policy by another insurer" [13].
- **Monthly true-ups** adjust each member's quota share for over/under-assignment, reversed assignments, MAIP risks moving voluntary, and credits.

Implication: an underpriced assignment is a **three-year annuity of underwriting loss**, not a one-year problem, and Commerce cannot cherry-pick its way out — only credits (or LADA outsourcing) alter the intake rate.

## 4. The credit machinery: urban-territory (voluntary) credits and take-out credits

Rule 29.D–F [7]:

- **Voluntary credit:** for each exposure written voluntarily in a credit-eligible territory/operator-class cell, the member receives credit equal to **the annual MAIP premium the risk would have carried × a credit factor**. Factors are banded by the cell's residual market share: factor 1.00 for cells with 5–17% residual market share, rising in steps (1.25 / 1.50 / 1.75 / 2.00 / 2.25) to **2.50 for cells with 47%+ residual market share**. The current factor table has been **unchanged since 2015** (applicable to policies effective April 1, 2017+); the richest cells today include class 20 in territories 21 and 22 (factor 1.75) and 16, 20, 26, 40, 44 (1.25–1.50) [7][9].
- **Take-out credit:** writing a MAIP risk voluntarily at expiration earns a one-year credit at **factor 1.0** of the MAIP premium, additive to any voluntary credit; the voluntary policy must be in force ≥90 days with coverage at least equal to the replaced policy. The Actuarial Committee reviewed the take-out credit in April 2025 and voted unanimously to leave it unchanged, noting usage "varies with the size of the residual market" [8].
- **Credits are transferable assets:** Rule 29.F — "ARCs may sell, transfer, or buy excess credits to or from other ARCs." CAR calculates each ARC's excess-credit premium eligible for transfer **monthly**; sale agreements must be reported to CAR within 30 days and reviewed for rule consistency [7]. **Transaction prices are private; no public source states an indicative $/credit price.** The 2019 DOI proceeding confirms the practice: carriers "unwilling to write MAIP business" buy surplus credits from urban writers [12].
- **Aggregate cap:** credited premium exceeding 100% of a member's overall quota share cannot be applied [7].

### The 11 "DOI territories"

CAR's exhibits star 11 protected territories — **16, 20, 21, 22, 26, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45** [11]. The 2019 DOI proceeding identified the corresponding urban areas as **Chelsea, Hyde Park, Dorchester, Roxbury, East Boston/Charlestown, Holyoke, Lowell, Springfield, Lynn, Lawrence, and Brockton** [12]; committee records confirm territory 42 = Springfield [9]. DOI hearing decisions (2016, 2017, 2019) have repeatedly blocked CAR from cutting credits in these areas, and CAR's committees now treat "no decreases to the DOI territories" as a design constraint [9][10][12].

## 5. Sizing the credit pool — the $1.4–1.5 billion quota-share offset economy

CAR Actuarial Committee exhibit, "4/1/2027 Credit Offer — Data Through November 2025 (2025/07 rates)" [11]:

| Measure (current 4/1/2026 credit factors) | Value |
|---|---|
| Total market exposures (all rate classes) | 4,954,518 |
| Credit-eligible exposures | **485,754 (9.8% of market)** |
| Potential credit premium | **$1,465,072,081** |
| Estimated credits used | **$1,363,724,595** |
| Credit utilization | **93.1%** |

By rate class, class 10 (adult experienced) dominates volume ($878.7M potential credit premium on 325,966 eligible exposures — ~$2,696/exposure), while class 20 (inexperienced) is the richest per unit ($240.7M on 42,174 exposures — **~$5,707 per exposure**) [11].

The 11 DOI territories (analyst aggregation of the same exhibit): **373,175 credit-eligible exposures (76.8% of all eligible) generating $1.081B of potential credit premium (73.8% of total)** — an average of **~$2,900 of quota-share offset per voluntarily written car year**. Per-exposure values by territory range from ~$2,200 (T41, Lynn area) to ~$3,700 (T45; T22 Boston ~$3,680; T21 ~$3,430) [11].

### How far the credits are from actuarial need

Applying Rule 29's own codified methodology to current data produces an indicated April 1, 2027 credit offer of only 240,294 eligible exposures (**-50.5%**) and $833.3M potential credit premium (**-43.1%**), with "99% of the decrease result[ing] from indicated decreases in the DOI territories" and 97% concentrated in class 10 [10][11]. A year earlier the indications were similar (-39.2% exposures / -29.2% premium using data through January 2025) [8]. In other words, **roughly $400–630M of today's credit premium exists only because factors have been frozen since 2015** — CAR's own formula says the residual market no longer justifies it.

The politics (from the 2025–26 committee records) [8][9][10]:

- **Alternative models:** a staff "tempered" model (allow increases, cap decreases at 10%, no DOI-territory decreases) yields +4.5% exposures / +5.2–5.6% premium; the **Plymouth Rock model** (redefines "disproportionate representation" at a 4% threshold) yields **+22.4% / +24.5%**; the **Vermont Mutual model** (3% threshold) yields **+68.3% / +68.1%**. The September 16, 2025 vote was 8–2 for no change; Bulletin 1200 notified the Commissioner accordingly [9][12b].
- Vermont Mutual's critique is the intellectually honest one for MAPFRE's purposes: with frozen factors, "companies today are offered credit for writing some business voluntarily that **would never be in the assigned risk pool**, solely to seek credit" [8] — i.e., the credit system has become a durable subsidy conduit from under-credited members (Commerce) to structurally credit-long members.
- History rhymes: CAR's 2018 proposal to phase credits down 20%/year was **supported by MAPFRE and Arbella** and opposed by Plymouth Rock, Progressive, GEICO, Preferred Mutual, Liberty Mutual, MAIA and the AG; the Commissioner disapproved it in April 2019 — the second rejection in three years [12]. MAPFRE has been on the losing side of this fight for a decade.

## 6. Commerce's MAIP burden: sizing the drag and the recapture opportunity

### 6.1 The market context (public data)

MAIP volume trend (CAR exhibit, data as of November 2025) [11]:

| Policy year | Assigned exposures (stat-reported) | Residual market share | Assignment applications |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020 | — | 0.7% | 17,823 |
| 2021 | — | 0.5% | 12,351 |
| 2022 | 20,518 | 0.43% | 15,689 (+27.0%) |
| 2023 | 50,309 | 1.04% | 48,562 (+209.5%) |
| 2024 | 88,108 | 1.81% | 79,061 (+62.8%) |
| 2025 (thru Nov) | 74,533 | 1.62% | 48,716 full-year (-38.4%) |
| Rolling 12 mo. (Nov 2025) | **81,196** | **1.64%** | — |

Commerce (MAPFRE) held **18.22% of statewide private passenger exposures (624,158 of 3,425,772) as of August 2025** [14].

### 6.2 Commerce's share of the burden

The client-provided figure — **21,433 assigned car years, PY2025** — cannot be independently verified from public sources (CAR's MAIP Quota Share and Premium/Loss reports are member-only [21]), but it is fully consistent in magnitude: it equals **28.8% of PY2025 statewide assigned exposures through November (74,533)**, or ~26–27% if PY2025 completes near the rolling-12-month 81K level. Against an 18.22% voluntary share, that is **~1.5–1.6x pro-rata** on public exposure denominators; the client's 1.7x on a premium-basis credit-adjusted quota share is plausible since quota shares are premium-weighted and credit-adjusted [7][11][14].

The controlled comparison is Safety Insurance (10-K, filed 2026): MAIP exposures of **1,388 (2023) → 5,537 (2024, +298.9%) → 5,464 (2025)** on 482,454 voluntary auto exposures [13]. Safety's ~5.5K car years is roughly **7% of the MAIP on a 9.62% voluntary share — ~0.7x pro-rata** [13][14]. A Massachusetts-centric urban writer with a strong credit book absorbs *less* than its share; Commerce absorbs substantially more. The gap between 1.5–1.7x and 0.7x is the credit system at work — and it is the recapturable subsidy.

### 6.3 Dollar estimates (explicitly assumption-based)

No public source discloses MAIP loss ratios or Commerce's assigned-book premium; the following is analyst arithmetic anchored to CAR exhibit averages.

- **Assigned premium:** CAR's credit exhibits imply basic-limits MAIP premium of ~$2,700–3,000 per urban car year [11]; full-policy averages (many MAIP risks carry compulsory-heavy coverage, and collections are capped by the lower-of rule) plausibly average **$2,300–2,900**. Commerce: 21,433 car years × $2,300–2,900 ≈ **$49–62M written premium** (PY2025 estimate).
- **Underwriting drag:** at an assumed 5 / 15 / 25 combined-ratio-point disadvantage versus Commerce's voluntary book, the annual drag is ≈ **$2.5–3M / $7.5–9M / $12–16M**. During 2023–24-style rate lag the high end is the right planning number; at mid-2026 rate levels the low-to-middle range is more realistic. (No public MAIP combined ratio exists to calibrate this; CAR's member-only Premium/Loss reports would resolve it [21].)
- **Credit-recapture scenario:** every incremental voluntary car year written in the 11 DOI territories generates ~**$2,900 average credit premium** (up to ~$3,400–5,700 for Boston-core and class-20 cells) [11]. Mechanically, each credit dollar reduces Commerce's credit-adjusted quota share dollar-for-dollar; with ~93% of systemwide credits already used, the marginal displacement of assigned premium per credit dollar is below 1:1 (rough analyst estimate: $0.5–0.7, calibration requires member-level data). Illustratively, **+10,000 urban voluntary car years ≈ $29M credit premium ≈ $15–20M less assigned premium**, i.e., roughly a **25–35% cut in Commerce's assignment intake**, before counting take-out credits (1.0x, one year) on converted expirations. Matching Safety's 0.7x credit efficiency would shed on the order of **12,000 assigned car years (~$28–35M premium)** — worth **$3–9M/yr** of avoided underwriting loss at the assumed drag range, *plus* the margin on the new voluntary urban book itself.

## 7. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy

1. **The credit system is a growth subsidy for urban expansion — currently being harvested by competitors.** $1.36B of credit premium is used annually to dodge MAIP assignments, ~74% of it generated in 11 urban territories where MAPFRE — despite being the #1 carrier statewide — is evidently under-indexed (that is what 1.5–1.7x pro-rata assignment intake means). Growing voluntary share in Chelsea, Dorchester/Roxbury, East Boston, Lynn, Lawrence, Lowell, Brockton, Springfield and Holyoke is effectively **double-paid**: adequately priced premium plus ~$2,900/car of quota-share offset that converts today's assigned-risk drag into avoided loss. The distribution asset to do it — urban independent agents, including the Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking agency channel — plays directly to MAPFRE's brand and agent-first model.
2. **Timing is favorable.** MAIP rates effective July 2025/July 2026 are the most adequate in years (assignments -38% in 2025), which means (a) the residual pool is shrinking back toward its ~0.5% floor, and (b) urban risks are being repriced in the voluntary market — the classic window in which take-out conversion (1.0x credit) and urban voluntary growth are least risky. Writing urban business at MAPFRE's own rates while MAIP manual rates sit high also softens the lower-of-rule exposure.
3. **The regulatory fight is unwinnable; arbitrage beats advocacy.** MAPFRE has backed credit phase-outs since at least 2018 and lost every time (2016, 2017, 2019 DOI decisions; 2025 status quo vote; 2026 deliberations again protect the DOI territories). The rational response is to switch sides economically: build the credit-generating urban book that Plymouth Rock, Arbella and Safety have monetized for a decade. Note that Rule 29's indicated factors would slash DOI-territory credits if ever adopted — so the credit-harvest window, while durable (frozen since 2015), is not guaranteed forever; the Feb 2026 committee is actively modeling transitions.
4. **Interim lever: buy credits.** Rule 29.F permits purchasing excess credits from over-credited ARCs at negotiated (non-public) prices — a bridge tactic while an urban voluntary book is built, and a data point worth acquiring in diligence (what do credits clear at?).
5. **Instrument the decision with member data.** Commerce, as a CAR member, has access to the member-only MAIP Quota Share and Premium/Loss reports [21] that would turn the §6.3 ranges into precise numbers: its credit-adjusted quota share, its current credit premium by cell, MAIP loss ratios by territory, and the marginal displacement rate per credit dollar. The public record is sufficient to size the prize at **high-single-digit millions of annual underwriting drag avoidable, plus a $1B+/yr credit-premium pool concentrated in 11 territories where the client is under-penetrated** — the member data will tell you exactly which cells to attack first (start with class 20 in territories 21/22 at 1.75x and the 1.25–1.50x cells in 16, 20, 40, 44).
