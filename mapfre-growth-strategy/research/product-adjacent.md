# Adjacent Products: Home, Umbrella, and Bundling Economics
## Research brief for MAPFRE Insurance (U.S.) growth strategy — fact base module

**Prepared:** July 2026. **Scope:** MAPFRE's home/property position in Massachusetts vs. its auto franchise; bundling economics; the MA homeowners market structure (including coastal cat and FAIR Plan dynamics); and attach opportunities in renters, umbrella, and specialty lines through the independent agent channel. All figures carry as-of dates and inline citations to the numbered Sources list.

---

## 1. MAPFRE's current home/property presence in Massachusetts vs. auto

### 1.1 Headline position: #1 in both lines, but the home share is barely half the auto share

MAPFRE (via The Commerce Insurance Company and affiliates) is the largest writer of **both** private passenger auto and homeowners insurance in Massachusetts — but with a large asymmetry between the two:

| Line (MA, 2024) | MAPFRE DWP | MAPFRE share | Rank | Market total | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Private passenger auto | $1,325.9M | 17.96% | #1 | $7.38B | NAIC data via Agency Checklists [4] |
| Homeowners (NAIC basis) | $399.1M | 10.62% | #1 | $3.76B | NAIC data via Agency Checklists [3] |
| Home insurance (MA DOI basis, all home products) | n/a (not disclosed) | 11.0% (largest voluntary share) | #1 | ~$3.6B written | MA DOI 2024 report via [1][2] |

- On the CAR/exposure basis, Commerce held **908,833 auto exposures, an 18.33% share of 4.96M total MA exposures, as of December 2025** — still the largest by a wide margin, but down roughly four percentage points since 2020 as Progressive grew from 5.1% to 12.17% [5].
- In home, the MA Division of Insurance reports MAPFRE Insurance Group (parent of Commerce) at **11.0% of 2024 voluntary-market home premium — down from 12.5% in 2023** [1][6]. The NAIC homeowners-line basis shows 10.62% ($399.1M DWP) for 2024 [3]. The two figures differ because the DOI "home insurance" report includes tenant/condo products and measures the voluntary market excluding the FAIR Plan; both are credible, and we treat the NAIC figure as the cleaner cross-carrier comparator and the DOI figure as the better trend indicator.

**Two implications.** First, home is only ~23% of MAPFRE's MA personal-lines premium ($399M of $1,725M combined auto+home, 2024) [3][4] — the client's own "~80% of revenue from MA personal auto" framing is consistent with this. Second, the share gap is the growth math: if MAPFRE held its auto share (17.96%) in the $3.76B MA homeowners market, it would write roughly **$675M of home premium — about $275M more than it does today** (analyst calculation on [3][4]). That gap is held largely by carriers distributing through the *same independent agents* MAPFRE already works with (Safety, Andover, Arbella, Plymouth Rock, Vermont Mutual).

### 1.2 The home franchise is shrinking while the market grows

MA home premium grew 12.7% in 2024 [1], yet MAPFRE's voluntary share fell from 12.5% to 11.0% [1][6] — i.e., MAPFRE grew slower than the market or shrank in a year when Safety, its closest agent-channel comparator, grew homeowners policy count 8.7% and average written premium per exposure 8.9% [14]. This is share leakage inside MAPFRE's own distribution channel, not disruption from direct writers (State Farm's MA entry notwithstanding — it is new and small).

### 1.3 Parent-level context: the U.S. turnaround creates capacity for growth

- MAPFRE North America combined ratio: **105.4% (2023) → 98.7% (2024) → 95.4% (2025)**; 2025 North America net result a record €139M (+42%); U.S. premiums ~€2.3B in 2025, roughly flat in local currency [12][13].
- MAPFRE Group 2025 net result: €1.1B (+19.6%) [12]. The Group has publicly celebrated the U.S. technical turnaround; the strategic question is whether the U.S. unit now pivots from remediation (rate, non-renewal, shrink-to-profit) to growth. The 2024–25 share declines in both MA auto and MA home look like the residue of remediation-era pricing/underwriting actions [1][5][12].
- MA homeowners was strongly profitable for MAPFRE in 2024: **30.03% direct loss ratio** on $399.1M DWP [3] — a benign-weather year (statewide direct loss ratio 31.4%, adjusted combined ratio 66.5% vs. 86.1% in 2023 [1]), but profitable headroom nonetheless.

## 2. Bundling economics: retention, loss ratio, lifetime value

### 2.1 Retention lift — the best-documented effect in personal lines

| Metric | Bundled | Monoline | Source, as-of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homeowner retention rate | 95% | 85% | J.D. Power 2022 U.S. Home Insurance Study [8] |
| Renter retention rate | 95% | 82% | J.D. Power 2022 [8] |
| Bundled vs. monoline policy retention (agency-level industry benchmark) | 91% | 67% | MarshBerry, account-rounding analysis [10] |
| Average tenure with insurer | 7.0 years | 5.5 years | J.D. Power 2025 U.S. Insurance Shopping Study [9] |

- A 10-point retention lift compounds: at 95% vs. 85% retention, expected customer lifetime is ~20 years vs. ~6.7 years — roughly a **3x difference in expected policy-years per customer** (analyst calculation on [8]). Even the more conservative J.D. Power tenure figures (7.0 vs. 5.5 years) imply ~27% more lifetime premium per relationship before any cross-line premium is added [9].
- One frequently cited secondary analysis (Forbes Advisor, via lead-gen commentary) puts bundled-customer lifetime value ~40% above single-policy holders; we could not trace the primary methodology and flag it as directionally consistent but unverified [see caveat in Sources note].
- Demand-side pull is real: **33% of auto insurance shoppers in 2025 were actively seeking to bundle auto with home** [9].

### 2.2 Loss-ratio and risk-selection effects

Public, carrier-level loss-ratio splits for bundled vs. monoline books are not disclosed; no reliable public figure exists and we say so explicitly. The observable proxies:

- MA homeowners direct loss ratios for the agent-channel bundlers in 2024 were uniformly excellent: MAPFRE 30.0%, Arbella 25.0%, Plymouth Rock 27.7%, Andover 28.8%, Safety 36.3% [3]. Homeowners written to auto customers is generally believed to select better risks (stability, verified information, multi-policy price-sensitivity rather than churn-shopping), but this is industry conventional wisdom, not a sourced statistic.
- The cautionary evidence runs the other way on *auto price competitiveness*: J.D. Power found **31% of bundlers say they will move their home policy if they switch auto insurers** after an auto rate increase [8]. For MAPFRE this cuts both ways — its home book is exposed if its auto pricing lags (Progressive's rise from 5.1% to 12.2% of MA exposures since 2020 shows the churn engine at work [5]), but a bigger bundled book would also armor the auto franchise: bundling is the single strongest defensive moat against Progressive/GEICO monoline auto poaching.

### 2.3 The economics of MAPFRE's own bundle offer

MAPFRE already prices for the bundle: its published MA "Account Discount" advertises savings of **up to ~25% on home and up to 15% on auto** when bundled (marketing pages vary between 22–29% home / 15–20% auto) [17]. Safety advertises bundle discounts up to 20% [14]. The discount economics only pay back through retention and expense efficiency — which argues for measuring and managing bundle *attach rate at point of new-business auto sale* (where the retention annuity is longest), not just in-force cross-sell.

**What we cannot source publicly:** MAPFRE's actual MA bundle penetration (share of its ~909K auto exposures with a MAPFRE home/tenant policy), bundle vs. monoline retention on its own book, and agent commission differentials. These are first-priority internal data requests.

## 3. The Massachusetts homeowners market: size, structure, cat exposure

### 3.1 Size and growth

- MA home insurance written premium: **~$3.6B in 2024**; premium paid ~$3.9B, **+12.7% vs. 2023**; 93.5% of premium is traditional homeowners [1][2]. On the NAIC homeowners-line basis, MA DWP was $3.76B — 45% of New England's $8.4B [3].
- Growth is rate-driven, not unit-driven: total policy count *fell* by 19,808 in 2024 (owners' policies +4,438; **tenant policies −23,534**) [1].
- 2024 profitability was exceptional: statewide direct loss ratio 31.4%; adjusted combined ratio 66.5% (86.1% in 2023); seven cat events totaling ~$157M in losses [1]. MA average premiums (~$1,700–$2,000/yr depending on methodology) remain well below the national average [see 1, 2 and secondary rate trackers] — this is a structurally attractive homeowners state in most years, with tail risk concentrated in coastal wind.

### 3.2 Competitive structure (2024, NAIC basis) [3]

| 1 | MAPFRE | $399.1M | 10.62% | 30.0% |
| 2 | Liberty Mutual | $302.6M | 8.06% | 27.0% |
| 3 | Safety | $264.3M | 7.04% | 36.3% |
| 4 | Andover | $245.5M | 6.54% | 28.8% |
| 5 | Chubb | $223.1M | 5.94% | 16.6% |
| 6 | Travelers | $217.5M | 5.79% | 24.9% |
| 7 | Arbella | $201.3M | 5.36% | 25.0% |

| 10 | Amica | $135.9M | 3.62% | 20.2% |

Top-10 = 63.4% of the market; top-25 = 92.7% of the non-FAIR-Plan market; ~68 licensed groups compete [1]. The market is moderately concentrated, regional-mutual-heavy, and overwhelmingly independent-agent-distributed — structurally favorable terrain for MAPFRE's channel.

### 3.3 Coastal cat exposure and the FAIR Plan — the market's fault line

- The **MA FAIR Plan (MPIUA)** wrote **8.7% of 2024 home premium** and insured **173,000+ properties in 2024, up from 158,660 in 2023 (+24,436 policies) — the first increase since 2017 and the largest single-year jump in two decades** [1][7].
- **~40% of homes on Cape Cod and the Islands** are now FAIR Plan-insured (33% in 2023); coastal Bristol/Plymouth plus Cape & Islands hold ~55% of all FAIR Plan policies [7].
- Private-market **nonrenewals more than tripled: 3,483 (2022) → 9,248 (2023) → 13,000+ (2024)**; Massachusetts has the fifth-highest home-insurance nonrenewal rate in the country [7].
- FAIR Plan coverage caps at $1M per home; it reorganized as a standalone direct insurer on April 4, 2024; a two-year moratorium on FAIR Plan rate filings (agreed as part of 2024 reforms) lifted in March 2026, and a filing for its first rate increase in ~20 years is expected — any increase requires DOI approval after public hearing [1][7]. We found no completed 2026 filing as of this writing; this is an open monitoring item.
- Wind deductibles are the voluntary market's cat lever: **58% of coastal policies carry percentage wind deductibles (up to 5% of Coverage A)** vs. 19.1% urban [1]. Flood take-up remains thin: 56,650 policyholders with flood coverage statewide (+4.3% YoY) [1].

### 3.4 Reinsurance cost environment — a tailwind, for now

Property-cat reinsurance is softening sharply: **U.S. property-cat risk-adjusted rates fell ~10–12% at the January 1, 2026 renewals**, following double-digit reductions through 2025 renewals (Florida/Southeast −15% to −25% at June 2025–2026), driven by record reinsurance capital and two benign U.S. hurricane seasons [18]. For a Northeast-wind-exposed writer, cat load per unit of home premium is cheaper in 2026 than at any time since 2022 — reducing the marginal cost of growing coastal-adjacent homeowners now. Loss-affected programs still saw increases, so this tailwind is conditional on continued benign experience [18].

## 4. Attach products: renters, umbrella, specialty (motorcycle, boat)

### 4.1 What MAPFRE already has on the shelf

MAPFRE's U.S. product set already includes renters/tenant coverage, **personal umbrella from $1M to $5M**, motorcycle, RV, and **watercraft/boat/yacht coverage available across all six New England states** — all distributed through its ~4,200 independent agents and brokers in 19 states [15][16][17]. The gap is not product availability; it is attach-rate execution and (per the client) the absence of any bindable direct/digital path.

### 4.2 Renters

- MA tenant policy count fell by 23,534 in 2024 [1] — the market is shrinking even as MA remains a majority-renter state in its urban core. Renters is a low-premium, low-severity line whose strategic value is (a) feeder pipeline to first-time-buyer homeowners, and (b) retention lift: **renters who bundle retain at 95% vs. 82% unbundled** [8].
- No public MA renters market-share table exists at the line level; MAPFRE's tenant share is not separately disclosed [gap noted]. Given MAPFRE's ~909K auto exposures skew statewide (including renter-dense Worcester/Springfield/Boston-adjacent markets) [5], a renters attach program priced as an auto endorsement-level transaction is the cheapest LTV insurance available.

### 4.3 Umbrella

- The U.S. personal umbrella market is **~$6.6B of premium (<1% of ~$1T total P&C DWP, 2024)** with household penetration of only **~10–15%** — large headroom [11].
- Underwriting caution required: Gen Re pegs the **2024 personal umbrella accident-year combined ratio near 200%, including ~55 points of adverse development**, driven by social inflation and nuclear verdicts; double- and triple-digit rate increases have been filed 2015–2025 and the line is "fundamentally underpriced" at legacy rates [11]. Average premium is only ~$400–$500 per $1M limit [11].
- Strategic read: umbrella is worth selling **as a bundle-cementing retention product, underwritten conservatively (attach only over MAPFRE auto+home with high underlying limits), at post-correction rates** — not as a standalone profit line. It also raises underlying auto liability limits (umbrella eligibility typically requires 250/500-type limits), which improves auto premium per exposure.

### 4.4 Specialty: motorcycle, boat, RV

- No public MA market-share data exists for motorcycle or watercraft at the state line level [gap noted]. The strategic logic is account-rounding: independent-agent benchmarks show **bundled-account retention of 91% vs. 67% monoline**, and agencies running structured account-rounding programs convert ~48% of rounding quotes [10].
- Massachusetts' coastal geography makes boat/watercraft a natural adjacency for the same Cape/South Shore agents wrestling with FAIR Plan-displaced home business — MAPFRE already offers boat/yacht in all six New England states [16]. Each added line deepens the agent's economic dependence on MAPFRE as its "account carrier," which is the real prize in an agent-controlled market.

## 5. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy (analytical take)

1. **Home is the single largest, lowest-risk growth lever MAPFRE has.** The 7-point share gap between its MA auto (18%) and home (10.6–11%) franchises is worth ~$275M of annual premium at parity [3][4] — in a line where MAPFRE ran a 30% loss ratio in 2024 [3], through agents it already contracts with, against regional competitors rather than national direct writers. No new state, channel, or product is required. The first move is internal: measure bundle attach on the ~909K-exposure auto book and set agency-level attach targets with compensation support.

2. **The bundle is defense as much as offense.** Progressive has taken ~7 points of MA auto exposure share since 2020 while Commerce ceded ~4 [5]. Monoline auto customers retain at 67–85%; bundled customers at 91–95% [8][10]. Every home policy attached to an auto account converts a churn-exposed monoline customer into a 7+-year relationship [9]. Conversely, MAPFRE's declining home share (12.5% → 11.0% in one year [1][6]) is actively de-armoring its auto book.

3. **The coastal dislocation is a contrarian opportunity — with guardrails.** 13,000+ nonrenewals and a FAIR Plan absorbing 40% of Cape homes [7] means thousands of agent-held customers are being dumped into a $1M-capped residual market just as (a) reinsurance costs fall double digits [18] and (b) the FAIR Plan prepares its first rate increase in 20 years [7]. A "coastal wind-managed" homeowners appetite — percentage wind deductibles (already standard on 58% of coastal policies [1]), inspection-driven, bundled-only — lets MAPFRE cherry-pick the best FAIR Plan-displaced risks while competitors retreat. This buys durable agent loyalty at exactly the moment agents are desperate for coastal markets. Sizing discipline matters: this should be a controlled tranche, not open appetite.

4. **Attach products are retention chemistry, not premium volume.** Renters (feeder + 13-point retention lift [8]), umbrella (bundle cement, sold conservatively at corrected rates given ~200% industry combined ratios [11]), and boat/motorcycle (agent account-rounding, 91% vs. 67% retention economics [10]) each individually move premium by single-digit millions but collectively determine whether MAPFRE is the *account carrier* or the *auto-only carrier* in each agency. Given the client has no bindable direct channel, agent-facing tooling — multi-line quoting, pre-filled cross-sell offers at auto new business, rounding campaigns — is the delivery mechanism.

5. **Timing is favorable and probably temporary.** MAPFRE North America has just completed its profitability turnaround (95.4% combined ratio, record 2025 result [12]); MA homeowners just printed a 66.5% adjusted combined ratio year [1]; reinsurance is softening [18]. Soft-cat, cheap-reinsurance windows are when disciplined property growth is cheapest — and when competitors (Safety +20% DWP in 2024 [14]) are already moving. Waiting a cycle likely means growing home in a post-event hard market instead.

**Priority internal data requests (not publicly available):** MAPFRE MA bundle penetration and attach rate at auto new business; retention and loss ratio splits bundled vs. monoline on own book; home quote-to-bind funnel by agency; renters/umbrella/specialty in-force counts; coastal aggregate and cat-model PML by county; agent compensation structure for multi-line accounts.
