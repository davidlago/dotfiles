# Massachusetts Small Commercial: Current Market Tables, MAPFRE's Commercial Book, and CAR Servicing Status

**Research brief for MAPFRE Insurance growth strategy engagement**
**Prepared: July 2, 2026. All figures carry their as-of dates; currency is USD unless noted.**

---

## 1. Executive summary

- The Massachusetts commercial multiple peril (CMP) market was **$1.958 billion of direct written premium (DWP) in 2024** — the 9th-largest CMP state in the country, 2.93% of the U.S. total — per the NAIC's 2025 state fact sheet. This replaces the previously inferred "$1.5–2.0B" estimate with a hard number at the top of that range [1].
- Massachusetts workers' compensation was **$1.338 billion DWP in 2024** (12th-largest WC state, 2.19% of U.S.) [1]. The market is fragmented: the 2023 leader, A.I.M. Mutual, held only **11.07%** [6]. The rate environment is deflation-then-freeze: WCRIBMA requested an 8.3% average rate *decrease* in early 2024 [7] and its subsequent request for a 7.1% *increase* was denied by the Commissioner in May 2025, holding rates flat [6].
- Massachusetts commercial auto reached **$1.330 billion DWP at year-end 2024** (70 active insurers). Commerce (MAPFRE) and Safety are effectively tied at the top with ~12.9% each [2]; through August 2025 Safety led with 13.10% vs. Commerce 12.15% [3], and in early 2026 Commerce regained the #1 slot (13.15% as of February 2026 YTD) [5].
- **The comparison of August 2024 vs. August 2025 CAR data shows Commerce's MA commercial auto premium *shrank* ~4.5% (from $118.4M to $113.0M YTD) while the market grew ~3% and Arbella grew ~14%** [3][4]. MAPFRE is currently losing share in the one commercial line where it is a leader.
- **Commerce is one of four CAR-appointed commercial servicing carriers** (with Arbella Protection, Pilgrim, and Safety), per CAR's current servicing-carrier list (accessed July 2, 2026) [8]. Servicing-carrier terms run five years; the current appointment cycle runs January 1, 2022 – December 31, 2026, so a re-appointment RFP cycle is imminent [11][9].
- The CAR commercial ceded pool is growing: **FY2025 projected pool written premium of $260.0M, up from $237.9M actual in FY2024** — roughly 18–19% of the state's commercial auto market — with FY2024 underwriting deductions at 108.0% of earned premium (a pooled deficit shared by all members in proportion to voluntary commercial auto writings), improving to a projected 96.6% (an ~$8.4M underwriting gain) in FY2025 [9].
- Competitor calibration: **Safety wrote ~$194M of commercial auto (15.2% of DWP) and ~$32M of BOP (2.5%) in FY2025**, updating the client's FY2024 proof point ($181.7M / $31.1M), on a 99.0% combined ratio [12][13]. **Hanover's Core Commercial ran a 97.4% combined ratio in FY2025 with "prudently increased loss selections in commercial auto liability and workers' compensation"** — a live illustration of commercial auto loss-trend risk [14].

## 4. MAPFRE/Commerce's commercial book: what public data shows

- **Commercial auto (MA):** ~$172.0M DWP in 2024 (12.93% share, #2) [2]; ~$113.0M through August 2025 (12.15%, #2, shrinking) [3]; #1 at 13.15% on February 2026 YTD data [5]. This is by far MAPFRE's largest U.S. commercial line and it is essentially all Massachusetts.
- **Product shelf:** MAPFRE markets BOP, commercial package, commercial auto, and umbrella in Massachusetts through independent agents [17]. MAPFRE marketing claims to be "the largest commercial auto insurer in Massachusetts" (true on some monthly CAR snapshots) [17].
- **By-line CMP/BOP/umbrella premium:** **not published in free public sources.** Commerce's statutory by-line detail (commercial auto liability, auto physical damage, CMP, other liability) is in its NAIC annual statement, accessible via iSite+/S&P — flagged as a data-room/procurement item. Given Safety's BOP book is only ~$32M [12][13] and MAPFRE's BOP presence is widely regarded as smaller, MAPFRE's MA CMP/BOP book is plausibly in the low tens of millions, i.e., ~1% share of a $1.96B market — but **this is an inference, not a sourced figure**.
- **Group context:** MAPFRE North America premiums were **>€2.6B in FY2025** (−4.6% in euro terms, ~flat in local currency), with a **record net result of €139M (+41.8%)** and a Non-Life combined ratio of **95.4% (−3.3 pts)**; the U.S. alone contributed **~€2.3B premium and €118.2M result** [16]. Auto ran 97.8% and General P&C 78.9% for the region [16]. Separately, MAPFRE Group ranked as the **21st-largest U.S. P&C group in 2023 with $1.40B DPW and dropped out of the NAIC top-25 in 2024** [15] — the U.S. book is flat-to-shrinking while profitability recovers.
- **Growth/loss ratios by commercial line 2022–2025:** not publicly disaggregated; the FY2025 regional Auto (97.8%) and General P&C (78.9%) combined ratios [16] are the closest public proxies.

## 5. CAR commercial servicing status and residual-market burden

**Is Commerce a CAR-appointed commercial servicing carrier? Yes.** CAR's current "Commercial Servicing Carriers" list (commauto.com, accessed July 2, 2026) names four: **Arbella Protection Insurance Company (No. 154), The Commerce Insurance Company (No. 279), Pilgrim Insurance Company (No. 732), and Safety Insurance Company (No. 773)** [8].

Mechanics and burden:
- CAR runs the **commercial auto residual market as a reinsurance pool** (unlike private passenger, which since 2008 is the assigned-risk MAIP). Servicing Carriers are contracted for **five-year terms**, books are reviewed annually for equitable distribution, and **all members share pooled results in proportion to voluntary commercial auto written premium** [9]. The current appointment term is **January 1, 2022 – December 31, 2026** [11], so the next RFP/appointment cycle is due now-ish (CAR ran the prior RFP process starting November 2020 [11]; the 2017 RFP contemplated distributing the ceded book among three to five carriers [10]).
- **Pool size:** FY2024 actual pool written premium **$237.9M**; FY2025 projected **$260.0M** [9]. Against a ~$1.33B (2024) commercial auto market [2], roughly **18–19% of MA commercial auto premium is ceded to the residual pool** — a strikingly high residual share by national standards. For context, the pool was ~$200M in 2020 [11] and ~$143M in the mid-2010s [10]; CAR's 2025 Annual Report notes 2024 ceded volume rose ~13% over 2023, driven by TTT (trucks/tractors/trailers) and bus classes, with zone-rated bus loss ratios still hit by large losses [9].
- **Pool economics:** FY2024 pool earned premium $217.6M with underwriting deductions at **108.0%** of earned (loss & LAE 81.7%, expense allowances 24.1%) — an underwriting deficit of roughly $17M shared across members. FY2025 is projected at **96.6%** deductions, an **~$8.4M net underwriting gain**, thanks to favorable re-estimates of policy years 2019–2024, partially offset by an initial PY2025 deficit projection [9].
- **What this means for Commerce specifically:** (i) as a ~13% voluntary commercial auto writer it absorbs roughly its market share of any pool deficit (order of $2M in a year like FY2024, a tailwind in surplus years); (ii) as a Servicing Carrier it earns ceding expense allowances (24%+ of ceded earned premium is paid to servicing carriers in aggregate [9]) and controls agent relationships for ceded business — a structural moat in the hard-to-place segment (taxi/limo/car service, ~$5.7M written via 129 exclusive representative producers as of 2020 [11]); and (iii) MAPFRE holds seats on CAR's Governing Committee and Commercial Automobile Committee (Sarah Clemens; Allison Ratliff/Kylee Morfitt attended the February 19, 2026 committee meeting) [9][25].

## 6. Competitor calibration: Safety, Arbella, Hanover

### Safety Insurance Group (the direct proof point)
- FY2025 DWP **$1,278.6M, +7.2%** vs. $1,193.1M in 2024; combined ratio **99.0% vs. 101.1%**; net income $99.3M [12].
- FY2025 mix: private passenger auto 54.9%, homeowners 25.2%, **commercial auto 15.2% (≈$194.3M)**, **BOP 2.5% (≈$32.0M)**; 80,154 commercial auto exposures and 7,918 BOP policies; Massachusetts was $1,204.8M of the $1,278.6M (94%), with NH $57.7M and ME $16.1M [13]. This updates the client's FY2024 calibration ($181.7M commercial auto; $31.1M BOP): **commercial auto grew ~7% and BOP ~3% in 2025**.
- Growth was rate-driven: average written premium per policy rose **+4.6% in commercial auto** (vs. +8.1% PPA, +9.7% homeowners) in 2025 [12]. Note Safety's 10-K commercial auto figure ($194M, three states) vs. the CAR MA-only figure ($172.1M in 2024 [2]) — the delta is NH/ME plus line-definition differences.
- Strategic read: Safety proves an MA independent-agent personal-lines carrier can build a ~$225M commercial book (commercial auto + BOP) at a ~99% combined ratio — but also that BOP remains small ($32M) even for the best-positioned local comparator, i.e., small-commercial property/liability is a slow build.

### Arbella
- MA commercial auto: $136.0M / 10.23% at YE2024 [2], growing **+14% YoY** on August 2025 YTD data ($108.4M, 11.65%) [3][4] and reaching 12.53% share by February 2026 YTD [5] — the fastest-growing large player, and a CAR commercial servicing carrier [8].
- Group writes **>$1B annual premium** across MA/CT/RI/NH (company profile; no public by-line breakdown located) [26]. Line-level mix requires statutory pulls — flagged as an open item. Arbella chairs CAR's Governing Committee (William Hughes) [9].

### Hanover
- MA commercial auto is small for Hanover: $31.9M / 2.40% (YE2024) [2], $23.2M through August 2025 (2.49%) [3] — Hanover's small-commercial strength is BOP/package distributed nationally, not MA commercial auto.
- FY2025: Core Commercial NPW **$2,273.7M, +3.6%**; combined ratio **97.4%** (92.3% ex-cat); total-company combined ratio 91.6%. Crucially, the current-accident-year loss ratio rose 1.3 pts, "primarily driven by prudently increased loss selections in **commercial auto liability** and in **workers' compensation**, partially offset by lower losses in commercial multiple peril" [14]. Q1 2026 Core Commercial improved (96.6% combined; NPW +4.3% to $630.4M) [27].
- Strategic read: even a top-tier small-commercial franchise is strengthening commercial auto liability picks — corroborating industry-wide commercial auto liability severity trend. Any MAPFRE small-commercial growth plan must underwrite to that trend, not to its personal-auto loss experience.

## 7. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy

1. **The adjacency is real and newly quantified.** Within MAPFRE's home state and existing agent plant, the addressable small-commercial pools are: commercial auto $1.33B (2024), CMP/BOP $1.96B (2024), WC $1.34B (2024) — ~$4.6B across the three lines [1][2]. MAPFRE already holds a top-2, ~13% position in exactly one of them.
2. **Fix the leak before funding growth.** The most actionable finding is that Commerce's commercial auto book shrank ~4.5% YoY into August 2025 while Safety (+8%), Arbella (+14%), Travelers (+9%) and Hanover (+10%) all grew [3][4]. Before any BOP/WC expansion narrative, the engagement should diagnose whether this is deliberate underwriting discipline (plausible, given MAPFRE's group-level combined-ratio repair to 95.4% [16]) or share loss to Arbella's agent push. Losing the commercial-auto anchor would undercut the whole small-commercial cross-sell thesis, because commercial auto is the door-opener product for MA main-street accounts.
3. **CMP/BOP is the white space; WC is not.** CMP is a $2.0B, growing, top-9-nationally market where MAPFRE's share is negligible and the natural cross-sell (commercial auto + BOP through the same independent agents) is proven by Safety's model — though Safety's $32M BOP book after two decades shows organic build is slow; an MGA partnership, renewal-rights deal, or small team lift-out would accelerate it [1][12][13]. WC, by contrast, is a $1.34B line with rates frozen by the Commissioner, an 11%-max-share fragmented field, and adverse trend flagged even by Hanover — better accessed via a fronting/partner market for agents than via MAPFRE's own paper [1][6][14].
4. **The CAR servicing franchise is a strategic asset with a 2026 deadline.** Commerce's status as one of only four commercial servicing carriers [8] confers expense-allowance income, agent stickiness in hard-to-place classes, and information advantage in a residual market that is an unusually large ~18–19% of the state's commercial auto premium and growing (TTT/bus driven) [9]. The current five-year term ends December 31, 2026 [11]; re-winning the appointment (and pricing the servicing proposal well) should be treated as a board-level item inside the growth strategy, as should the depopulation opportunity — CAR explicitly asks servicing carriers for depopulation strategies [11], and a carrier with appetite could voluntarily write the better ceded risks at adequate rates.
5. **Residual-market exposure cuts both ways.** Growing voluntary commercial auto share mechanically raises MAPFRE's participation in pool results [9]. In deficit years (FY2024: 108% deductions) that is a ~13%-share tax; in the projected FY2025 surplus it is a small credit. The plan's commercial-auto growth targets should model pool participation explicitly rather than treat MA commercial auto margins as purely voluntary-book economics.
6. **Data gaps to close with paid sources / client data:** Commerce's statutory by-line commercial premium and loss ratios 2022–2025 (NAIC iSite+/S&P); current MA CMP and WC carrier-level share tables (BestLink/S&P); Commerce's ceded-vs-voluntary commercial auto split and servicing-carrier expense-allowance economics (client internal + CAR settlement data); Arbella's by-line book (statutory).
