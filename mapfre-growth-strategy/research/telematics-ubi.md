# Telematics and Usage-Based Insurance (UBI): Research Brief for MAPFRE USA Growth Strategy

**Prepared:** July 2026 (all figures carry as-of dates; note that some 2026-dated trade-press sources reflect late-2025/early-2026 data)
**Scope:** National UBI adoption and leading programs; Massachusetts-specific telematics landscape and regulation; evidence on loss-ratio, retention and segmentation impact; build-vs-partner economics for a mid-size regional carrier.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- Telematics has crossed from pilot to mainstream: roughly **21 million U.S. policyholders shared telematics data with their insurer in 2024**, a **28% CAGR since 2018** (IoT Insurance Observatory, reported February 2026) [1]. Yet penetration of the total insured base remains modest — one 2025 consumer survey found only ~12% of respondents currently use a UBI program [2] — so the growth runway is long.
- The leaders have made telematics a core pricing weapon, not a gadget. Progressive calls UBI its **"most predictive rating variable"** — more than 3x more predictive than the next-best variable — and observed **renewal rates 6% higher** among customers earning the largest Snapshot discounts and **16% lower** among surcharged drivers (as of March 2023) [3]. Allstate reported telematics **new-business penetration of 30–40%** in states where its programs are offered, and **over 50% of online new business**, with 2.5 million telematics policies at end-2021 [4].
- Massachusetts is unusually telematics-friendly on the supply side — **Cambridge Mobile Telematics (CMT), the world's largest mobile-telematics provider, is headquartered in Cambridge, MA** and serves nearly all of the top 25 U.S. auto insurers [5][6] — but constrained on the regulatory side: MA's managed-competition regime (since April 2008) bans credit, age, sex, occupation, education and homeownership as rating factors and requires Division of Insurance approval of all rating plans [7][8]. That makes telematics one of the few *permitted* new segmentation variables in the state — a strategic point in MAPFRE's favor.
- **MAPFRE already has a program**: MotionSmart, launched in Massachusetts in July 2021 in partnership with CMT, originally offering up to 10% at enrollment and up to 20% at renewal [9][10]. As of early 2026, MAPFRE's website advertises only **"up to 2%"** on a new policy, with renewal rating based on driving data and possible increases for the riskiest drivers [11]. Public sources give no enrollment figures — a key data gap. Meanwhile Progressive — the #2 carrier in MA and the one taking MAPFRE's share — runs the most sophisticated telematics machine in the market.
- The build-vs-partner question is largely settled industry-wide: virtually every mid-size carrier partners. The realistic strategic question for MAPFRE is not "launch telematics" but **"scale MotionSmart from a marketing discount into a pricing, retention and claims asset"** — including at-quote telematics scores (Arity IQ-style), continuous monitoring, and agent-channel enrollment targets.

Two consistent findings across surveys: (a) consumer willingness far exceeds actual enrollment — awareness and point-of-sale offering are the bottleneck, which matters for an agency-distributed carrier; (b) adoption skews young, which is a customer-acquisition lever, not just a pricing one [1][13].

**Progressive's timeline is the benchmark for how long organic capability-building takes**: Autograph (1996) → TripSense (2004) → MyRate (2008) → Snapshot (2010) → surcharge capability (2014) → mobile app (2016) → continuous monitoring (2022) [3]. No mid-size carrier can replicate that data asset organically; partnering is the only realistic path to comparable segmentation (Section 5).

Notable: at launch, MotionSmart offered up to 10% at enrollment and up to 20% at renewal [9][10]; the current MAPFRE site advertises only up to 2% at enrollment [11]. We could not find a public explanation for the reduction (plausibly a rate-filing recalibration); this is a question for the client team. No MA carrier publishes telematics enrollment or penetration figures — another data gap.

### 3.3 How Massachusetts regulates telematics

- **Managed competition since April 2008**: carriers set their own rates, but every rating plan must be filed with and approved by the Division of Insurance (DOI) [7][8]. Per the NAIC, state regulators must approve UBI rating plans and require statistical support for telematics variables [30].
- **Prohibited rating factors**: sex, marital status, race, creed, national origin, religion, age (except a 65+ discount), occupation, income, education, homeownership, and credit information [8]. Massachusetts is one of the most restrictive rating environments in the country.
- **Strategic implication**: because so many conventional segmentation variables are banned, telematetics is one of the few DOI-permissible ways to add pricing sophistication in MA — evidenced by the fact that the DOI has already approved telematics-based programs for MAPFRE, Progressive, GEICO, Safety, Plymouth Rock and Arbella [9][15][23][27][29]. CMT states that "several regulators have approved the use of its models for rating" [28]. MAPFRE's own MotionSmart terms — renewal premiums that "may increase for the riskiest drivers" [11] — indicate the DOI accepts telematics-informed renewal rating, not merely flat discounts, though the precise filed limits are not public. **We could not locate a Massachusetts statute or regulation specifically governing telematics**; programs are governed through the ordinary Ch. 175E/managed-competition rate-filing process [7][8]. Confirming the DOI's current posture on surcharge magnitude and at-quote telematics scores is a priority follow-up (via SERFF filings).

## 4. Evidence on telematics impact

### 4.1 Segmentation / pricing power

- Progressive (the largest evidence base): UBI is its **single most predictive rating variable, >3x the predictive power of the second-best variable** (company presentation, March 2023) [3]. Its surcharge capability (since 2014) is what converted telematics from a marketing tool to a profit engine [3].
- Arity claims carriers using its DriveSight score can **improve loss ratio by more than 50% on the highest-risk drivers** and estimates telematics could unlock **$39B of pricing sophistication** industry-wide (~$29B in surcharges to risky drivers, ~$10B in discounts to safe ones) [16][31]. Vendor claims — treat as directional, but consistent with Progressive's disclosed experience.
- Root reports its updated UBI model is **~10% more predictive than its predecessor** and among the most significant variables in its pricing engine (Q3 2025 shareholder letter) [20].
- Academic actuarial literature (ASTIN Bulletin, British Actuarial Journal, 2019–2024) consistently finds telematics variables materially improve claim-frequency prediction over traditional factors [32].

### 4.2 Loss-ratio and claims impact (behavior change, not just selection)

- A CMT study (January 2024) analyzing telematics data against 1,500 claims found the most engaged risky drivers improved **distracted driving 20%, hard braking 9%, and speeding 27%** over three months, translating to an estimated **5.5% reduction in bodily-injury claims** among that group [33].
- CMT data cited at MotionSmart's launch: active app users cut **phone distraction 39%, speeding 30%, and hard braking 51% within 30 days** [9].
- Consultant/trade estimates put achievable portfolio-level impact at ~**3 points of loss ratio within months** from behavioral programs, up to ~**7 points of combined ratio** for a fully deployed telematics pricing-and-engagement stack [34]. These are practitioner estimates, not audited results — the hard, disclosed evidence (Progressive's renewal-rate spread and sustained sub-90 combined ratios; Root's ~96–98 combined ratio as a telematics-native carrier) is more credible but less precisely attributable [3][14][20].
- NAIC cites studies that UBI feedback reduces crash risk substantially (its overview cites ~50% crash-risk reduction in monitored populations — an upper-bound figure from early studies; treat cautiously) [30].

### 4.3 Retention

- Progressive: Snapshot customers earning the largest discounts renew at rates **6 points higher**; surcharged drivers renew **16 points lower** — i.e., telematics simultaneously retains the profitable and sheds the unprofitable [3].
- Industry backdrop: U.S. auto retention fell from **83% to 80%** during the 2023–24 hard market, with 41% of households shopping in 2023 (LexisNexis) [12][35] — retention tools have rarely been more valuable.
- Arity claims improved retention on lower-risk drivers for carriers using telematics scores [16].
- Engagement halo: 82% of policyholders say they would recommend a telematics app offering rewards, feedback and crash assistance [1]; >50% would even pay ~$4.99/month for a connected dash-cam safety service [1].

### 4.4 Honest caveats

- Selection vs. causation is hard to separate: part of the "telematics benefit" is favorable self-selection of safe drivers into programs.
- Discount-led programs without surcharge capability (the typical MA design, including MotionSmart's current 2% enrollment discount) give away premium on good risks without repricing bad ones; Progressive's disclosed economics only turned decisively positive after surcharges arrived in 2014 [3].
- Privacy resistance is real: a large share of drivers still decline tracking (73% not using UBI in one 2025 survey [2]), and NAIC flags privacy as the leading regulatory concern [30].

## 5. What it would take for a mid-size regional carrier: build vs. partner

### 5.1 Why "build" is effectively off the table

- The moat is data-scale: Progressive needed ~15 years and billions of miles to make UBI its top rating variable [3]; Arity has >2 trillion miles tied to claims [17]; CMT processes 75+ petabytes across 55M+ drivers [6]. NAIC explicitly notes proprietary telematics data creates a barrier for new entrants [30].
- Industry practice has converged on partnering: CMT alone serves **nearly all of the top 25 U.S. auto insurers** (21 of 25 at the 2021 TrueMotion merger; "nearly all 25" per March 2026 reporting) [5][6]. Even Progressive uses CMT for crash detection/accident response [36].
- Full in-house platform builds in P&C run **$5M–$50M over 18–36 months** (general platform benchmarks, 2026) [37] with no access to a claims-linked scoring dataset at the end. Vendor mobile-telematics deals are typically per-enrolled-policy subscription pricing (specific per-policy rates are not public; commercial fleet telematics runs ~$15–$70/vehicle/month, a poor but directional proxy) [38].

### 5.2 The realistic partner set

| Partner | Offer | Relevance to MAPFRE |
|---|---|---|
| **CMT (Cambridge, MA)** | DriveWell Fusion platform: app/SDK, scoring, crash detection, claims triage, engagement. Founded ~2010 (MIT roots); $500M from SoftBank (2018); acquired TrueMotion (2021); **$350M from TPG Rise/Allianz X/State Farm at >$1B valuation, March 2026** [5][6][39] | **Already MAPFRE's vendor** (MotionSmart) and literally headquartered in MAPFRE's home market. Deepening, not switching, is the obvious move. Also powers Plymouth Rock and Arbella [28] — differentiation must come from program design, not the sensor stack. |
| **Arity (Allstate subsidiary)** | Arity IQ: **at-quote driving scores** from 40M+ drivers / 2T+ miles without requiring app installation; Experian distribution partnership [17][31] | Fills the gap CMT app programs don't: pricing *new business* at point of quote. Caveat: data flows to an Allstate affiliate; evaluate competitive and privacy optics. |
| **TransUnion + CMT** | Portable, consumer-consented driving scores usable across carriers (2025 partnership) [40] | Emerging "portable score" ecosystem to watch — could commoditize telematics segmentation over time. |
| Connected-car / OEM data (Toyota-Root model [20]; Nationwide connected-car SmartRide [19]) | Embedded vehicle data with driver consent | Longer-term; post-2024 privacy backlash over OEM data sales (GM/LexisNexis) makes consent hygiene critical. |

### 5.3 What "launch" (or relaunch) actually requires

For a carrier of MAPFRE USA's size (~908k MA auto exposures [24]), the checklist is:
1. **Actuarial/rating**: file telematics rating variables (not just a flat discount) with the MA DOI, with statistical support [30]; decide surcharge posture within what DOI will allow.
2. **Vendor contract**: per-enrolled-policy SaaS with CMT (existing) plus optionally Arity IQ/TransUnion for at-quote scores.
3. **Agent enablement**: the entire distribution is independent agents; Allstate's data shows telematics take-rates are far higher when embedded in the sales flow (>50% online vs 30–40% agent/mixed) [4] — MAPFRE needs point-of-sale defaults, agent incentives, and simple scripts. Nationwide publishes agent-facing telematics sales playbooks for exactly this reason [41].
4. **Engagement operations**: coaching content, rewards, crash response (CMT offers turnkey modules [36]).
5. **Economics**: at a plausible all-in vendor + operations cost of low tens of dollars per enrolled policy per year against MA average premiums well above $1,000, a 1–3 point loss-ratio improvement on enrolled business clears the hurdle comfortably — but this is our inference, not a sourced figure; no public per-policy pricing exists for CMT/Arity contracts [38].

## 6. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy (analytical take)

1. **Frame it as defense against Progressive first, growth second.** MAPFRE/Commerce has lost ~3.8 points of MA share since 2020 while Progressive gained ~7.1 points [24]. Progressive's advantage is compounding: its telematics data improves pricing, which wins the best risks, which improves results, which funds growth. Every year MAPFRE runs MotionSmart as a token 2% discount, the segmentation gap widens.
2. **MAPFRE's telematics problem is program design and distribution, not technology.** It already has the best-in-class vendor (CMT) in its backyard. But "up to 2% at enrollment" [11] is below every visible competitor benchmark (Safety 5% flat [29]; Nationwide 10% participation + avg 21% earned [18]; Progressive up to 15% participation [3]). Recommended moves: restore a competitive participation discount, push renewal differentiation toward the filed maximum, and set explicit agent-channel enrollment targets (Allstate's 30–40% new-business penetration is the proven benchmark for offered states [4]).
3. **Exploit the MA regulatory asymmetry.** With credit, age, occupation, education and homeownership banned as rating factors [8], telematics is one of the few approvable sophistication levers in MAPFRE's core state. A carrier with 18% share and dominant agent relationships that *also* prices on driving behavior would be hard to displace. Conversely, this same asymmetry is why Progressive is deploying its national telematics machine in MA aggressively.
4. **Add at-quote telematics to fix the new-business funnel.** App-based programs only reprice at renewal. Arity IQ / TransUnion-CMT portable scores allow pricing driving behavior at quote [17][31][40] — particularly valuable given MAPFRE's weak direct channel: the independent agent can present a telematics-informed quote with no app friction.
5. **Use telematics as the retention and engagement layer MAPFRE currently lacks.** With industry retention down to ~80% [12] and MAPFRE lacking a direct digital relationship with customers, a CMT-powered app (safety coaching, crash response, family features) is the cheapest way to build daily digital engagement inside an agent-distributed model — Plymouth Rock and Arbella are already doing this with the same vendor [27][28].
6. **Sequence**: (a) 0–6 months: diagnose MotionSmart enrollment/economics, refile discount structure, secure DOI posture on renewal differentiation; (b) 6–18 months: agent-channel enrollment push with targets and incentives, add crash-response and engagement modules; (c) 18–36 months: at-quote scores, telematics-informed claims triage, extend to other MAPFRE states and eventually leverage MAPFRE S.A.'s global telematics experience.

**Bottom line:** the evidence (Progressive's disclosed renewal-rate spread and predictive-power data, Root's telematics-native combined ratios, CMT/Arity behavioral studies) supports telematics as one of the highest-confidence levers available to MAPFRE — and it is the rare lever where MAPFRE's home-state regulation, incumbent vendor relationship, and agent network all align. The gap is executional ambition, not capability access.
