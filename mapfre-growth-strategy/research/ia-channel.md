# The Independent Agent Channel: Economics and What Makes Carriers Win It

**Research brief for MAPFRE Insurance (U.S.) growth strategy engagement**
Prepared July 2, 2026. All figures carry as-of dates; sources are numbered and listed in full at the end.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- The independent agency (IA) channel placed **62% of all U.S. P&C premium in 2025** (61.5% in 2024), but only **39.5% of personal lines** — and personal auto is the IA channel's *weakest* line at roughly **32% penetration** (2024) [1][2].
- **Massachusetts is the most IA-penetrated state in America: 78.8% of P&C premium flowed through independent agencies in 2024** — the structural reason MAPFRE/Commerce has been able to build ~18–19% auto share on an agency-only model [3].
- But the moat is eroding at both ends: **Commerce's MA private passenger auto share has fallen from 22.1% (2020) to 18.33% (Dec 2025)** while Progressive has grown from 5.1% to 12.17% over the same period — a 250% share gain — and Progressive+GEICO together now hold 23.67% [4].
- Agency distribution itself is consolidating: **695 U.S. agency M&A deals in 2025 (down 12% from 787 in 2024), with PE-backed/hybrid buyers doing ~73% of them**; Massachusetts saw **31 agency transactions in 2025** with repeat local and national acquirers (Kaplansky, Duffy, DiVirgilio, Trucordia, Patriot Growth, World, Hilb) [5][6][7].
- What agents say they want is remarkably consistent across Big "I", J.D. Power, IVANS and Deep Customer Connections research: **ease of doing business, easy quoting, real-time appetite/connectivity, claims communication, underwriting flexibility, and carriers that stay committed to the market**. Only **40% of personal lines agents describe their carriers as "easy to work with"** (2025) — a large open lane for a carrier willing to win on service [8][9][10][11].
- Compensation remains the second lever: personal lines base commissions typically run **8–15%** with profit-sharing/contingents adding **2–5%+ of premium** for profitable books; in Massachusetts, IA-only competitor Safety Insurance runs an explicit tiered loyalty architecture (Chairman's Elite through Preferred Agent's Club, plus a loss-ratio-based incentive commission) on top of base commission [12][13][14].

## 2. How much personal lines premium flows through independent agents — and where is it going?

### 2.1 National picture

| Metric (Big "I" Market Share Reports, based on AM Best data) | 2020/21 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IA share of ALL P&C premium | ~61% (5-yr avg 61.3%) | 62.2% | 61.5% | 62.0% |
| IA share of commercial lines | — | 87.3% | 87.2% | 87.7% |
| IA share of personal lines | 35.7% (2020) / 36.7% (2021) | 38.7% | 39.0% | 39.5% |
| IA share of personal auto | — | — | ~32% (lowest of any line) | n/a |

Sources: [1][2][3][15]. Total U.S. direct written premium was $1.05 trillion in 2024, up 9.6% from $952 billion in 2023 [1].

Two things are true simultaneously and both matter for MAPFRE:

1. **The IA channel is gaining share in personal lines** — up roughly 3–4 points since 2020 (35.7% → 39.5% in 2025). Drivers cited by the Big "I": the hard market pushed consumers toward choice and advice, direct writers pulled back on advertising and appetite in 2022–23, and captive agents converted to independence (8% of agencies in the 2024 Agency Universe Study had converted from captive within two years) [1][2][16].
2. **Personal auto is still the IA channel's weakest line (~32%)**, because Progressive, GEICO, State Farm and USAA dominate national auto distribution. The IA gains are fragile: they coincided with a hard market that is now softening (industry combined ratio improved to ~88 in 2025 from 92 in 2024), which historically re-energizes direct-writer marketing spend [1][2].

### 2.2 Massachusetts: the most agency-centric state in the country — but tilting

- **Massachusetts had the highest IA penetration of any state: 78.8% of P&C premium in 2024** (Big "I" 2025 Market Share Report) [3]. This is the legacy of decades of fix-and-establish auto rate regulation (pre-2008), which kept national direct writers out and made MA a de facto IA monopoly until "managed competition" took effect April 1, 2008, after which GEICO, Progressive and Allstate (re)entered [17].
- The MA private passenger auto market (CAR data, ~4.96 million exposures) as of **December 2025** [4]:

| Rank | Carrier | Exposures | Share (Dec 2025) | Channel |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Commerce (MAPFRE) | 908,833 | 18.33% | Independent agents |
| 2 | Progressive | 603,279 | 12.17% | Agents + direct |
| 3 | GEICO | 569,979 | 11.50% | Direct |
| 4 | Safety | 460,999 | 9.30% | Independent agents |
| 5 | Plymouth Rock | 390,838 | 7.88% | Agents + direct |
| 6 | Arbella | 351,278 | 7.08% | Independent agents |
| 7 | Travelers | 307,092 | 6.19% | Independent agents |
| 8 | Liberty Mutual | 247,128 | 4.98% | Direct + agents |
| 9 | USAA | 212,813 | 4.29% | Direct |
| 10 | Amica | 149,607 | 3.02% | Direct |

- **Trend:** Commerce fell from **22.1% (2020) to 18.33% (Dec 2025)**; Progressive rose from **5.1% to 12.17%** over the same window, overtaking GEICO in May 2025; Progressive+GEICO now hold **23.67%** combined [4][18]. Agency Checklists' commentary (Nov 2025) attributes Commerce's still-leading position to "deep agency distribution," while noting Progressive's momentum comes through *both* its agency network and digital platforms [18].
- I could not find a public figure for the IA share of *MA personal auto specifically* (as opposed to all-lines 78.8%). A defensible triangulation from the CAR table: carriers that are exclusively or predominantly agency-distributed (Commerce, Safety, Plymouth Rock, Arbella, Travelers, Norfolk & Dedham, Quincy Mutual, Hanover, plus Progressive's agency book) plausibly account for roughly 55–65% of MA auto exposures — still far above the ~32% national figure, but visibly declining as GEICO/Progressive-direct/USAA/Amica grow. This is an estimate, not a sourced number.

**So-what:** MAPFRE's home market is the best IA market in America *and* the one where direct writers are taking share fastest off a low base. The channel is an asset with a slowly shrinking perimeter — worth defending aggressively, not abandoning.

## 3. Agency consolidation and PE roll-ups

### 3.1 National M&A

- **695 announced agency M&A deals in 2025, down 12% from 787 in 2024**; Q4 2025 (157 deals) was the slowest quarter since 2019, ~47% below the 5-year average. OPTIS expects a "new normal" of roughly 750–800 deals/year and "more large deals and recapitalizations in 2026 as the chase for scale continues" [5][19].
- **PE-backed/hybrid buyers did ~73% of all 2025 acquisitions**; the buyer universe now includes family offices, pension funds and sovereign wealth funds. Top buyers 2025: BroadStreet (69 per OPTIS' initial tally; 29 per the IJ recap — the two published counts conflict, and I would trust the OPTIS-attributed 69 pending the full report), Hub International (49), Inszone (45) [5][19][20].
- Mega-deals are consolidating the top of the distribution pyramid: Gallagher–AssuredPartners and Gallagher–Woodruff Sawyer, Brown & Brown–Risk Strategies/One80, Baldwin–CAC (all 2025) [5].
- P&C agencies made up 66% of 2025 targets (455 deals) [5].

### 3.2 Massachusetts specifically

- **31 MA agency transactions in 2025 (vs. 30 in 2024, 37 in 2023)** — steady consolidation [6].
- Active buyers of MA agencies (2024–2025): **Kaplansky Insurance** ("continued aggressive expansion"), **Duffy Insurance** (multiple deals incl. Waverley Insurance Agency), **DiVirgilio Insurance** (7th acquisition, Ebba J. Holland Agency), **Burgin Platner**, **Trucordia**, **King Risk Partners**, **Patriot Growth** (Morse Insurance), **World Insurance Associates** (Wm F. Borhek, B.R. Alexander, Thomas J. Woods of Worcester), **Hilb Group** (New England P&C, largely personal lines) [6][7][21]. Agency Checklists characterizes the market as "reshaped by repeat acquirers, platform expansion across New England, and sustained demand for established independent agencies with durable books of business" [6].
- Nationally, agency count is shrinking and average size growing: the 2024 Agency Universe Study counts **~39,000 independent agencies (down from 40,000 in 2022)**, with 45% of "jumbo" agencies involved in an acquisition in the past year [16].

### 3.3 What consolidation means for carriers

Implications consistently flagged in trade coverage and directly inferable from the data above:

- **Fewer, larger, more professional counterparties.** A carrier's book concentrates into platforms (Hub, World, Patriot, Trucordia, Hilb, plus large regionals like Kaplansky/Duffy) that negotiate compensation centrally, demand API/download connectivity, and can move books wholesale between carriers ("book rolls" are an explicit IVANS survey priority — 51% of respondents cited data upload/book roll capability in 2025) [11].
- **Local loyalty erodes post-acquisition.** Legacy MA agencies with 40-year Commerce relationships become branches of national platforms whose placement decisions are driven by aggregated compensation agreements and ease-of-use, not history. Carrier appetite/tech that isn't competitive gets deprioritized in the platform's placement stack.
- **A window to influence the consolidators.** Carriers that court the acquirers (national compensation agreements, dedicated platform teams, book-roll tooling) can convert consolidation from a threat into a share-shift mechanism. Carriers that only service the shrinking population of one-office agencies inherit the runoff.

## 4. What independent agents say they want from carriers

Convergent findings across four major research streams:

### 4.1 Big "I"/Future One Agency Universe Study (2024)

- **#1 agency challenge: "finding carriers that will maintain their commitment to their market" — 56% of agencies, up from 31% in 2022.** #2: carriers addressing new personal lines risks with new products/coverages (49%) [16][22].
- Only **11% of agencies say carriers are meeting current market challenges "extremely well"**; 21% say "extremely poorly" [22].
- Agencies want support in market/product access, client communication, and technology [22].

### 4.2 J.D. Power / Big "I" Independent Agent Satisfaction Study

- **2024 study (published Oct 2024):** the top satisfaction drivers for personal lines agents were (1) an **easy quoting experience** (carrier portals are the most common quote-initiation channel), (2) **claims communication** that keeps the agent informed, (3) **training** (product + business-growth), and (4) **underwriting flexibility**. Personal lines satisfaction averaged 774/1,000; top personal lines carriers: **Erie, Auto-Owners, The Hanover** [9].
- **62% of personal lines agents proactively shop clients to other carriers** ahead of renewal — shelf position is contested at every renewal, not just at new business [9].
- **2025 study (fielded May–July 2025):** only **56% of personal lines agents say carriers meet their foundational needs**; **25% feel "not valued"**; only **40% describe carriers as easy to work with**. Satisfaction is **274 points (on a 1,000-point scale) higher** when working with the insurer is rated "very easy" [10].

### 4.3 IVANS Agency-Carrier Connectivity surveys

- **2025 (published Dec 2, 2025; 700+ respondents):** for the first time, **real-time appetite information is the #1 factor in carrier selection (29%, up from 12% in 2024)**, ahead of policy download (23%) and quoting/submission capability (22%). 52% want customized products; agents "want to connect with carriers in the workflows they use every day" [11].
- **2024 survey (1,456 respondents):** **83–87% of agents said they would write MORE business with carriers that provide real-time appetite and quoting inside their agency management systems** [23][24].

### 4.4 Deep Customer Connections (ease-of-doing-business index)

- DCC's annual index has ~8,700 agents rating 250+ carriers on 11 ease-of-doing-business factors; agents rate EDB's importance in carrier choice at **3.82 on a 4-point scale, with 96% strongly agreeing it is critical** (older survey wave; directionally consistent with the 2025 J.D. Power findings) [12][25]. Notably, MA rival **Safety Insurance has ranked as high as #4 nationally** on the DCC index — Safety explicitly markets ease-of-doing-business as its agent value proposition [12].

**Synthesis — the agent's decision function, in rough priority order:** (1) price competitiveness/stability for their client, (2) ease of quote-and-issue in *their* systems, (3) confidence the carrier will stay in the market (appetite stability), (4) claims handling that doesn't embarrass them, (5) compensation, (6) service that offloads work (download accuracy, service centers). Compensation is a tie-breaker and loyalty-builder, not a substitute for the first four.

## 5. Technology that decides shelf space

- **Comparative raters are the personal-lines storefront.** In agency-distributed personal auto, the agent's CSR typically enters risk data once into a comparative rater and presents the returned quotes. The dominant raters relevant to Massachusetts agencies are **Vertafore PL Rating** (deep integration with Vertafore AMS360/QQCatalyst shops) and **Applied's EZLynx** (20,000+ agencies, 8M+ rating transactions/month, real-time quotes in 48 states), plus ITC TurboRater and IBQ in some shops [26][27][28]. A carrier that is absent, slow, or inaccurate in the rater is invisible at the moment of quote. (I could not find a public, current census of which raters hold what share among MA agencies specifically — a candidate for primary interviews.)
- **Real-time rating and API connectivity** are now the top-cited carrier selection factor (IVANS 2025: 29%) [11]; 83–87% of agents say they'd write more with carriers offering real-time appetite/quoting inside their AMS (IVANS 2024) [23][24].
- **Policy download** (carrier-to-AMS data sync via IVANS) remains table stakes: #2 connectivity factor at 23% in 2025 [11]. Poor download quality creates re-keying work that agents punish with placement.
- **Carrier portals still matter** — they are the most common quote-initiation channel, and easy-to-find support material on the portal measurably lifts satisfaction (53% of personal lines agents say material is easy to find today) [9][10].
- **Service centers**: carriers such as Safeco (four centers) and Travelers (four personal lines centers, including **Fall River, Massachusetts**) run fee-based service centers that take over policy servicing/billing for agency customers; agencies using them can shift CSR time toward sales dramatically (from ~10% to as much as 50% per Rough Notes reporting — dated 2003, but the model persists) [29]. For consolidating agencies with CSR staffing pressure, a credible service center is a differentiator.
- **Book-roll tooling**: 51% of IVANS 2025 respondents prioritize data upload/book-roll capability [11] — directly relevant to winning displaced books when agencies are acquired or a competitor pulls back.

## 6. Compensation: commissions and profit sharing

**Base commissions (national norms, 2024–2026 trade sources; carrier-specific rates are rarely public):**

| Component | Typical range (personal lines) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| New business commission | ~12–15% (sources cite 8–15%, some up to 20%) | Personal auto usually at the low end [13][14] |
| Renewal commission | ~10–15% (often same as new for IA carriers) | IA renewals structurally richer than captive (4–7%) [13][14] |
| Contingent/profit-sharing | ~2–5% of written premium | Loss-ratio-gated (e.g., <40% LR → ~4%; <50% → ~2%; >60% → 0), usually with volume ($100K–$500K) and growth gates [13] |
| Contingents' share of agency profit | ~45% of average agency's total profit (trade estimate) | Makes profit-sharing design a powerful loyalty lever [13] |

**Massachusetts specifics:**

- Pre-managed-competition, MA regulation set a floor commission (13.0% of premium in 2007–2008); carriers competed above it. Safety's disclosed structure at the time: **President's Club agents earned 18%** and Executive Club agents 15% on safe-driver policies, plus an **agency incentive commission of up to 8% of premium based on loss ratio** [30]. Post-2008, commissions are carrier-set, but this remains the best public window into MA auto commission architecture.
- Safety's FY2024 10-K confirms the model persists: an agent force of **828 agencies / 1,079 locations** across MA/NH/ME, "competitive commissions," a **loss-ratio-based incentive commission program**, and a five-tier recognition ladder (**Chairman's Elite, Chairman's Club, President's Club, Executive's Club, Preferred Agent's Club**). Voluntary agents produced 95.8% of Safety's auto DWP in 2024 [14].
- MAPFRE/Commerce's own current commission schedule is not public; MA's AG unsuccessfully challenged the rate-filing inclusion of contingent commission expense in 2008, confirming contingents are an accepted MA cost structure [30].

**How carriers use compensation to win shelf space:** tiered base rates tied to volume/loyalty status; loss-ratio-triggered contingents that reward clean books; growth bonuses; national/aggregator agreements for platforms and networks (SIAA-type market-access groups take commission overrides); and "club" recognition with trips/perks that create emotional switching costs. The competitive lesson from Safety and Erie/Auto-Owners (J.D. Power's perennial personal lines leaders [9]) is that **compensation is fused to relationship architecture** — top-tier agents get better economics *and* better access (dedicated underwriters, marketing reps), making the carrier's shelf position self-reinforcing.

## 7. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy

1. **The IA channel is the right core to defend — but "defend" now means re-earning shelf space every renewal.** Massachusetts is the most agency-penetrated state in the country (78.8% of P&C, 2024) [3], and 62% of personal lines agents proactively shop clients [9]. Commerce's 4-point share loss since 2020 [4] is best read as agents quietly re-placing marginal risks with Progressive (which pays agents *and* out-executes on tech). The first strategic move is a fact-based diagnosis of where Commerce sits in MA agents' quote stack (rater win rate, quote-to-bind, EDB scores) versus Progressive, Safety, Plymouth Rock and Arbella.
2. **Win the rater and the API, or lose invisibly.** Real-time appetite/quoting inside agency systems is now the #1 stated driver of carrier selection [11], and 83–87% of agents say they'd write more with carriers that provide it [23]. Rating accuracy/speed in PL Rating and EZLynx, flawless IVANS download, and published APIs are the highest-ROI channel investments available — they compound with, rather than compete against, any future direct capability.
3. **Treat consolidators as a segment, not a threat.** With ~73% of deals PE-backed [5] and 31 MA agencies changing hands in 2025 [6], MAPFRE should build a named-account program for the 10–15 platforms rolling up its distribution (World, Patriot, Hilb, Trucordia, Kaplansky, Duffy, DiVirgilio, King Risk, etc.): national compensation agreements, book-roll tooling, dedicated service teams. Simultaneously, deepen moats with the durable independents through top-tier economics and service-center support — the Safety playbook, executed with MAPFRE's scale advantage.
4. **Exploit the satisfaction gap.** Only 40% of personal lines agents call their carriers easy to work with; 25% feel unvalued [10]. Erie, Auto-Owners and Hanover show that EDB leadership is a durable, compounding advantage [9]. As MA's #1 auto writer, MAPFRE can credibly aim to be the *easiest* MA auto carrier to do business with — a positioning no direct writer can copy and one that blunts Progressive's agency-side advance.
5. **Compensation is the lock-in, not the hook.** Match the market on base commission; differentiate on profit-sharing design (growth + retention kickers, not just loss ratio), tier benefits (dedicated underwriting access, marketing co-op, service center), and recognition. Given contingents are ~45% of agency profit [13], a well-designed program buys measurable placement priority for a known cost.
6. **The direct-channel question should be sequenced, not avoided.** Progressive's MA rise shows the winning model is *dual-channel executed without alienating agents* (agent-branded direct handoffs, agent-of-record preservation). Fixing quote-to-bind on MAPFRE's own site matters for brand-driven demand capture, but the channel math says the near-term premium pool at stake sits with the ~1,000+ MA agencies, not with a nascent direct book.
