# MAPFRE S.A. Global Strategy and What It Means for the U.S. Unit

**Research brief — MAPFRE Insurance (U.S.) growth strategy engagement**
Prepared July 2, 2026. All figures carry their as-of dates; euro figures are as reported by MAPFRE S.A. (IFRS), U.S. state-level figures are statutory/CAR data.

---

## 1. Executive summary

- MAPFRE S.A. is executing its **2024–2026 Strategic Plan**, originally targeting >6% average premium growth, 10–11% ROE and a 95–96% combined ratio [1][2]. It has **raised the targets twice** — at the March 2025 AGM (ROE 11–12%, CR 94–95%) [3] and again at the March 2026 AGM (**ROE >13% and CR 93–94% for 2026**) [4][5] — after record results: FY2025 net profit of €1.1 billion (+19.6%), group non-life combined ratio of 92.2% and ROE of 12.4% [4][6].
- **North America is framed as a completed turnaround, not a growth engine.** After a ~€1.8 million net loss in 2023, the region swung to €97.6 million profit in 2024 (CR 98.7%) [7] and €139 million in 2025 (CR 95.4%, "best ever" per management) [6][8]. Management's stance entering 2026: pivot from rate-driven repair to "profitable growth" in policy count and average premium — but explicitly **"not planning to broaden our footprint"**; U.S. M&A appetite is limited to "complementary, single-state operations" [8].
- The U.S. retrenchment was deeper and longer than commonly remembered: announced December 2017 (exit NY, NJ, KY, TN, IN; sale of NY/NJ books to Plymouth Rock) [9][10][11], followed by Florida commercial runoff (2019) and Florida personal auto runoff (2022) [12], and the quiet shutdown of the Verti U.S. digital venture in Pennsylvania (ceased selling by ~late 2021) [13][14]. Today MAPFRE writes personal auto in **11 states** (MA, CT, RI, NH, ME, VT, AZ, CA, ID, OR, WA per a December 2025 review) [15], with Massachusetts overwhelmingly dominant.
- In its core market, **Commerce's Massachusetts private passenger auto share has eroded from 22.1% (2020) to 18.33% (December 2025)** while Progressive grew from 5.1% to 12.17% [16]. Two affinity pillars are in play: the AAA Washington JV ("Auto Club MAPFRE Insurance Company", 2021) [17][18] and the AAA Northeast exclusive marketing agreement (>$200 million of 2025 premium), which **expires December 31, 2026 and is now the subject of litigation** after AAA Northeast began selling through its own carrier [19][20].
- Globally, MAPFRE's inorganic playbook is **bancassurance and affinity JVs plus bolt-on acquisitions** (Santander Spain JV to 2037 [21][22]; €571 million exit payment from the CaixaBank/Bankia unwind [23]; Insignia Life, Mexico, 2023 [24]) and **digital direct via the Verti brand** — a success in Germany (~1 million policies, #2 direct auto insurer) [25] but a failure in the U.S. [13]. This asymmetry matters: the parent has proven direct-channel capability it has never successfully redeployed in the U.S.

## 2. MAPFRE S.A.'s global strategic plan: targets and capital allocation

### 2.1 The 2024–2026 plan and its two upgrades

| Metric | Original plan (AGM, Mar 2024) | Raised (AGM, Mar 2025) | Raised again (AGM, Mar 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Avg. premium growth (ex-Life Savings, constant FX) | >6% | unchanged | unchanged |
| ROE | 10–11% avg (11% aspirational for 2026) | 11–12% avg | **>13% in 2026** |
| Combined ratio | 95–96% avg (95% aspirational end-2026) | 94–95% avg | **93–94% in 2026** |
| Solvency II ratio | 200% ±25 p.p. | unchanged | unchanged |
| Dividend payout | >50% of net result | unchanged | unchanged |
| Leverage | ~24% debt ratio | unchanged | unchanged |

Sources: [1][2][3][4][5]. Both 2026 targets are explicitly conditioned on inflation not rising significantly [4].

Delivery to date: FY2024 premiums €28.1 billion (+4.5%; +6.6% constant FX), net result €902 million (+30%), ROE 12%, non-life CR 94.4% [7]. FY2025 net result €1.1 billion (+19.6%, first time above €1 billion), premiums +3.6% in euros / +7.8% constant FX, CR 92.2% (best in company history), ROE 12.4%; record dividend of €0.18/share (€554 million, 51% payout) approved March 2026 [4][6].

### 2.2 Capital allocation philosophy

- **Dividend-first**: >50% payout commitment; the dividend has been raised each year of the plan [1][4].
- **Solvency headroom used sparingly**: Solvency II ~200%+ leaves room for deals, but management's stated M&A frame (Q4 2025 earnings call, February 2026) is *deepening existing markets, not entering new ones*: priorities named were distribution in Spain, Germany, Brazil and Mexico (respecting the Banco do Brasil agreement), and in the U.S. only **"complementary" single-state operations**; preference for growth "through new agreements and alliances in bancassurance" [8][26].
- Historic pattern confirms bolt-on scale: roughly six acquisitions in the last decade averaging ~$95 million, most recently Insignia Life (Mexico, July 2023) [24]. (Tracxn aggregator figure — directional, not audited.)

## 3. How the parent characterizes North America / USA

**Investor-communication framing has moved through three phases: fix-it (2017–2023) → turnaround proof-point (2024–2025) → selective profitable growth (2026).**

| North America (MAPFRE regional segment) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Premiums | ~€2.7bn (implied) | €2.8bn (+3.2% €; US: €2.4bn, +3.8%) | €2.6bn (−4.6% €, ~flat local FX; US: €2.3bn) |
| Net result | −€1.8m | €97.6m | €139m (+41.8%; US: €118.2m) |
| Non-life combined ratio | ~105.4% (implied from −6.7 p.p. improvement) | 98.7% | 95.4% |

Sources: [6][7][8]. FY2023 figures are back-calculated from MAPFRE's stated year-over-year deltas in the FY2024 release [7]; treat as approximate.

Language to note:

- FY2024 release attributes the €99 million swing to "significant tariff adjustments being implemented and benign weather" [7] — i.e., a rate-and-underwriting repair story, not a growth story.
- FY2025 release credits "technical measures and tariff adjustments implemented in recent years" plus benign H2-2025 weather; management called U.S. combined ratios "the best ever" [6][8].
- Q4 2025 call (Feb 2026): North America goal is now "profitable growth" — growing both policy count and average premium — with 2026 group emphasis on growth "especially in those segments where profitability has already reached acceptable levels, including motor" [8].
- A Q1 2025 presentation summary put the U.S. target combined ratio at **95–96% for 2025–2026** [27]. (Secondary source; the primary investor materials frame CR targets at group level, so treat the US-specific number as indicative.)

**Bottom line:** the U.S. is neither a designated growth market (like Iberia/LatAm) nor a harvest/exit candidate; it is a *conditionally retained* market that has just earned back the right to grow — organically, within its existing footprint. The strategic plan's public materials contain **no U.S.-specific growth commitments** [1][4].

## 4. The U.S. retrenchment: what happened, and where MAPFRE still writes

### 4.1 Timeline of retrenchment (2017–2022)

| Date | Action |
|---|---|
| Dec 2017 | Reorganization announced: exit **NY, NJ, KY, TN, IN** (together ~5% of MAPFRE USA premiums but a disproportionate share of underwriting losses); focus on 11 core states (MA, CT, RI, NH, ME, VT, OH, CA, OR, WA, ID) with FL, PA, AZ retained pending "reinforced underwriting and distribution foundation"; sale of NY subsidiary and life unit initiated [9][10][11][28] |
| Oct 2017 / Dec 2017 | Verti USA digital auto soft-launched, then formally launched, Pennsylvania only [10][29] |
| 2018–2019 | Definitive agreements with Plymouth Rock affiliates for the NY and NJ businesses; Plymouth Rock approval to acquire MAPFRE Insurance Co. of New York [11][30] |
| Jun 2019 | Florida commercial lines placed in runoff [12] |
| ~late 2021 | Verti U.S. stops selling new policies (no formal announcement; Coverager documented the site dark by Oct 2022, noting sales had ceased "at least 11 months" earlier; BBB later listed the business as out of business) [13][14] |

### 4.2 Current footprint (as of December 2025)

Personal auto is sold in **11 states: Arizona, California, Connecticut, Idaho, Massachusetts, Maine, New Hampshire, Oregon, Rhode Island, Vermont, Washington** (NerdWallet review dated Dec 19, 2025) [15]. MAPFRE's own site says it serves policyholders in 11 states through **3,000+ independent agents and brokers**, and describes itself as the **21st-largest U.S. private passenger auto insurer** and the largest home and auto insurer in Massachusetts [31]. Ohio appears in the 2017 "core 11" list [9] and in some older company materials but not in the December 2025 state list [15]; Pennsylvania retail presence effectively ended with Verti's shutdown. **Unresolved:** whether MAPFRE still writes any residual Ohio/Pennsylvania business — verify against NAIC state pages/statutory filings.

## 5. Parent expectations vs. U.S. performance

- **Group benchmarks the U.S. must clear:** ROE >13% and CR 93–94% at group level for 2026 [4]; every region is expected to contribute to "technical excellence." The only publicly stated U.S.-specific bar found is the ~95–96% CR for 2025–26 [27] — which the unit **met in 2025 (95.4%)** [6].
- **Implied U.S. ROE:** MAPFRE does not disclose allocated-equity ROE for the U.S. unit. With €118.2 million of 2025 net result on €2.3 billion of premiums [6], the unit is profitable but almost certainly still below the group's 12.4% ROE; AM Best's rating rationale for MAPFRE U.S.A. Group (FSR A/stable, lead carrier The Commerce Insurance Company) still assessed operating performance as **"marginal"**, with the ratings explicitly supported by implicit and explicit parent support [34][35]. No public source quantifies the parent's ROE hurdle for the U.S.; flag as an open question for management interviews.
- **Trajectory:** U.S./North America CR ~105% (2023) → 98.7% (2024) → 95.4% (2025) [6][7]; auto-line CR for the region improved 6.0 points in 2024 to 101.2% [7]. Management repeatedly credits rate ("tariff") actions and benign weather — meaning part of the 2025 result is weather luck, and the parent knows it [6].
- **Premium reality check:** 2025 U.S. premiums *shrank* in euros (−4.6% for the region) and were roughly flat in dollars [6][8] — the profitability was bought partly with share (see the 4-point MA share loss [16]). The parent's 2026 "grow policies and average premium" instruction [8] therefore lands on a unit that has been trading growth for margin.

## 6. Global appetite for M&A, bancassurance/affinity, and digital — and U.S. relevance

**Bancassurance/affinity is MAPFRE's signature inorganic move.**
- Santander Spain JV (signed Jan 2019; operating from 2020): MAPFRE paid €82.2 million for 50.01% of a JV with exclusive distribution of auto, SME and liability products through Santander's Spanish network **until December 2037** [21][22].
- CaixaBank/Bankia unwind (2021): MAPFRE exited the Bankia bancassurance JV after the CaixaBank merger, collecting **€571 million** [23] — evidence it also manages these partnerships with financial discipline.
- Q4 2025 call: growth "through new agreements and alliances in bancassurance" named as the preferred inorganic route [8].

**In the U.S., the affinity analog is AAA — and it is both an asset and an acute risk.**
- **Auto Club MAPFRE Insurance Company**: 50/50-style JV with AAA Washington (announced Aug 10, 2021) writing AAA-branded auto and homeowners in Washington and North Idaho; AM Best assigned ratings in Aug 2024 [17][18][36]. This is the live template for affinity-driven U.S. growth.
- **AAA Northeast**: a 20-year exclusive joint marketing agreement (from 2007) gives MAPFRE sole rights to sell auto insurance to AAA Northeast members under the AAA emblem at group discounts; the channel produced **>$200 million of premium in 2025** (roughly 10% of U.S. premiums). AAA Northeast began selling through its affiliated Motor Club Insurance Co. on Jan 1, 2026; MAPFRE sued and was **denied a preliminary injunction on Feb 3, 2026**; the JMA **expires Dec 31, 2026** [19][20][37]. A >$200 million affinity book is at risk within the strategy horizon.

**M&A**: bolt-on only. ~6 deals in a decade at ~$95 million average; most recent Insignia Life (Mexico, 2023); none completed in 2024–2025 per Tracxn [24]. For the U.S., management's own words limit appetite to "complementary" single-state operations [8] — i.e., a New England regional book or an agency-channel tuck-in is plausible; a transformational U.S. deal is not.

**Digital (Verti)**: global direct brand since 2011, present in Spain, Germany, Italy (and formerly the U.S.) [25][38]. Verti Germany reached ~1 million policies and #2 among German direct auto insurers [25], though MAPFRE took a €90 million partial goodwill writedown on Verti Germany in 9M 2024 [39]. **Verti USA failed**: launched in Pennsylvania Dec 2017 [10][29], stopped selling by roughly late 2021, never expanded beyond one state [13][14]. The group therefore has direct-channel operating know-how in Europe but a burned hand in the U.S. — consistent with the client's non-functional direct channel today.

## 7. So what for MAPFRE Insurance's U.S. growth strategy

1. **The window for a growth ask is open — for the first time in a decade.** The parent has record profits, a raised-twice plan, solvency headroom, and has publicly switched the 2026 message to growth in motor where profitability is "acceptable" [4][8]. The U.S. unit just posted its best-ever CR and €118 million of profit [6]. A credible, capital-light growth plan will get a hearing in Madrid now; it would not have in 2019–2023.
2. **But the parent's guardrails are explicit: no new states of consequence, no big U.S. M&A.** "Not planning to broaden our footprint"; only "complementary" single-state deals [8]. The growth strategy should therefore be built on **share recapture and product density in the existing 11-state footprint — above all Massachusetts** — plus affinity partnerships, not geographic expansion.
3. **The burning platform is share erosion to direct writers, and 2026 makes it worse.** Four points of MA share lost since 2020, with Progressive alone taking ~7 points [16]; the AAA Northeast channel (>$200m premium) may walk on Dec 31, 2026 [19][20]. A strategy that only defends margin will see the parent's "profitable" unit shrink into irrelevance in its own home market.
4. **Direct/digital is the group's most awkward gap in the U.S.** Verti proves the group will fund direct ventures and can run them at scale (Germany) — and also that a subscale, single-state U.S. launch fails [13][25]. The realistic pitch is not "relaunch Verti" but a **bind-capable digital experience for the existing brand and agents** (agent-assisted digital, AAA-style affinity digital storefronts), framed as defending the €2.3 billion U.S. book rather than as a new venture.
5. **Affinity is the parent-endorsed growth vector.** The Santander template (exclusive multi-decade distribution JV) and the AAA Washington JV show exactly what Madrid will approve [8][17][21]. Priority moves: renegotiate/replace the AAA Northeast relationship before Dec 2026, and pursue additional affinity/embedded partners (credit unions, regional banks, auto clubs) in-footprint.
6. **Frame every initiative against the 2026+ group bar: CR ≤95–96% U.S., trending to group's 93–94%, and visible ROE progression** [4][27]. The unit's license to grow is conditional; growth proposals that risk the CR will re-trigger the retrenchment reflex demonstrated in 2017–2022 [9][12].
