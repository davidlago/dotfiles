# MAPFRE USA / The Commerce Insurance Company — Company Profile & Performance
### Research brief for growth-strategy fact base | Prepared July 2, 2026 | All figures carry as-of dates; sources numbered at end

---

## 1. Who the company is

MAPFRE U.S.A. Corp. ("MAPFRE USA", brand name "MAPFRE Insurance") is the U.S. property/casualty arm of Madrid-based MAPFRE S.A., itself majority-controlled (68.2%) by Fundación MAPFRE [11]. Its lead underwriting entity is The Commerce Insurance Company of Webster, Massachusetts, founded in 1972 [14][16]. Commerce is the lead insurer of an intercompany reinsurance pool of the group's U.S. carriers; as of December 31, 2023 the pool participations were: Commerce Insurance Company 70.8%, American Commerce Insurance Company 10.9%, Citation Insurance Company 7.4%, Commerce West Insurance Company 6.1%, MAPFRE Insurance Company 2.4% (per the Massachusetts Division of Insurance statutory financial examination) [11].

The company describes itself (company website, accessed July 2026) as serving policyholders in 11 states through more than 3,000 independent agents and brokers, and as the 21st-largest private passenger auto insurer and 23rd-largest personal lines carrier in the U.S. [14]. It is, by a wide margin, the #1 private passenger auto insurer and the #1 voluntary-market homeowners insurer in Massachusetts (details in Section 3) [1][23].

## 2. Premium volumes, underwriting results and 5-year trend

### 2.1 MAPFRE "North America" segment (IFRS, group reporting; includes Puerto Rico)

MAPFRE S.A. reports the U.S. inside a "North America" regional segment (U.S. + Puerto Rico). The story of the last five years is a classic post-COVID auto-inflation shock followed by a rate-driven recovery:

| Metric (IFRS, €) | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North America premiums | ~€2.6bn (implied) [9] | ~€2.7bn (+3.6%) [9] | ~€2.8bn (+3.2%) [8] | >€2.6bn (−4.6% in €, ~flat in local currency) [10] |
| — of which United States | n/a | ~€2.3bn (+2.4%) [9] | ~€2.4bn (+3.8%) [8] | ~€2.3bn [10] |
| NA attributable result | loss (motor-inflation-driven) [9] | −€1.8m [8][9] | +€97.6m [8] | +€139m (+41.8%), incl. U.S. €118.2m [10] |
| NA combined ratio (non-life) | ~108.3% (derived: 2023's 105.4% improved 2.9 p.p. y/y) [9] | 105.4% [9] | 98.7% (−6.7 p.p.) [8] | 95.4% (−3.3 p.p.) [10] |
| NA auto combined ratio | ~109.6% (derived) [9] | 107.1% [9] | 101.2% (−6.0 p.p.) [8] | 97.8% (−3.3 p.p.) [10] |
| NA general P&C combined ratio | ~100.8%+ [9] | 100.8% [9] | 84.3% (−16.5 p.p.) [8] | 78.9% (−5.5 p.p.) [10] |

Key facts behind the table:

- **The 2022–23 trough.** MAPFRE's U.S. motor book was hit hard by claims severity inflation. In H1 2023 the U.S. non-life combined ratio was 107.5% and the U.S. auto combined ratio 108.7%; group CFO Fernando Mata said it would "take a few more quarters" for U.S. pricing actions to translate into improved underwriting performance [25]. Full-year 2023 North America produced an attributable loss of €1.8m despite Puerto Rico contributing +€27.9m — i.e., the U.S. alone lost roughly €30m [9][8].
- **Cumulative rate taken.** MAPFRE disclosed U.S. tariff (rate) increases of "more than 29% since January 2022" as of the FY2023 presentation (February 2024) [9].
- **The 2024–25 recovery.** 2024 result swung +€99m to €97.6m; 2025 was a record €139m for the region (U.S. €118.2m, Puerto Rico €20.3m), with the auto combined ratio finally under 100% at 97.8% and General P&C at a very strong 78.9%, helped by "technical measures and tariff adjustments" and benign second-half 2025 weather [8][10]. Group Executive Chairman Antonio Huertas framed 2025 as "the final stretch of the 2024-26 Strategic Plan with record results" [10].
- **The growth problem.** Note the top line: 2025 North America premiums *declined* ~1.1% in local currency [10, and prior search of FY2025 activity report], and MAPFRE Insurance Group **dropped out of the NAIC top-25 U.S. private passenger auto writers in 2024**, having ranked 21st in 2023 with $1.40bn of PP auto direct written premium (vs. State Farm's $67.75bn, Progressive's $60.05bn in a $358.97bn 2024 market) [22]. The recovery has been a profitability story, not a growth story.

### 2.2 Statutory results — The Commerce Insurance Company (lead pool entity)

From the Massachusetts DOI financial examination as of December 31, 2023 (Commerce's ~70.8% share of the pooled group) [11]:

- Premiums earned (2023): **$1,144.2m**
- Net underwriting loss (2023): **−$109.2m** (total underwriting deductions $1,253.4m against $1,144.2m earned — a statutory "combined" burn of roughly 109.5% on earned premium)
- Net investment gain: $36.2m; **net loss of −$41.5m** for 2023
- Policyholders' surplus: **$561.0m at YE2023**, down from $589.8m at YE2022, after a $25m stockholder dividend
- The exam also states the MAPFRE Group wrote direct premium "of over $291 million in 2023... across fifty-two states and territories" — this is almost certainly a typographical error in the exam report for ~$2.91bn, which reconciles with the group's reported North America premiums of ~€2.7bn ($2.9bn) in 2023 [11][9]. Flagged rather than silently corrected.

**Policy counts:** Commonwealth Automobile Reinsurers (CAR) data via Agency Checklists shows Commerce with **936,254 Massachusetts private passenger auto exposures at December 2024** [2]. National in-force policy counts across all lines are not published; the company cites 3,000+ agents and 11 states but no policyholder total for the U.S. specifically [14]. (Not publicly sourced: total U.S. PIF count.)

## 3. Market position, product mix and geography

### 3.1 Massachusetts private passenger auto — the core franchise

- **#1 in Massachusetts, continuously, with ~18–19% share.** At December 2024: **19.02% share, 936,254 exposures** in a 4,921,462-exposure market; the next largest were GEICO 12.61%, Progressive 9.78%, Safety 9.63%, Plymouth Rock 7.70%, Arbella 7.25% [2].
- At August 2025: Commerce **18.22%** (the report notes Commerce held a narrow 18.1–18.3% band all year), with **Progressive overtaking GEICO in May 2025** (11.92% vs. 11.56%); the two direct writers together now hold ~23.5% of the MA market [1]. Direction of travel: Commerce's share is slowly eroding (19.0% → 18.2% during 2025) while direct writers grow.
- Massachusetts PP auto is distributed overwhelmingly through independent agents, and Commerce's dominance is built on that channel plus AAA affinity marketing (Section 5).

### 3.2 Massachusetts homeowners

- MAPFRE Insurance Group was the **largest voluntary-market home insurer in Massachusetts in 2024 with 11.0% of written premium** (market total ~$3.6bn), **down from 12.5% in 2023** — a meaningful one-year share loss in its #2 product [23]. MA home premiums rose 12.7% market-wide in 2024, so MAPFRE's dollar volume was roughly flat while the market grew [23].

### 3.3 Product and geographic mix

- Per the 2023 statutory exam: core products are **private passenger auto, homeowners, and commercial auto**, marketed "primarily through a network of independent agents." Commerce itself (the pooled lead company) writes business entirely within six states — **Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Maine, Vermont, Connecticut, Rhode Island** — and the group's top five states by direct premium are **Massachusetts, Connecticut, California, Washington, and Rhode Island** [11].
- Group carriers outside the Northeast: Commerce West (California), American Commerce (Ohio-domiciled, historically the AAA-club vehicle), and **Auto Club MAPFRE Insurance Company**, a JV with AAA Washington formed in 2021 to write auto and homeowners in Washington and North Idaho [26][11].
- The client's own framing (~80% of revenue from MA personal auto) is consistent with, though somewhat stronger than, what public data show: MA PP auto DWP of roughly $1.2–1.3bn (19% of a ~$6.5bn market) plus MA home (~$0.4bn) against total U.S. premiums of ~$2.5bn implies **Massachusetts is roughly two-thirds of U.S. premium, and MA personal auto alone roughly half**; adding other Northeast personal auto gets close to the 80% figure. (Precise split not published; NAIC state-line pages would confirm.) [1][2][11][23]

## 4. AM Best rating and what it says

- **Current rating:** FSR **A (Excellent)**, stable outlook. In its October 2024 group action, AM Best affirmed FSR A / Long-Term ICR "a+" (stable) for MAPFRE S.A.'s rated operating subsidiaries including The Commerce Insurance Company, Citation, Commerce West, American Commerce and MAPFRE Insurance Company — reflecting MAPFRE S.A.'s "very strong" balance sheet (BCAR above the strongest threshold), partly offset by "poor performance on the motor line of business in most geographies" [4]. The U.S. entities' standing therefore leans significantly on **parent support**.
- **Standalone view of the U.S. group (pre-group-lens affirmations, 2019–2021):** AM Best characterized MAPFRE U.S.A. Group as having "strong" balance sheet strength but **"marginal operating performance, neutral business profile and appropriate enterprise risk management,"** explicitly crediting "implicit and explicit support provided by its parent company, MAPFRE" [3]. AM Best has also noted the group's five-year average return on revenue/equity "lags industry averages by a considerable margin," hurt by adverse reserve development and weather losses [source: AM Best commentary summarized in rating coverage; see 3, 5].
- **Concentration risk:** AM Best's "neutral" (rather than favorable) business profile assessment for a top-20 personal lines writer reflects the geographic and product concentration in Massachusetts personal auto; the public press releases do not spell this out in detail, but the assessment language and the reliance on parental support are the observable markers [3][4].
- **Warning light on the AAA Washington JV:** In August 2025 AM Best placed **Auto Club MAPFRE Insurance Company** (the WA/ID JV) **under review with negative implications** citing a decline in surplus and risk-adjusted capitalization as of Q2 2025 [5].

## 5. History and the AAA relationships (now in litigation)

- **1972:** Commerce Insurance founded in Webster, MA [14][16]. **1995:** Commerce Group acquired California's Western Pioneer (renamed Commerce West) and pioneered AAA group-discount marketing of auto insurance in Massachusetts [14]. **1999:** acquired Automobile Club Insurance Company (Columbus, OH) in a joint venture with AAA clubs; rebranded American Commerce Insurance Company [14]. (Note: the company's own "Who We Are" page loosely attributes these pre-2008 moves to "MAPFRE"; they were Commerce Group transactions [14].)
- **June 4, 2008:** MAPFRE S.A. completed the acquisition of The Commerce Group, Inc. for **$36.70/share in cash, total consideration ~$2,211m** — the largest acquisition in MAPFRE's history. Commerce was then the 20th-largest U.S. auto insurer and the largest auto and home insurer in Massachusetts, and the largest employer in Webster (~1,900 staff) [15][16]. **2010:** rebranded MAPFRE U.S.A. Corp. [14].
- **December 2017 reorganization ("less is more"):** MAPFRE USA announced exit from five states (NY, NJ, KY, TN, IN) to focus on 11 core states — MA, CT, RI, NH, ME, VT, OH, CA, OR, WA, ID — where Massachusetts and the Northeast comprised 67% of U.S. premium; in June 2018 it sold MAPFRE Insurance Company of New York to Plymouth Rock and transferred its NJ personal lines book to Plymouth Rock's Palisades affiliates [12][13].
- **AAA affinity today — under severe threat.** Since **2007**, a joint marketing agreement (JMA) gave MAPFRE exclusive rights to market auto insurance to AAA Northeast members under the AAA emblem, running to **December 31, 2026**; MAPFRE says it generated **"more than $200 million in premiums... in 2025 alone"** [6]. On January 7, 2026 MAPFRE sued AAA Northeast, alleging AAA began selling auto policies through its affiliated insurer Motor Club Insurance Co. a year before expiry, at heavily discounted member rates [6]. On **February 3, 2026, Massachusetts Superior Court (Judge Kenneth Salinger) denied MAPFRE's preliminary injunction**, finding the harm compensable in money damages; AAA contends exclusivity effectively ended in 2008 and that it need only keep ≥50% of its auto business with MAPFRE [7]. Practical read: **~$200m of affinity-sourced premium is now contestable, and the JMA expires at end-2026 regardless.**
- **AAA Washington JV (2021):** Auto Club MAPFRE Insurance Company formed with AAA Washington for WA/North Idaho [26] — now capital-stressed per AM Best (Section 4) [5].

## 7. Recent news, rates, technology, organization (2023–2026)

- **Rates:** MAPFRE implemented U.S. auto rate increases exceeding 29% cumulative from January 2022 through end-2023 [9], including MA increases up to ~13% in 2023 [25]; the Boston Globe reported (Oct 17, 2024) MAPFRE had two further approved MA rate filings effective after October 17, 2024 [24]. Separately, Massachusetts raised minimum auto limits effective July 1, 2025 (H.5111, signed Dec 2024), lifting premiums on minimum-limits policies [27].
- **Technology:** MAPFRE USA completed a core-systems modernization — Guidewire InsuranceSuite (policy, billing, claims, rating) live for personal lines as of November 2020 with commercial lines moving to Guidewire Cloud, plus omnichannel EnterpriseEngage portals [20]; CGI's case study describes the modern stack (Guidewire + Duck Creek components) enabling API integration with partners, analytics, and AI-assisted claims [21]. Important nuance for strategy work: the digital plumbing for a direct/omnichannel play largely exists; the direct channel itself does not.
- **Organization:** COO role created/filled (Olohan, 2024) [18]; new GC (Pasko, 2025) [19]; MAPFRE USA celebrated its 50th anniversary in Webster in 2022 [businesswire, 14].
- **Litigation/affinity:** AAA Northeast dispute (Section 5) [6][7]. AM Best review of the AAA Washington JV [5].

## 8. So what for MAPFRE's growth strategy (analytical take)

1. **Profitability is fixed; growth is not.** The 2022→2025 turnaround (NA combined ratio 108%→95.4%, record €139m result) was bought with ~30%+ cumulative rate, and premiums went flat-to-negative in local currency in 2025 while MAPFRE fell out of the national top-25 auto writers [8][9][10][22]. The client enters 2026 with underwriting headroom to invest in growth for the first time in years — but with momentum pointing the wrong way on volume.
2. **The moat is eroding on two fronts simultaneously.** In MA auto, Commerce's share slipped from 19.0% (Dec 2024) to ~18.2% (Aug 2025) while Progressive+GEICO reached ~23.5% and Progressive passed GEICO — direct/digitally-led carriers are winning the growth [1][2]. In MA home, share fell 12.5%→11.0% in one year [23]. A one-point share loss in MA auto is roughly $65–70m of premium — larger than most growth initiatives would deliver.
3. **The AAA shock is the burning platform.** Up to ~$200m/year of affinity premium (≈8% of U.S. premium) is at risk as AAA Northeast stands up its own insurer and the JMA expires December 31, 2026; the injunction denial means courts will not preserve the channel [6][7]. Any growth strategy must (a) model AAA attrition scenarios, (b) decide whether to renegotiate, replicate (other affinity partners), or replace that volume, and (c) treat it as a 2026–27 revenue cliff, not a distant risk.
4. **Concentration is a rating and strategy constraint, and also the asset.** AM Best's "neutral business profile / marginal operating performance" language and reliance on parent support [3][4] argue for diversification; but the realistic near-term growth levers exploit the existing franchise: deepen New England non-MA states (NH/ME/VT/CT/RI, where infrastructure already exists [11]), cross-sell home/umbrella into the 900k+ MA auto base, and win a larger wallet share of its 3,000 agents [14].
5. **The direct-channel gap is now a strategic liability with an existing technical foundation.** Guidewire/EnterpriseEngage plumbing is live [20][21], yet quotes cannot be bound online — while the two fastest-growing MA competitors are direct writers [1]. A credible strategy will likely pair an agent-friendly digital bind capability (protecting the IA franchise) with a measured direct offering, rather than conceding the digitally-native segment entirely.
6. **Parent tailwind.** MAPFRE S.A. is at record profitability (>€1.1bn net result 2025) and its 2024–26 plan is concluding successfully [10]; a well-argued U.S. growth investment case lands in Madrid at the most receptive moment in a decade.

**Data gaps to close with client/proprietary sources:** exact U.S. product-line and state premium splits (NAIC state pages), U.S. policy-in-force counts and retention rates, AAA-sourced share of new business, expense ratio vs. MA peers (Safety, Plymouth Rock, Arbella), and the current AM Best credit report text on concentration.
