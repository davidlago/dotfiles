# MAPFRE USA Growth Strategy — Channel & Digital Transformation Team Proposal

**Prepared: July 2, 2026 | Team lens: how MAPFRE sells and serves — direct/bind-online, hybrid channel design, telematics, agent digital enablement, aggregators and embedded/affinity channels**
**All corrections in `work/fact-check.md` are applied. Assumptions are labeled as such and shown with arithmetic.**

---

## 1. Core thesis

**MAPFRE's Massachusetts franchise is not being beaten on price, product, or capital — it is being beaten on distribution technology, by a competitor that attacks through MAPFRE's own agents and through a direct channel MAPFRE does not have. The fix is not to choose between agents and direct; it is to become the most digitally capable *agent* carrier in Massachusetts and then bolt a Plymouth Rock-style "direct-to-agent" bind channel onto that foundation, at existing filed rates, with every policy assigned to an agent.** The build is cheap because the core is already modern; the risk is manageable because the channel-conflict problem has a ten-year-old, in-state, documented solution; and the cost of the status quo is now quantified: roughly 0.8 points of share per year and a >$200M affinity book walking out the door on December 31, 2026.

### The evidence, in five facts

1. **The share loss is a channel story, not a price story.** Commerce fell from 22.1% of MA private passenger auto exposures (2020) to 18.33% (Dec 2025) while Progressive rose from 5.1% to 12.17% — essentially a one-for-one transfer (`competitors.md`; CAR data via Agency Checklists). Progressive is the #1 IA auto writer in America and has appointed MA agents since 2014: its MA growth is substantially *agent-fed*. The three MA-domiciled agent carriers collectively held ~24% throughout — MAPFRE is not losing to fellow agent carriers; it is losing to the one carrier that runs both channels with superior digital tooling and telematics pricing.

2. **The digital gap is a productization gap, not a legacy-systems gap.** MAPFRE USA completed a full Guidewire InsuranceSuite modernization for personal lines in 2020, including digital portals (`tech-ops.md`; Guidewire, Nov 2020). Yet the website still cannot bind, MAPFRE is absent from EZLynx's MA rater panel (Jan 2024 grid) and invisible on every consumer comparison site (`agency-consolidation-response.md`, `ma-rate-filings-price-position.md`; ValuePenguin May 2026). The plumbing exists; nothing customer- or agent-facing has been built on it.

3. **Agents reward exactly what MAPFRE hasn't built.** Real-time appetite/quoting inside agency systems is the #1 carrier-selection factor (29% in IVANS 2025, up from 12%); 83% of agents say they would write more with carriers that provide it (IVANS 2024); agent satisfaction runs 274 points higher when a carrier is "very easy," and only 40% of agents say theirs are (J.D. Power 2025) (`ia-channel.md`, `tech-ops.md`). Massachusetts is the most IA-penetrated state in America (78.8% of P&C premium, 2024) — the highest-ROI battlefield available.

4. **The direct leak is real, the frontal-assault economics are prohibitive — and the hybrid template is proven in this exact state.** Direct writers hold ~23.5% of MA exposures; direct-channel new business grew +12.6% YoY nationally in Q4 2025 while the IA channel was -0.1%; a record 47.1% of policies were shopped in the past year; and the July 1, 2025 minimum-limits change (+36% on minimum-coverage premiums) handed every price-sensitive customer a shopping trigger (`ma-auto-market.md`, `customer-demographics.md`; LexisNexis, Bankrate). But a GEICO-style attack requires national-scale advertising (Progressive ~$3.5B in 2024, GEICO ~$1.4B), and Progressive's direct channel ran an 89.6 combined ratio vs 85.0 in agency YTD Sept 2025 (`direct-digital.md`; S&P Global, Progressive 10-Q). Plymouth Rock's Prime/eSales — quote and bind online, every policy assigned to a local agent for a one-time marketing fee, agent keeps full commission and lifetime ownership — has run continuously in Massachusetts since October 2015 (`direct-hybrid-calibration.md`). MAPFRE's own Verti USA (greenfield brand, out-of-footprint, 2017–~2021, no disclosed premium, silent shutdown) is the controlled experiment proving which direct model fails (`direct-hybrid-calibration.md`, `mapfre-parent.md`).

5. **The window is open — financially and competitively — and it closes.** MAPFRE North America posted a record €139M result and 95.4% combined ratio in 2025 (auto 97.8%), and Madrid's Q4 2025 message shifted to "profitable growth" within the existing footprint (`mapfre-parent.md`; fact-check confirmed). Safety and Plymouth Rock are still filing rate increases into late 2026 while Commerce is at adequacy; GEICO is re-arming but not yet re-accelerated in MA; Liberty just retired Safeco (April 25, 2026) with no competitor publicly recruiting its agents; and MCIC shows no measurable MA share yet, so the AAA book migrates mostly in January 2027+ renewals (`ma-rate-filings-price-position.md`, `agency-consolidation-response.md`, `aaa-northeast-endgame.md`). 2026–27 is the cheapest point in the cycle to act.

One honest caveat, stated precisely: in J.D. Power's 2025 New England auto study MAPFRE scored 597 — well below Amica's 735 and the regional average, though not lowest (Progressive 582, Farmers 587, The Hanover 594 scored lower); it slipped to 595 in the 2026 study. The durable finding is a six-plus-year pattern of bottom-quintile national auto-claims satisfaction (23rd of 25 in 2019; 17th of 21 in 2024) (`tech-ops.md`, corrected per fact-check) — a retention liability for a #1-share carrier, addressable with tools MAPFRE already licenses.

## 2. Initiatives

Total program: **~$70–105M over five years** (excluding retention-discount margin costs), phased so that Initiatives 5's claims economics substantially self-fund the later years. Against a ~$2.5B US premium base, this is ~0.6–0.8% of premium per year — well inside the headroom created by the 95.4% CR.

### Initiative 1 — "Easiest carrier in Massachusetts": agent digital enablement and the rater/API layer

**What exactly:** (a) Real-time rating/quote APIs from the Guidewire stack connected to **every** rater MA agents use — verify and harden SinglePoint (Boston Software, the MA incumbent with ~1,700 claimed agents), maintain PL Rating, and *close the EZLynx gap*, since national consolidators standardize on national rater stacks; (b) straight-through new-business issuance for clean risks; (c) real-time appetite service and flawless IVANS download; (d) agent-facing digital claims transparency; (e) a named-account program for the 10–15 consolidator platforms rolling up MAPFRE's ~1,300 MA agencies (Kaplansky/Alera, Duffy, DiVirgilio, World, Trucordia, Patriot, King Risk, Hilb) with pre-negotiated platform agreements and Ivans Bookroll tooling; (f) a first-mover **Safeco transition desk** — fast appointments, book-roll support, and an explicit "we will not compete against you for your customer" pledge that MAPFRE, uniquely, can make credibly today (`ia-channel.md`, `agency-consolidation-response.md`).

**Why it wins vs. named competitors:** Progressive wins agent quotes on speed and its FAO Portfolio platform; Safety wins on loyalty architecture and ease-of-doing-business (historically #4 nationally on the DCC index). Nobody in MA owns "easiest carrier to place MA auto with" as measured by agent-verifiable service levels; Erie/Auto-Owners prove EDB leadership compounds (J.D. Power 754/749). Liberty's Safeco retirement leaves ~22,000 agencies (247k MA auto exposures, 4.98% share, shrinking) sold by a brand that competes with them direct — a recruiting wedge no one has publicly claimed (`agency-consolidation-response.md`).

**Impact (arithmetic):** The counterfactual is continued erosion of ~0.8 pt/yr (2020–2025 run rate). Assume enablement plus current rate positioning converts that to flat-to-+0.3 pt/yr — consistent with share already stabilizing in 2025–26 and with 83% of agents saying real-time tooling shifts volume. Each 1.0 pt of MA PPA share = ~$74M DWP ($7.38B market, 2024). Holding vs. losing 0.5 pt/yr for three years = **~$110M of annual premium defended by year 3**; a 0.5 pt recapture adds **~$37M**. The deeper value is the renewal annuity and the agent shelf position every other initiative depends on. Add ex-Safeco book-roll wins: 5% of Liberty's ~247k MA exposures ≈ 12,000 exposures ≈ **$22M premium** (assumption: $1,800 avg premium).

**Investment:** $18–28M over 3 years (API/rater engineering on existing Guidewire core, STP rules, IVANS work, platform team of ~8–10 FTE, Safeco campaign). Order of magnitude anchored to the fact that this is productization on a modern core, not replatforming (`tech-ops.md`).

**Horizon:** 0–12 months to first API/rater deliverables; full program 1–3 years.

**Single biggest risk:** Progressive's agent tooling keeps improving on a budget MAPFRE cannot match — enablement achieves parity, not advantage, and the share stabilization proves temporary. Mitigant: pair with Initiative 3 (telematics pricing) so the agent has a *price* reason, not just a workflow reason, to lead with MAPFRE.

### Initiative 2 — Bind online, assign to an agent: the Prime-pattern hybrid channel

**What exactly:** Enable quote-to-bind on mapfreinsurance.com in Massachusetts at **existing filed rates**, with every digitally-bound policy assigned to a local partner agent who pays a one-time marketing fee and thereafter owns the business at full commission — the documented Plymouth Rock eSales mechanic (`direct-hybrid-calibration.md`). Year 1: MVP bind flow wrapped around the existing Commerce rating engine (a 3–9 month build; Nationwide stood up a greenfield digital product on Socotra in nine months), licensed-BPO inside-sales pod ($48–65/productive hour) for phone-assist, full funnel instrumentation, and recycling of abandoned quotes to agents as consented leads. Year 2: agent white-label funnels (unique agency URLs — the eSales pattern), capped **calibration** buys on EverQuote/MediaAlpha to measure true MA CAC and loss-ratio quality before scaling. Year 3: scale paid digital only where measured CAC beats the $500–900/bound-policy benchmark, telematics-qualified where possible. If channel-separated economics are later needed, file the direct book on an existing affiliate (Citation or American Commerce) — the Progressive Direct/Progressive Casualty paired-entity structure has been the accepted MA template since 2008, so no novel regulatory ask exists (`ma-rate-filings-price-position.md`).

**Why it wins vs. named competitors:** It closes the single dumbest leak in the franchise — a quote page that generates intent and hands the close to Progressive and GEICO — without triggering the agent war Liberty Mutual's single-brand direct conflict illustrates. Plymouth Rock proved for a decade, in this state, that agents accept the model; against MCIC it gives defecting AAA customers a native digital re-anchoring path (Initiative 4). Verti's failure is the internal proof that this design — brand density, existing rates, agent ecosystem — is the only direct design MAPFRE should attempt (`direct-hybrid-calibration.md`).

**Impact (arithmetic, assumptions labeled):** Pilot: 10,000 bound policies ≈ **$17–20M premium** at ~$1,700–2,000 average MA premium, with $5–9M acquisition spend at the $500–900 CAC benchmark, partially offset by agent marketing fees (assume $150–250/policy → $1.5–2.5M; Plymouth Rock's fee level is not public). Scale case by year 5: 40–55k digitally-originated policies in force ≈ **$70–100M premium** (~1–1.3 share points) — faster than Plymouth Rock's Core Direct ($110M after ten years) because MAPFRE starts with 18% brand share and 909k in-force customers, not zero. Expect the channel to run 3–6 CR points worse than agency in years 1–3 (Progressive's direct-vs-agency spread is 4.6 points in a growth year); underwrite it on lifetime economics vs. the ~12–15% perpetual commission alternative.

**Investment:** $5–15M build + program (triangulated in `direct-hybrid-calibration.md` from Spire, Celent/Novarica benchmarks, and Verti's cost shadow), plus $5–9M/yr demand spend at pilot scale. Total ~$25–40M over 3 years.

**Horizon:** MVP live within 12 months; channel maturity 1–3 years; scale decision gate at 3 years.

**Single biggest risk:** Adverse selection in intermediated digital demand — Plymouth Rock's aggregator-fed Partner book (~$260M) ran elevated loss ratios for years. Mitigant: launch on owned traffic (the existing quote page, renewal cross-sell, agent white-label) first; cap aggregator buys at calibration size; require telematics enrollment (Initiative 3) as the price-qualification device for paid digital demand.

### Initiative 3 — Re-arm MotionSmart: telematics as the pricing and retention layer

**What exactly:** Re-file MotionSmart with an 8–10% participation discount and a symmetric renewal swing (target ±20%) — squarely inside the DOI's *revealed* tolerance: Plymouth Rock's DriveSavely already has 10% at quote and a −20%/+20% renewal band approved, and Snapshot operates in MA with surcharges (`ma-rate-filings-price-position.md`). Deepen the existing CMT relationship (the world's largest mobile-telematics vendor, headquartered in Cambridge, serving nearly all top-25 carriers). Set agent-channel enrollment targets with point-of-sale defaults, scripts, and incentives — Allstate's 30–40% new-business telematics penetration is the proven benchmark (`telematics-ubi.md`). Phase 2 (18–36 months): at-quote scoring (Arity IQ / TransUnion-CMT portable score) so agents can price driving behavior at new business without app friction — no MA filing has yet used a purchased third-party at-quote score, so this is a first-mover filing project, informed by the Arity Geosight precedent of third-party telematics data in an approved MA filing.

**Why it wins vs. named competitors:** Massachusetts bans credit, age (except 65+), sex, occupation, education, and homeownership as rating factors — telematics is effectively the only high-dimensional segmentation lever the DOI permits, and the carrier eating MAPFRE's share (Progressive) calls UBI its single most predictive variable at >3x the next best (`ma-regulation.md` summary, `telematics-ubi.md`). MotionSmart's current "up to 2%" is below every visible MA benchmark (Safety 5%, Plymouth Rock 10% at quote, Nationwide 10% participation/21% avg earned). Fixing it denies Progressive its cleanest selection game: skimming MAPFRE's safest drivers with Snapshot discounts.

**Impact (arithmetic, assumptions labeled):** Assume ~110k new-business policies/yr (~12% of the 909k-exposure book) and 30% enrollment by year 3 → ~90–100k enrolled policies ≈ **~$170–190M monitored premium**. Evidence supports 2–3 loss-ratio points on enrolled business from behavior change and selection (CMT: engaged risky drivers cut distracted driving 20%, speeding 27%, ≈5.5% BI claim reduction; practitioner estimates run to 7 CR points fully deployed — we plan conservatively): **$3.5–5.5M/yr loss-cost benefit by year 3**, plus Progressive-documented retention effects (+6 pts renewal among top discount earners; −16 pts among surcharged — both favorable). Net of the participation-discount giveaway, model 0.5–1.0 portfolio CR point by year 4.

**Investment:** $4–7M/yr at scale (CMT per-policy fees, engagement operations, agent incentives, actuarial/filing). Low-single-digit millions to relaunch.

**Horizon:** Re-file and relaunch 0–12 months; enrollment scale and at-quote scoring 1–3 years.

**Single biggest risk:** A discount-led program without an approved surcharge back-end gives away premium on good risks while bad risks stay unpriced — the exact failure mode of MotionSmart 1.0 and of most UBI programs pre-surcharge (Progressive's economics turned only after 2014 surcharges). Mitigant: file the symmetric structure from day one; the MA Data Privacy Act (both pending versions make precise geolocation opt-in) argues for consent-native, first-party design now rather than retrofit later.

### Initiative 4 — The AAA endgame: digital defense of the $200M book and an affinity/embedded portfolio to replace it

**What exactly:** Treat the AAA Northeast JMA as expiring on schedule (injunction denied Feb 3, 2026; the litigation is now a damages/settlement-leverage instrument — `aaa-northeast-endgame.md`). Run a 2026 retention offensive on the >$200M AAA-sourced book *before* the January 2027+ renewal migration: agent-of-record re-anchoring into the regular agency channel, bundling offers (auto+home account discount up to ~25%/15% already filed), and the Initiative 2 bind path as the self-service re-anchoring vehicle. In parallel, industrialize the affinity machine MAPFRE already owns: 280 approved §193R group deviations — the largest franchise in the state — but at a shallower modal discount (4%) than Plymouth Rock/Safety/Arbella (5%, peer approvals to 9–10%). Deepen the top 30–50 groups to 5–6%, add payroll deduction and *digital group enrollment* (a co-branded quote-and-bind flow per anchor group), prioritizing credit unions and banks already on the list (DCU, Eastern Bank et al.). Use the litigation to negotiate wind-down terms: renewal-rights protections, member-data terms, or a paid second-carrier slot in AAA's agency.

**Why it wins vs. named competitors:** MCIC (backed by the A+ Auto Club Enterprises pool, cutting MA rates effective Jan 1, 2026, staffing a 370-job RI operations center) will win the AAA emblem; MAPFRE cannot out-brand it inside AAA's own channel. But MCIC has no measurable MA share yet and no MA quote path on AAA's site as of July 2026 — every policy re-anchored before 1/1/2027 raises AAA's migration cost. On replacement: the 35% group-participation rule is waived through 2030, group deviations are the one regulator-sanctioned headline price advantage in MA, and GEICO (8 deviations) and Progressive (none) structurally cannot match an agent-channel group machine (`ma-rate-filings-price-position.md`, `aaa-northeast-endgame.md`).

**Impact (arithmetic):** The AAA feed is >$200M of 2025 premium ≈ 2–3 points of MA share. Passive base case: lose 70–80% over 2027–29 as renewals migrate. Target: retain 50–60% via re-anchoring + bundle + price (retention offers ≈ 1–2% margin cost on defended premium ≈ $2–4M/yr). **Delta vs. passive ≈ $60–100M of annual premium preserved.** Group-deviation deepening: moving ~$300M of group-eligible premium (assumption: top groups' share of book) from 4% to 5–6% costs ~$3–6M/yr in rate but is the approved mechanism to buy targeted share back through agents; new CU/bank anchor partnerships target 10–20k policies over 3 years (**$18–36M premium**, assumption-flagged).

**Investment:** $8–12M over 2 years (retention campaign, group-enrollment digital build, partnership team) plus the discount margin costs above.

**Horizon:** 0–12 months (the window is now through the 2027 renewal cycle); portfolio build 1–3 years.

**Single biggest risk:** Post-expiry, MAPFRE cannot use the AAA emblem or member discount, and AAA likely holds the member relationship data — retention offers may under-convert against MCIC's member-branded pricing. Mitigant: this is precisely why the offensive must run in 2026 while MAPFRE is still the incumbent carrier of record with renewal contact rights; settlement negotiations should price member-data and non-solicitation terms explicitly.

### Initiative 5 — Digital claims and self-funding operations: fix the weakest customer-facing flank

**What exactly:** Scale what is already licensed but under-deployed: push ePICS photo-appraisal toward touchless norms (90% touchless demonstrated at Tractable's flagship deployment — vendor-reported), deploy two-way claims texting and proactive status communication (Plymouth Rock runs Hi Marley + gen-AI FNOL today), make One Inc instant payment the default, and steer via CAR EZ DRP. Fund it with claims-economics self-help: subrogation analytics (industry salvage+subrogation averages 4.5% of net claims paid, 6.2% among firms with any recovery; ~15% of recoverable claims missed; $15–20B annual industry leakage — corrected per fact-check), fraud AI (Shift is a group partner), and DRP severity management (`tech-ops.md`).

**Why it wins vs. named competitors:** Claims experience is MAPFRE's most measurable liability (bottom-quintile J.D. Power claims scores 2019–2024) while cycle time is the industry's proven retention lever (19.3-day average repair cycle, down from 22.3). Amica monetizes experience; Progressive monetizes speed. A #1-share carrier whose share is held by agent inertia and price, not experience, is structurally short retention — and retention is cheaper than acquisition in a flat-exposure market.

**Impact (arithmetic, assumptions labeled):** US paid losses ≈ $2.5B premium × ~65% loss ratio ≈ $1.6B. Each +0.5 pt of net subrogation/salvage recovery = **~$8M/yr**, rate-free. Fraud AI and DRP steering: 0.3–0.7 CR pt (**$7–17M/yr** on US book, planning midpoint ~$10M). Retention: closing half the claims-satisfaction gap is worth an assumed 0.5–1.0 pt of retention on claimants (≈10–12% of book/yr) — small in year 1, compounding thereafter. Combined, this initiative plausibly funds Initiatives 1–3's run-rate by year 3.

**Investment:** $12–20M over 3 years (subro analytics, texting platform, touchless scaling, fraud models).

**Horizon:** 1–3 years, with subrogation analytics payback inside 12 months.

**Single biggest risk:** Execution/change-management in a claims organization that has fielded these tools for years without moving the J.D. Power needle — the constraint is operating model, not software. Mitigant: put claims-experience KPIs (touchless %, cycle time, texted-claims share) on the same executive scorecard as the CR.

### Initiative 6 — Serve the growth segments the channel currently misses: in-language digital + community agents

**What exactly:** A focused overlay, not a new brand: Portuguese- and Spanish-language quote/bind and service flows on the Initiative 2 stack; bilingual community-agent recruitment in Framingham, Everett, Lawrence, Lynn, Springfield/Holyoke; Puerto Rico brand-bridge marketing (MAPFRE operates at scale in PR; Puerto Ricans are Boston's largest Latino origin group) (`customer-demographics.md`).

**Why it wins:** Massachusetts' only organically growing customer pools are immigrant communities (Latinos ≈ 8 in 10 net new residents 2014–2023; Brazilians the largest immigrant group at ~140k+, Portuguese-speaking, owned by no carrier). ~40% of Spanish-dominant consumers only make high-value purchases with native-language support — translation alone fails. No MA carrier has built end-to-end in-language capability plus trusted local agents.

**Impact (assumption-flagged):** 15–25k incremental policies over 5 years ≈ **$27–45M premium**; also feeds urban voluntary writing that earns MAIP credit offsets (~$2,900 average offset per voluntary urban car year — `maip-credit-economics.md` summary).

**Investment:** $6–10M over 3 years. **Horizon:** 1–3 years. **Single biggest risk:** Underwriting and service execution in thin-file, first-time-buyer segments degrades loss ratios; mitigate with telematics-at-quote (Initiative 3) as the segmentation device the DOI permits.

## 3. What we would explicitly NOT do

1. **No GEICO/Progressive-style national DTC brand assault.** The entry ticket is $1.4–3.5B/yr of advertising and multi-year tolerance of a 20%+ expense-ratio channel; at Commerce's scale a meaningful media budget buys a rounding error of share in an ad market Progressive is deliberately flooding (`direct-digital.md`).
2. **No new direct brand, no out-of-footprint digital greenfield.** Verti USA is the controlled experiment: zero brand equity + new state + retrenchment agenda = silent death (`direct-hybrid-calibration.md`).
3. **No channel-differentiated pricing that undercuts agents on the Commerce entity.** Same filed rates at launch; if direct economics later justify separation, use a separate affiliate filing (the approved MA template) — never a cheaper "web rate" on the agency company.
4. **No scale aggregator buying at the 2026 price peak.** EverQuote's largest customer is 39% of its revenue; Plymouth Rock's Partner book proves intermediated demand adversely selects. Calibration buys only, capped, with loss-ratio gates.
5. **No in-house telematics platform.** Progressive needed ~15 years and billions of miles; CMT is already under contract and in MAPFRE's backyard. Program design, not sensor stacks, differentiates.
6. **No core replatforming.** Guidewire is done; any proposal framed as "modernization" should be re-scoped as productization or rejected.
7. **No bet-the-franchise on winning the AAA litigation.** The injunction denial and the Dec 31, 2026 expiry make this a damages and wind-down-terms negotiation. Plan distribution as if the JMA is gone.

## 4. The strongest objection, stated fairly — and our response

**The objection:** *"Direct is a decade-long money pit and a channel-conflict grenade. Plymouth Rock — your own template — got to only ~$110M of Core Direct, under 5% of its book, after ten years, and its chairman says scaling it means years of deliberate underwriting losses. Meanwhile 100% of your premium flows through agents whose goodwill you need more than ever with AAA walking and Safeco's agents in play. Every dollar spent on bind-online is a dollar not spent on the agent war you are actually losing — and if agents read the website as competition, they will quietly move their next quote to Safety or Progressive, and you will have paid to accelerate your own erosion. The rational strategy is 100% agent-side — rater APIs, commissions, claims, telematics — and no direct channel at all."*

This is the best argument against our lens, and it is half right: the agent war is the main war, which is why Initiative 1 is first, largest, and unconditional, and why the direct build is explicitly sequenced behind agent enablement — activating bind-online before fixing agent ease-of-doing-business would inflame the channel exactly when agent goodwill is scarce.

But the pure-agent strategy fails on three facts. **First, the leak is already happening and is priced.** Direct/hybrid writers hold ~23.5% of MA exposures and rising; 47.1% of policies were shopped last year; direct new business grew +12.6% while the IA channel went negative. Refusing to bind online does not keep those customers in the agent channel — it hands them to Progressive's funnel, which then *also* out-competes MAPFRE inside the agencies. The status quo is not neutrality; it is a subsidy to the attacker. **Second, the channel-conflict premise is empirically false here when the model is designed correctly.** Plymouth Rock has run bind-online-with-agent-assignment in Massachusetts for ten years while remaining agent-first (IA group +23% in 2024); Progressive's agency channel grew 21% the same year its direct channel grew 27%. Agents do not punish carriers for capturing digital demand *on the agents' behalf* — they punish carriers that compete against them (Liberty/Limu) or are hard to work with. Under our design the agent is paid, owns the customer, and receives abandoned-quote leads: the website becomes the agent's storefront, and "every bind gets you a customer" becomes the recruiting pitch to ex-Safeco agencies. **Third, the modest scale is the point, and the option value is asymmetric.** We underwrite the direct channel to reach only ~1 point of share by year 5 — but it is the retention vehicle for the AAA book (Initiative 4), the funnel for in-language growth segments (Initiative 6), the instrumented lab that finally reveals MAPFRE's true CAC and conversion economics, and the capability that makes every future affinity/embedded partnership (the parent's stated preferred growth vector) executable. The build is $5–15M against a $2.5B book. The expensive mistake was Verti — and this is not Verti. A carrier with 18% share, 909k customers, a modern core, and 1,300 agent storefronts does not face a build-vs-don't-build decision; it faces a "who captures our own demand" decision. We propose that MAPFRE, and its agents, capture it.

## 5. Sequencing at a glance

| Phase | Moves |
|---|---|
| **0–12 months** | Rater/API sprint incl. EZLynx; STP issuance; Safeco transition desk; consolidator agreements; MotionSmart re-filing; AAA retention offensive + settlement asks; bind-online MVP with agent assignment; subrogation analytics |
| **1–3 years** | Agent white-label funnels + lead recycling; capped aggregator calibration; telematics enrollment scale + at-quote score filing; group-deviation deepening + digital group enrollment; claims texting/touchless scale; in-language flows |
| **3–5 years** | Scale paid digital only where measured CAC < $500–900; direct-affiliate rate filing if justified; extend the stack to CT/RI/NH agents; affinity/embedded partnerships on the bind API |

**Falsifiable success metrics:** MA share ≥18.5% by YE2027 and ≥19.5% by YE2029; ≥50% AAA-book retention through 2028; 30% telematics new-business enrollment by 2028; digital-origination ≥4% of MA new business by 2029 at CAC <$700; agent "very easy" rating and DCC/J.D. Power agent scores in the state's top 3; US combined ratio held ≤96% throughout.
